## Likely questions and answers

How the simplified model differs from the earlier 12-sheet version, and the questions those changes invite.

- **"Your market was $28.7B; now it's $28.6B. Why?"** "I simplified the signalling carve-out. Instead of 24 regional parameters I use the two world-level shares from my carve-out: 74% passenger and 30% urban. The regional blend came to 74.2%, so rounding to the world figure takes $0.07B off. Nothing in the argument depends on that decimal, and the headline is still about $29B."
- **"Why is 2031 now $39.3B?"** "Signalling now has one growth rate, 5.0% nominal, instead of six regional rates that blended to 4.85%. 5.0% is just above SCI Verkehr's 4.6% nominal forecast and in line with UNIFE's outlook of about 3.2% a year real plus inflation. UNIFE's realised rail-control growth was 5.7% a year in real terms, so history does not argue for a lower rate. The change adds $0.15B by 2031."
- **"Why is urban signalling $6.2B, not $6.3B?"** "It is the same simplification: a 30% world urban share, against a regional blend of 30.1%."
- **"What happened to the urban 4.7% and mainline 4.9% growth rates?"** "Those were computed in the slide script, not in the model. The model now forecasts signalling as one block at one rate, which is also how the sources forecast it."
- **"The North American figure fell from $0.9B to $0.6B."** "It now measures only what the thesis targets, North American bus software and services. The old figure added about $0.3B of North American rail ITS from an analyst regional share. That slice grows about 5.7% a year; the 7% applies to the global open pool, and I never attach it to North America."
- **"Why is the software-exit MOIC 2.0x, not 2.1x?"** "Exit B is now IVU's observed 2.6x revenue. Before, it was 12x EBITDA times an assumed 22% exit margin. That margin was above IVU's actual ~16% EBITDA margin (15.8% LTM), so the old version needed two assumptions to land on roughly the same multiple."
- **"Why pay 2.0x when Hitachi paid 1.6x?"** "2.0x sits inside the INIT-to-IVU trading band and allows for a competitive process for a founder-owned asset. If we enter at Clever's 1.6x, the returns are 1.4x and 2.4x, and the answer still turns on the exit multiple. Break-even is about 1.3x revenue."
- **"Slides 5 and 6 have no numbers. How big is Modaxo's share?"** "Indicatively about a third of the North American pool, with Hitachi plus Clever around 13%. Those figures rest on headcount and scope assumptions, and Conduent's piece includes fare collection, which is why the slide says only that Modaxo is the largest owner and Hitachi number two."
- **"Challengers usually win. On what evidence?"** "In 39 decision points on incumbent-held systems from 2016 to 2026, 18 went to a competitive rebid and challengers won 14. The other 21 kept the incumbent without a documented rebid: 15 were extensions, sole-source renewals or cooperative buys, and for 6 the method is not stated. In bus CAD/AVL the incumbent lost all 8 rebids, but every winner was an established vendor (Clever won 4), so the record shows incumbent risk rather than new-entrant odds. Systems are a median 16 years old when they reach a decision."
- **"Your upside case is 2.0x gross over five years. Why call this attractive?"** "The verdict is conditional: attractive only as a software-led buy-and-build. At the Clever precedent the platform returns about 1.2x, roughly 4% a year gross; at IVU's multiple it is about 2.0x, roughly 15% a year; entering at Clever's multiple and exiting at IVU's gives about 2.4x, roughly 19-20%. So the case only works if recurring software revenue becomes the majority and the exit is priced like IVU. At a 15% margin, 2.6x revenue is about 17x EBITDA, against IVU's ~15x LTM EBITDA, so margin expansion is part of the exit story. These IRRs are MOIC^(1/5) − 1; they are deliberately not in the model."
- **"The platform exits at $128M of revenue, but the North American slice is only ~$0.6B. Is that a fifth of the market?"** "No. The $0.6B is software and services only; the platform's revenue also includes hardware. Against all North American bus ITS ($1.1B in 2025, about $1.5B in 2031) the platform is about 5% at entry and under a tenth at exit, below Hitachi plus Clever today (~13%)."
