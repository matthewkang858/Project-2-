## Slide 1: Executive summary

This slide argues that public ITS (intelligent transportation systems) for bus and passenger rail is a large, growing market, but a newcomer can win only a ~$4B software and services slice, best reached by buying and building on a North American bus ITS vendor.

| Element | What it says | Research and reasoning |

| Title | "~$29B market growing ~5% a year, ~$4B software and services pool, best entered via North American buy-and-build" | The model gives $28.6B for 2025, 5.4% a year growth and a $4.2B open pool, and Berg Insight sizes Europe's bus ITS at EUR 2.55B against North America's EUR 1.22B. The title rounds these so they stick and still picks North America, because national procurement regimes split Europe's larger pool while North America has one federal framework and privately held targets. |
| Market size and segmentation, finding 1 | "~$29B (2025, ex-fare), but nearly three-quarters rail signalling, half mainline signalling bought mainly by infrastructure managers" | The Market sheet's $28.6B adds Berg Insight bus ITS net of fare collection, the passenger share of rail industry body UNIFE's signalling market and other rail ITS: signalling is 72.8%, mainline 50.9%. | Growth to 2031, finding 2 | "Open software and services pool outgrows the market at ~7% a year, excluding signalling and gaining share from hardware" | The Growth sheet takes the open pool from $4.2B to $6.3B, 7.0% a year against the market's 5.4%, while signalling grows 5.0%, bus ITS 6.3% and other rail ITS 7.0%. | Competitive dynamics, finding 1 | "Signalling is a closed oligopoly (Siemens, Hitachi, Alstom, CRSC) protected by safety certification and long maintenance tails" | Research note 22_gap gives the top four about 73% of a EUR 21B signalling market that includes freight, about 60% of UNIFE's larger base, and Deutsche Bahn's 2025 signalling frameworks total EUR 6.3B. Four firms holding most of the market on either base make it an oligopoly, and SIL-4 safety certification (the highest safety integrity level) plus multi-year frameworks keep newcomers out. |
| Competitive dynamics, finding 2 | "North American bus ITS consolidating around Modaxo and Hitachi, cloud challengers overlay legacy systems, private long tail remains" | Modaxo bought Conduent's transit business and Hitachi bought Clever Devices in 2026, yet a real-time feed census finds at least 26 US agencies paying cloud vendor Swiftly to overlay another vendor's system. It names North America because Europe's largest vendor, INIT, holds only about 6%, and the roughly 29% outside North America's top five owners is the private tail an entrant can buy. |
| Risks and barriers, finding 1 | "Barrier is tenure, not rebid odds: installed systems rarely rebid, but challengers usually win when they are" | An award dataset built from agency board records holds 39 decisions in 2016-26: only 18 went to a competitive rebid, challengers won 14, and systems were a median 16 years old. Incumbents keep accounts because systems come up only after well over a decade and often skip a rebid, not because they win, so buying an installed base beats waiting for tenders. |
| Risks and barriers, finding 2 | "Near-term risks: the US federal funding reset in FY2027 and ETCS slippage in Europe" | Fiscal 2027 scenarios put US federal transit funding 5-22% below fiscal 2026's $20.96B after the infrastructure law's $4.25B-a-year advance money lapsed, and the European Commission calls ETCS "significantly behind schedule". Slide 5 rates them MID (medium) because formula funds for system refresh rise in both fiscal 2027 proposals, and even the low case grows North American bus ITS 2.5% a year. |
| Investment thesis, finding 1 | "Buy privately held North American mid-market bus ITS vendor below a software multiple, add cloud, AI enforcement, zero-emission modules" | The Returns sheet enters at 2.0x revenue, between listed INIT (0.98x) and IVU (2.60x), and the 2025 National Transit Database shows agencies with 50-999 buses run 54.5% of US full-size buses. Mid-market vendors serve that majority tier, and buying below IVU's software multiple, then adding cloud, bus-lane enforcement cameras and electric-bus software, builds the recurring revenue that earns that multiple. |
| Investment thesis, finding 2 | "Returns hinge on a software exit multiple (~2.6x revenue, like IVU), Clever's ~1.6x barely returns capital" | The Returns sheet's $157.5M of equity returns 2.0x at IVU's 2.60x but 1.2x at Clever's 1.58x, the $347M Hitachi paid including debt on $220M of projected 2026 revenue (Hitachi Q1 FY2026 results). The same platform roughly doubles its money priced like IVU, whose revenue is 51.8% recurring, but barely returns capital priced like Clever, so recurring revenue is the first diligence question. |
| Verdict bar | "Attractive only as software-led buy-and-build in bus ITS, not a highly levered hardware-integrator buyout, not signalling" | Moody's put Cubic's 2021 buyout leverage above 9x and Cubic deferred interest from January 2026, while the case's deal borrows 2.5x EBITDA (roughly yearly operating cash profit), half repaid by exit. Returns work only at a software exit multiple, hence "only", and the two nos mark the edges: project-heavy integrators cannot carry heavy debt, and signalling is closed to newcomers. |

This slide argues that an entrant's hard part is getting a chance to compete, not winning, so buying an installed base beats organic entry, while the near-term funding, ETCS and leverage risks are moderate.
