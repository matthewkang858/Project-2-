# 08 — Bus / Roadway Transit ITS: Vendor Landscape and Competitive Dynamics

**Scope of this file:** vendors selling software, hardware and services to public bus/roadway transit operators (fixed-route bus, BRT, paratransit / demand-response, trolleybus), mapped to seven product buckets: (1) vehicle tracking / CAD-AVL, (2) scheduling and operations software, (3) passenger information and displays, (4) automatic passenger counting (APC), (5) onboard video and AI surveillance, (6) telematics and asset management, (7) services. Fare collection / ticketing is **ADJACENT** and is flagged wherever a vendor's numbers bundle it (INIT, Conduent/Novvan, Cubic, Vix, Flowbird, Ticketer, Genfare, Masabi all span both).

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The North American bus-ITS incumbency has been re-shuffled in 2026 by two strategic deals.** Hitachi Rail closed its acquisition of Clever Devices on 1 July 2026 (projected 2026 revenue >$220m, 600+ staff, 8 of the 10 largest NA agencies; price undisclosed) and rebranded it Hitachi Mobility Multimodal Solutions; Modaxo (Constellation Software) closed its $164m purchase of Conduent's Public Transit unit on 1 October 2026 (unit revenue $371m in 2025 incl. fare collection; 2,000 staff) and relaunched it as Novvan (fare) and Routeward (CAD/AVL-fleet management). Together with Trapeze and Vontas, Modaxo now owns three of the historical NA CAD/AVL franchises (Trapeze, Vontas, Conduent/Routeward) plus TransLoc, TripSpark and Routematch.
2. **The only listed pure-play bus-ITS comps are small, European and currently mixed in momentum.** INIT SE: FY2025 revenue EUR 329.7m (+24.1%), EBIT EUR 32.5m (9.9% margin), order backlog EUR 343m (plus ~EUR 1.2bn of contracted service obligations by mid-2026); 9M-2026 revenue +29% to EUR 292.6m but Q3-2026 EBIT collapsed to EUR 1.6m and FY2026 EBIT guidance was cut on 2 Nov 2026 to EUR 33-37m (from 38-42m) on Middle-East demand, materials availability and mix. IVU Traffic Technologies: FY2025 revenue EUR 149.7m, EBIT EUR 18.6m; 9M-2026 revenue +9.6%, EBIT +56.5%, 51.8% recurring revenue, 2026 guidance >EUR 160m / EBIT ~EUR 22m. Via Transportation (NYSE: VIA, IPO Sept 2025 at ~$3.65bn) is the growth comp: FY2025 revenue $434m, net loss $96.4m, 832 customers in 30+ countries.
3. **Berg Insight (10th ed., Mar 2026) is the only transit-specific vendor-ranking source and it names the same short list:** Trapeze and INIT have significant installed bases in both Europe and North America; Clever Devices and Conduent lead North America, with Cubic and Avail also holding major NA shares; Siemens Mobility is prominent in software on both continents; Equans and RATP Smart Systems lead France; Vix, Flowbird and Ticketer lead the UK; IVU dominates German-speaking Europe; GMV, Indra, Grupo ETRA, Thales, Atron, FARA, Pilotfish, Icomera, Consat, Swarco and Kontron are the other named players. Berg sizes bus+tram ITS at EUR 2.55bn (Europe) and EUR 1.22bn (North America) in 2025, growing 5.3% / 5.0% p.a. to 2030, with on-board computer penetration already >90% of vehicles in both regions.
4. **Share arithmetic (derived, directional):** against Berg's NA pool of EUR 1.22bn (~$1.3bn), Clever's >$220m projected 2026 revenue is ~17%, INIT's NA revenue of EUR 130.8m (2025) is ~11% (both include fare-collection and rail work), and Conduent's former transit unit ($371m globally, fare-heavy) would be the largest single NA transit-technology franchise if most of it is NA. The top five NA incumbents therefore plausibly hold 50-60% of the NA pool, leaving a long tail of mid-fleet specialists (Avail, Passio, ETA, Transit Technologies/Ecolane, Connexionz, Routematch/TripSpark) and SaaS challengers.
5. **The challenger wave is SaaS, agency-count-led and PE/VC funded:** Swiftly (200+ agencies in 10 countries, more than half of the 25 largest US agencies by bus ridership; Cove Hill-led round May 2025 with JMI; acquired Hopthru 2024 and TextMarks May 2026; launched AI-powered CAD/AVL in 2026 citing a survey in which only 6% of 75+ agencies would strongly recommend their legacy CAD/AVL; new CEO Oct 2026), Optibus (7,000 cities, 35+ countries; $260m raised; $1.3bn valuation at the May 2022 Series D), Spare (C$42m Series B Sept 2024; 8 of the 10 largest US cities; MBTA RIDE at ~1m trips/yr), Via (public), Transit app (200 official agency partners, 9m+ monthly users), Moovit (Mobileye; 1.7bn users) and Masabi (400+ agencies, Accel-KKR) are all selling into the same agencies as the incumbents, typically on subscription.
6. **Business-model split is stark and matters for valuation.** Incumbents (INIT, Clever/Hitachi, Trapeze/Vontas, Conduent/Routeward, Cubic, Avail, Luminator, Hanover) sell capex-heavy integrated systems (onboard computer + CAD/AVL + signage + APC + installation) followed by multi-year maintenance/hosting; INIT reports contracted service obligations of ~EUR 1.2bn and contract terms "up to 20 years"; IVU reports 51.8% recurring revenue. Challengers (Swiftly, Optibus, Spare, Via, Masabi, Moovit, Transit) are cloud SaaS or managed services with little or no hardware. Luminator's Sept 2026 "hardware to digital partner" repositioning and Swiftly's entry into CAD/AVL are the two clearest signals that the boundary is dissolving.
7. **Consolidators, not sponsors, are setting prices.** Disclosed 2024-26 transactions were all strategic: Hitachi Rail (Thales GTS EUR 1.66bn in 2024; Clever Devices 2026), Modaxo/Constellation (Conduent transit $164m; Conduent curbside/public safety 2024; SISCOG 2025; Cammax; FARA; SS Telematica; ExPretio; Fiscaltech Nov 2026), INIT (DILAX, Sept 2024, ~EUR 20m revenue, 160 staff), Via (Remix 2021, Citymapper 2023, Downtowner Dec 2025, Knode.ai 2026), Siemens (Hacon 2017, Sqills), Clarience/Genstar (Safe Fleet incl. Seon, 2024), EasyPark/Arrive (Flowbird, Jan 2025). Modaxo states it "never sells" its businesses, which removes a large share of the installed base from any future sponsor's target list.
8. **Hardware-heavy balance sheets are the cautionary tale.** Cubic (Veritas/Elliott take-private 2021) was described by Mobility Payments in 2026 as a "troubled fare-system vendor" being turned around, lost or is at risk on several large fare contracts (Miami $42m award to Masabi upheld on protest; Vancouver), and its Clipper 2.0 launch (Dec 2025) drew public criticism; sibling file 10 cites Bloomberg on a July 2025 recap, a Jan 2026 interest deferral and ~USD 2.1bn of debt (not independently verified here). Conduent exited transportation entirely (transit sold, tolling sale pending year-end 2026).
9. **Private mid-market incumbents are ageing founder/employee-owned assets:** Avail (founded 1999, now 67% and moving to 100% employee-owned, new CEO Jan 2025), GIRO (founder-led since the 1970s, HASTUS in 30+ countries), Hanover Displays (family-owned, 40 years, 80+ countries), ETA Transit (founded 2003, privately held, woman-owned), Passio and Ecolane (both inside Transit Technologies LLC), Pantonium, Iris/DILAX (now inside INIT). These are the realistic bolt-on pipeline for a new entrant or platform.
10. **Adjacent fare-collection and enforcement pools are larger and faster-moving than core CAD/AVL** (Masabi, Genfare/SPX, Cubic Umo, Novvan, Vix, Ticketer/Corvia, Flowbird/Arrive; Hayden AI in automated bus-lane enforcement) and are the main cross-sell pool for CAD/AVL incumbents; a new entrant's wedge is more likely to be data/SaaS (real-time, APC analytics, scheduling, demand-response) than hardware.
