# 01 — Top-down ITS and public-transit ITS market size estimates

**Workstream:** Public ITS CDD case (Roadway/bus + Railway software, hardware, services)
**Prepared:** 2026-11-07
**Status of research:** WebSearch-based (25+ distinct queries, "extended" mode). Direct WebFetch of vendor pages, Berg Insight PDFs and press releases was blocked by the session network policy (ENOTFOUND on every host, retried once), and the shared WebSearch budget was exhausted before the final batch (segment-share detail for Grand View / Fortune / Technavio / GMI APTS, rail signalling, tolling, fare collection and regional ITS queries did not run). Every figure below was seen in a search-result snippet or summary and carries the URL it came from. Nothing is extrapolated without being labelled **DERIVED**.

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **"Global ITS" is a ~$40–60B (2025) market by most vendor definitions, growing 5–11% p.a.** The tightest cluster is MarketsandMarkets $42.55B (2025), Precedence $41.42B (2025), Research&Markets/TBRC $41.51B (2026), Maximize $40.32B (2025), Straits $37.83B (2025); a higher cluster sits at $50–58B (Grand View $58.3B 2025, IMARC $51.8B 2025, Mordor $52.6B 2026, Market.us $50.8B 2024, Zion $54.5B 2024). A low cluster ($29–31B: Verified Market Research 2024, Fortune 2025, Fact.MR 2024) uses narrower or differently defined scopes.
2. **None of the global ITS numbers is a usable proxy for our scope.** All of them bundle roadway traffic management (ATMS, the single largest segment at ~38% per Mordor), tolling/pricing, parking, commercial-vehicle operations, aviation and maritime. Public transport appears only as the "Advanced Public Transportation Systems (APTS)" sub-segment plus a slice of the railway mode. Roadway (all applications) is the largest mode at ~33–39% of total ITS (Allied ~1/3 in 2022; Grand View 39.0% in 2025).
3. **The two estimates that best match "public transit bus + rail ITS" are (a) Berg Insight's *Public Transport ITS in Europe and North America* (10th ed., March 2026) and (b) Global Market Insights' *Advanced Public Transportation System (APTS)* report (2025 base).**
   - Berg Insight (bus + tram, on-vehicle and depot/stop/terminal systems): **Europe €2.55B (2025) → €3.30B (2030), 5.3% CAGR; North America €1.22B (2025) → €1.56B (2030), 5.0% CAGR.** DERIVED combined: €3.77B (2025) → €4.86B (2030), ~5.2% CAGR (trade press rounds this to "€4.9B by 2030"). On-board computer (GPS + wireless) penetration: Europe 90.3% (2025) → 93.9% (2030); North America 91.9% (2025) → 94.1% (2030). Fleet base: ~0.85M registered buses & coaches in Europe and ~1.1M in North America (2023).
   - GMI APTS (global): **$6.8B (2025) → $15.5B (2035), ~8.8% CAGR; US $2.4B (2025), ~8% CAGR 2026–2035; hardware 47% of 2025 revenue; public-transit operators 49% of end-user revenue.** DERIVED 2031 value at 8.8%: ~$11.3B global, ~$3.8B US.
   - These two reconcile reasonably: Berg's Europe+NA bus/tram figure (~$4.1B at 1.10 USD/EUR, DERIVED) is ~60% of GMI's global APTS, with the remainder plausibly rail-mode APTS and APAC/RoW.
4. **Berg's back-editions show the market has repeatedly beaten its own forecasts, especially in North America.** Europe €2.12B (2021) → €2.15B (2022) → €2.55B (2025); North America €0.76B (2021) → €0.88B (2022) → €1.22B (2025), i.e. a DERIVED ~11.5% CAGR for NA over 2022–2025 versus the 8.8% Berg forecast in its 2024 edition. Successive 2-region end-point forecasts have been revised up: €3.9B by 2026 (2022 ed.) → €4.3B by 2027 (2024 ed.) → ~€4.9B by 2030 (2026 ed.).
5. **Railway-side sizing is the weakest link.** Published "smart railways" figures (MarketsandMarkets $36.49B 2025 → $54.31B 2030, 8.3%) and UNIFE's train-control/signalling supply volume (€22.3B p.a., 2021–2023 avg., 10th World Rail Market Study) include mainline signalling, freight and infrastructure control well beyond passenger-facing transit ITS. The only narrower rail number found is Verified Market Research's railway passenger information system market ($9.45B 2023 → $19.93B 2030, 11.75%). A bottom-up rail carve-out (metro/LRT/commuter PIS, CCTV, APC, train tracking, asset management) will be needed; the 11th edition UNIFE (2026, Bain) segment split was not retrieved.
6. **Component mix is consistently ~47–49% hardware with software the fastest-growing slice** (Mordor: hardware 48.55% of 2025 ITS, software CAGR 13.84% to 2031; GMI APTS: hardware 47% in 2025). This supports a thesis that value is migrating from on-vehicle boxes to SaaS/analytics layers.
7. **Component sub-markets relevant to the hardware/software framework are each sized in the high single-digit billions and growing 8–12%:** automated passenger counting & information systems $9.70B (2025) → $14.80B (2030), 8.8% (MarketsandMarkets); passenger information systems $35.60B (2025) → $68.57B (2031), 11.6% (MarketsandMarkets; scope spans rail/road/air and is clearly broader than transit-only). These overlap heavily with each other and with APTS, so they must not be summed.
8. **Vendors named repeatedly as installed-base leaders in public-transport ITS:** Trapeze Group (Canada, Modaxo) and INIT (Germany) in both Europe and North America; Clever Devices and Conduent in North America; IVU in Europe (older Berg editions).

## 4. Reconciliation — why the numbers differ and which to use

**4.1 Why "global ITS" estimates range from $30B to $60B for the same year**
1. **Traffic-management weighting.** ATMS (signal control, incident detection on highways, VMS) is the largest system type in every report that discloses it (Mordor ATMS 38.05% of 2025). Vendors that weight toward urban traffic/smart-city infrastructure (Grand View, Mordor, IMARC, Zion) land at $50–58B; those that treat ITS as a narrower set of deployed systems (MarketsandMarkets, Precedence, Straits, Maximize) land at $38–43B.
2. **Tolling, parking and "ITS-enabled transportation pricing".** MarketsandMarkets' system list (via its Research and Markets listing) explicitly includes "Tolling & Parking Management Systems" and "Smart Ticketing Systems"; Grand View's system types include ATPS (advanced transportation pricing). These are adjacent to, not part of, our scope.
3. **Consumer/automotive telematics.** Verified Market Research's ITS is segmented by "automotive telematics" and "collision avoidance" applications; the 13%+ CAGR estimates (Future Market Insights, Statifacts, Dataintelo) are consistent with connected-vehicle/V2X content being included. These should be discarded for our purposes.
4. **Aviation and maritime.** All mainstream reports include airway and maritime modes (roughly a quarter to a third of the total if roadway is 33–39% and railway is the second-largest mode; exact rail/air/maritime splits were not retrievable).
5. **Edition drift and base-year rebasing.** MarketsandMarkets revised its long-run CAGR down to 5.4% in the Aug-2025 edition while Grand View's 2026 edition raised its base to $58.3B; comparing different editions of the same house produces apparent contradictions (e.g. MarketsandMarkets' US page citing both 5.0% and 5.4%).
6. **Currency and rounding.** Berg Insight reports in EUR; all others in USD. Berg's "€2.6B" in trade press is the rounded €2.55B.

**4.2 Scope mapping to our definition (public transit bus + rail; HW/SW/services)**
- Global ITS → roadway mode (33–39%) → APTS application (share not disclosed by any vendor retrieved) → **not derivable top-down from the global ITS reports alone.** Treat global ITS as a context/"umbrella" number only.
- **APTS (GMI, $6.8B 2025, 8.8%)** is the closest global proxy for bus + urban rail passenger-facing ITS (CAD/AVL, PIS, APC, scheduling/operations software, on-board surveillance). Hardware 47% / software + services 53%. Bus vs rail split not retrieved.
- **Berg Insight (€3.77B 2025 combined, DERIVED sum)** is the highest-quality bottom-up-validated number for bus + tram in Europe and North America; it is the recommended anchor for the Roadway segment in those two regions, with penetration (90–94%) and fleet (0.85M + 1.1M vehicles) enabling a per-vehicle revenue sanity check (DERIVED: €3.77B / ~1.95M vehicles ≈ €1,900 per registered bus/coach per year, before adjusting for the share of fleets actually in scheduled public service).
- **Rail**: no clean top-down number. Candidate building blocks: rail PIS ($9.45B 2023, VMR); train control/signalling supply (€22.3B p.a. global, UNIFE — includes freight and mainline, so only a fraction applies to metro/LRT/commuter "trackside detection and signaling"); "smart railways" ($36.49B, MarketsandMarkets — too broad). A rail carve-out should be built bottom-up from fleet/line counts (other workstreams) and cross-checked against these ceilings.
- **Component sub-markets (APC&IS $9.70B; PIS $35.60B)** overlap with each other and with APTS and span airports/airlines; use them for growth-rate triangulation (8.8–11.6%) rather than for additive sizing.
