# 22. Gap-fill: supplier-side bottom-up check of the EUR 20-22bn global CCS market and 2025 vendor share table

**Prepared:** 2026-11-07 (research sub-agent). **Purpose:** fill the gaps left by `09_rail_competitors.md` (Hitachi Rail, CRSC, CASCO, Nippon Signal, Kyosan, MERMEC, Stadler Signalling, CAF Signalling, Knorr-Bremse/Alstom deal price) and cross-check the top-down CCS anchor (SCI Verkehr ~EUR 20bn 2025 / UNIFE EUR 22.3bn 2021-23, both incl. freight, trackside + onboard + services) against reported vendor revenues.

**Scope reminder.** Public ITS = software, hardware and services sold to public transit operators/agencies. RAILWAY = metro, light rail/tram, commuter/regional and mainline passenger rail. The CCS (control, command and signalling) anchor and most vendor "signalling" lines bundle freight rail; freight-weighted lines are flagged in a separate column. Ticketing/fare collection (inside ex-Thales GTS and Nippon Signal "AFC") and road-traffic signals (inside Kyosan "Signaling Solutions") are ADJACENT and flagged.

**Method / caveats.** The shared WebSearch budget was exhausted before this agent started and WebFetch could not resolve any external host, so every document below was pulled directly with `curl` through the session proxy and read in full (HKEX filings, Hitachi/Siemens/Alstom/Stadler/CAF/Nippon Signal/Kyosan IR documents, SCI Verkehr and UITP PDFs, Railway Gazette articles, ECB FX file). Hosts that could not be read: thalesgroup.com (Incapsula bot wall; the Thales 2023 URD sanity check is therefore replaced by Hitachi's own disclosure of ex-GTS revenue), ertms.net deployment map (data is loaded by JavaScript into a Google Map; no supplier attributions in the HTML), www.hitachi.com `/New/cnews/...` paths (intermittent DNS failures; the 2021 Thales announcement and June-2025 IR Day deck could not be fetched, but the FY2025 results and June-2026 IR Day decks were), ir.knorr-bremse.com (JavaScript single-page app; Annual Report 2025 not retrievable), cafmobility.com deep pages (connection resets, succeeded on the 5th attempt), railjournal.com and casco.com.cn (403). ## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The bottom-up vendor sum lands at ~EUR 17bn for 2025, i.e. ~80% of the EUR 20-22bn top-down CCS anchor, which validates the anchor.** Adding the three previously unsourced houses (Hitachi Rail Mobility sector FY2025 revenue JPY 1,321.5bn = EUR 7.8bn, of which signalling ~52-60%; CRSC rail-transit control systems RMB 31.9bn = EUR 3.9bn; Nippon Signal railway signalling JPY 47.8bn and Kyosan signalling solutions JPY 71.1bn) to Siemens Rail Infrastructure (EUR 4.1bn derived), Alstom Signalling (EUR 2.7bn reported), Wabtec Digital Intelligence (USD 1.03bn), KB Signaling (~EUR 0.3bn), MERMEC (~EUR 0.43bn) and Stadler Signalling (CHF 117.5m) gives EUR 16.6-17.6bn depending on the Hitachi signalling share. Stripping out non-CCS content (Siemens electrification, Kyosan road-traffic signals, Wabtec NDT/inspection) gives a "clean" ~EUR 15.5-16bn, i.e. 70-80% coverage. The ~EUR 4-6bn residual is CAF Signalling (undisclosed), Progress Rail (undisclosed), the retained MERMEC/Angel Signaling business (EUR 2bn backlog), second-tier Chinese suppliers (Traffic Control Technology, Unittec, HollySys), Korean/Indian houses, and component/telecom specialists.
2. **The global market is a four-way tie at the top, not a "big three".** On a EUR 21bn midpoint anchor, Siemens Mobility Rail Infrastructure (~20%), Hitachi Rail signalling (~19-22%), CRSC (~19%) and Alstom Signalling (~13%) each hold 13-22%; everyone else is <5%. Ex-China the picture is a genuine big three: Siemens + Hitachi + Alstom = ~EUR 10.9bn = roughly two-thirds of the ~EUR 16-17bn non-Chinese market (my derivation: CRSC's China revenue EUR 4.0bn is >90% of the Chinese CCS market implied by SCI's regional statements). SCI's 2018 shares (CRSC 23% global; Siemens >35% Europe) are directionally confirmed: CRSC's share has drifted down to ~19% as Chinese new-build slowed, and Siemens won 44% (up to EUR 2.8bn of EUR 6.3bn) of DB's 2025 digital-interlocking/ETCS frameworks, consistent with a >35% European share.
3. **Hitachi Rail is now the largest pure signalling house by revenue and the most profitable of the Western three.** Mobility sector FY2025 (Mar-26) revenue JPY 1,321.5bn (+13%; EUR 7.8bn), adjusted EBITA JPY 108.1bn (8.2%; 9.2% excluding integration cost), FY2026 guidance JPY 1,350bn / 9.4%, FY2027 target 11%+, FY2030 13-14%. The former Thales GTS business alone generated JPY 391.0bn (EUR 2.3bn) at an 11.4% adjusted EBITA margin in FY2025. Portfolio target for FY2027: 52% signalling, 37% rolling stock, 11% digital; 76% recurring/software-based. Order backlog JPY 7.1tn (EUR 42bn, 5.4x revenue), gross margin in backlog 20.3%, 90%+ of FY2026 revenue already in backlog. Europe is 62% of Mobility revenue (JPY 822.5bn), North America 8%, Japan 6%.
4. **CRSC is a EUR 4.3bn domestic monopoly-like supplier with a tiny export book.** 2025 revenue RMB 34,553m (+6.4%; EUR 4.26bn), net profit RMB 3,686m (10.7% net margin), rail-transit-control-systems segment RMB 31,907m (+8.0%) at a 30.0% gross margin (design & integration 39.4%, equipment 36.6%, system implementation 13.9%). Overseas revenue was only RMB 2,357m (6.8% of revenue, -3.5%), with a 16.7% gross margin that fell 7.9 pts; overseas new contracts fell 33.9% to RMB 3.9bn. CRSC states it supplies the core train control for "the majority" of China's operating high-speed lines and holds ~40% of the domestic urban-rail control-system market (>190 lines in >40 cities), with full-chain services on >80% of operating/under-construction urban lines. Backlog RMB 115.86bn (3.4x revenue). CASCO (CRSC 51% / Alstom 49%, consolidated by CRSC) had sales of EUR 758m and net income of EUR 136m (17.9%) in the year to March 2026 per Alstom's URD, so Alstom's reported EUR 2.7bn signalling excludes its ~EUR 0.37bn economic share of CASCO.
5. **The Japanese houses are small, domestic and mid-margin.** Nippon Signal (TSE 6741) FY Mar-2025: railway-signalling sales JPY 47.8bn (EUR 0.29bn), orders JPY 41.6bn, backlog JPY 76.2bn (1.6x); Transportation Infrastructure segment JPY 56.5bn with an 8.0% segment margin; overseas transportation-infrastructure sales JPY 13.7bn (Taiwan Hualien-Taitung line, Vietnam AFC). Kyosan (TSE 6742) FY Mar-2025: Signaling Solutions sales JPY 71.1bn (EUR 0.43bn, includes road-traffic signals and platform doors; rail not split), overseas JPY 10bn (India electronic interlockings; Poland pilot), group operating margin 7.2%; plan FY Mar-2028 JPY 77bn signalling sales of which JPY 18bn overseas. UITP's automated-metro supplier chart (Dec-2018 base, 1,026 km) gives Kyosan 2% and Kobelco 6% of fully automated km, i.e. the Japanese suppliers matter in Japan/Asia GoA4 but nowhere else.
6. **The second tier is being absorbed by the first tier.** Knorr-Bremse paid USD 690m (EUR ~0.64bn) for Alstom Signaling North America (closed 2 Sept 2024), a ~EUR 300m revenue, ~16% EBIT-margin conventional wayside business (800 staff, six sites) that completed Alstom's EUR 2bn deleveraging plan; Siemens Mobility agreed (May 2026) to buy MERMEC's Italian signalling and global diagnostics businesses (~EUR 430m FY25 revenue, 1,700 staff, 70+ countries; closing expected end-2026; price undisclosed), leaving Angelo Holding with Angel Signaling (800 staff, ~EUR 2bn backlog, incl. the ex-Hitachi French/German/UK mainline signalling units and the AngelStar ETCS JV with Stadler); Wabtec bought Frauscher (train detection, ~USD 792m). Stadler Signalling (CHF 117.5m revenue 2025, +140% on the back of the CHF 500m Atlanta CBTC order; backlog CHF 549.9m = 4.7x revenue; ~3% of Stadler group revenue) and CAF Signalling (revenue not disclosed; ~EUR 60m Helsinki metro resignalling award in 2025) are the only rolling-stock OEMs still building signalling organically, and both say explicitly they did so to stop buying critical subsystems from direct competitors.
7. **Margins and backlog depth confirm the barrier-to-entry story.** Disclosed 2025 signalling-related margins cluster at 8-12% EBITA for the system integrators (Siemens Mobility 8.8% group; Hitachi Mobility 8.2% reported / 11.4% in ex-GTS; Alstom group 6.1% falling to ~(4)% in H1 FY26/27) versus 16-18% for component/wayside specialists (KB Signaling ~16% EBIT; CASCO 17.9% net; Knorr RVS 17.1%; Wabtec Freight 19.5%) and 30% gross for CRSC's control-systems segment. Backlog cover is 3.4-5.4 years for every system house (Alstom signalling 3.6x, Siemens Mobility 4.2x, Hitachi 5.4x, CRSC 3.4x, Stadler Signalling 4.7x), which is the practical moat.
8. **Freight-weighted revenue is EUR ~1.5-2bn of the vendor sum and must be flagged, not added, for a public-transit case:** Wabtec Digital Intelligence (USD 1,030m 2025, +31%, majority PTC/locomotive electronics plus Evident NDT from Jul-25 and Frauscher from Dec-25), Progress Rail (undisclosed; Caterpillar "Transportation" USD 5.3bn bundles rail + marine), KB Signaling (North American wayside, Class I freight customers alongside MBTA/LA/Chicago transit), and roughly half of CRSC's "Railway" product line (RMB 21.5bn covers mixed-traffic conventional lines as well as passenger-only HSR).
