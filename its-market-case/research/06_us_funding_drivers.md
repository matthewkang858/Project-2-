# 06 — US Public-Sector Demand Drivers and Funding for Public Transit ITS (Bus + Rail)

**Research date:** 2026-11-07 | **Analyst scope:** US federal/state funding, policy and regulatory drivers that create (or suppress) demand for ITS software, hardware and services sold to public transit agencies (bus and passenger rail). Fare collection and highway/tolling ITS are out of scope except where sources bundle them.

**Method / caveats:** All figures below were taken from web-search result snippets of the cited sources. Confidence: **high** = primary source or 2+ consistent independent sources; **medium** = single secondary source; **low** = inferred/unclear scope.

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The federal transit money is still large but is now in a "lame-duck" phase.** IIJA authorized up to **$108.15B for FTA over FY2022–FY2026** (avg. **$21.4B/yr** vs. $12.8B/yr under the FAST Act). FY2026 enacted appropriations (P.L. 119-75, Feb 3 2026) provided **$21.1B for public transit** (+$168M vs FY2025) and **$15.9B for passenger/freight rail** (−$298M). IIJA authorizations expired **Sept 30 2026** and were extended only to **Dec 11 2026 (P.L. 119-103)** *without* the ~$17.45B/yr of IIJA advance appropriations for transit and rail, implying roughly a **20% cut to transit and 82% cut to rail** funding versus enacted levels while the extension runs (UCS/NACo figures). Multi-year formula funds already apportioned (FY2022–FY2026) remain obligable, which cushions agency ITS procurement through roughly FY2028–FY2029, but new-award pipelines (Low-No, CIG, SMART) have already shrunk sharply.
2. **Reauthorization direction of travel is "flat-to-down, formula-heavy, discretionary-light."** The House T&I **BUILD America 250 Act (H.R. 8870)**, reported 62-2 in May 2026, authorizes **$103.3B (incl. $87.6B guaranteed) for transit over FY2027–FY2031**, which APTA scores as **−$16.5B (−14%)** vs. the IIJA baseline; but it *raises* Section 5307 urbanized-area formula funds to **~$39.99B over five years (from $33.54B under IIJA; $7.745B in FY2027)**, provides **no dedicated Low-No program**, no multiyear advance appropriations, and makes CIG ($3B/yr authorized) fully dependent on annual appropriations. The Senate (Banking/EPW/Commerce) had taken no action as of mid-2026. The FY2027 President's request would cut FTA **22–23% (to ~$16.3B)** and CIG new budget authority from $1.7B to $1.2B. Net for ITS vendors: formula-funded, state-of-good-repair-type purchases (CAD/AVL refresh, APCs, onboard video, PIDs, signaling renewal) are the most durable federal demand through 2031; large discretionary-funded expansions and zero-emission fleet programs are the most exposed.
3. **The operating "fiscal cliff" has largely been patched by states in 2025–2026, not solved.** Illinois (NITA Act, ~$1.5B/yr, signed Dec 16 2025), the Bay Area (Regional Measure RTM/SB 63, ~$1B/yr for 14 years, passed Nov 3 2026 with ~62% yes; plus a $590M AB 117 state bridge loan), New York (MTA 2025–2029 $68.4B capital plan funded via $31.5B payroll-mobility-tax expansion + $3B state + $3B city + $3B MTA efficiencies) and Oregon (HB 3991, transit payroll tax doubled to 0.2% through Jan 1 2028, but suspended by a referendum petition) have moved; Pennsylvania has **not** (FY2026-27 budget signed July 2026 with no new transit operating money; SEPTA still carries a $192M structural deficit after a $394M two-year capital-to-operating transfer). Washington repealed a 2025 services sales tax that Sound Transit was counting on. Implication: agency *technology* budgets are being protected where states acted (IL, CA, NY) and squeezed where they did not (PA, OR, WA); several rescue packages explicitly tie new money to reform/oversight (NITA) that favors integrated data, planning and performance-management software.
4. **Ridership has recovered to ~81–85% of 2019** (APTA: 8.1B trips in 2025, +6.0% y/y; 81% of pre-pandemic for full-year 2025, 83% in Dec 2025, 85% peak in April 2025) and is still growing in 2026 (March 2026 +12% m/m). Recovery is uneven by mode and market, which sustains demand for real-time passenger information, service planning and demand-responsive tools, but farebox recovery remains structurally below 2019, keeping agencies dependent on the state packages above.
5. **Zero-emission bus adoption is still growing in stock terms (8,116 full-size ZEBs funded/ordered/delivered as of July 2025, +16% y/y; FCEB +49%) but the federal tailwind has reversed.** FY2025 Low-No/Bus awards (~$2B, 165 projects) went 97% to *low*-emission (not zero-emission) buses; the FY2026 NOFO shrank to **$610M** ($589M Low-No + ~$21M Bus) and explicitly prioritizes low-emission over zero-emission. NFI delivered 1,326 ZEBs in 2025 (~30% of 4,451 EUs) with a $13.0B backlog and guided to a *lower* ZEB mix in 2026 plus tariff cost pressure. California's ICT rule (50% ZEB purchases for large agencies in 2026–2028, 100% from 2029) remains the main hard mandate, and CARB has deferred the Class 5–6 cutaway phase-in for 2026–2028. For ITS, each ZEB still carries incremental telematics/charge-management/energy-optimization software demand, but order volumes for new vehicles (and therefore factory-installed ITS) are flat (NA heavy-duty deliveries ~4,873 in 2025, expected flat).
6. **Regulatory mandates are a steadier ITS demand floor than discretionary grants.** PTASP (final rule effective May 13 2024: safety committees, risk-reduction programs, safety targets), TAM/NTD annual asset-inventory and condition reporting, NTD's FTA-approved APC methodology requirements (approval of benchmarking plans, triennial recertification, statistician-certified sampling, CEO D-10 certification), FRA PTC certification for every system change (Amtrak, PATH and MBTA all filed amendments in 2026), and TSA cybersecurity Security Directives for rail transit/passenger rail (SD 1582-21-01E, Jan 2026; SD 1580/82-2022-01E, May 2026) with a pending permanent surface cyber rule (NPRM Nov 7 2024 covering ~293 operators; not finalized as of June 2026) all create non-discretionary spend on counting, monitoring, incident-detection, cybersecurity and signaling systems through 2031.
7. **Federal administration actions in 2025–2026 add execution risk rather than eliminating demand.** FTA lost ~30% of staff by May 2025 (791→553; 781 FTE in FY2024 → 552 in FY2026), DOT reviewed >3,200 unobligated grants and pulled/withdrew selected awards (e.g., $65.8M from seven RAISE projects; Boston's Roxbury FTA grant), froze ~$18B for NY's Hudson Tunnel and Second Avenue Subway on Oct 1 2025 (later ruled "flagrantly" illegal and restored), and attempted immigration-enforcement and DEI grant conditions (permanently enjoined Nov 4 2025; appeal filed then moved to dismiss). Buy America remains strictly enforced for rolling stock (70% domestic content, US final assembly) with narrow waivers (vans/minivans extended 5 years in Nov 2024; electric minibus partial waiver proposed Aug 2026). For a new ITS entrant: grant timing slippage, Buy America/BABA documentation and the political re-labelling of projects (away from "green/equity") are now routine commercial frictions.
8. **USDOT ITS JPO is a small, replication-oriented funder for transit ITS, not a scale driver.** ITS4US ($40M across four sites incl. NFTA and HIRTA transit deployments; five-year post-award operations commitment) and the 2026 ITS4US Deployment Accelerator (up to 5 × $325K, applications due Jan 11 2027) are pilots; the IIJA SMART program ($100M/yr FY2022–26; $16.7M to 11 transit projects in its second round) had **$204.9M of unobligated balances reallocated** by the FY2026 appropriations act. Expect federal *innovation* money for transit ITS to be marginal through 2031; the volume is in formula and SGR funds.

### 2.2 Surface transportation reauthorization (status as of Nov 7 2026)

- **Expiration and extension.** IIJA surface authorizations expired Sept 30 2026. Congress extended them to **Dec 11 2026 via P.L. 119-103**, attached to a continuing resolution (Senate 90-6; reported Aug 8 or Aug 11 2026 depending on source). The extension **does not continue IIJA advance appropriations**, leaving a **$36.8B annual shortfall across surface programs from Oct 1 2026** (NACo) and, per UCS, a 25% cut to enacted transportation funding overall, **20% for transit and 82% for rail**. NACo warns of >15% overall reductions. Analysts expect further short-term extensions; advance appropriations are unlikely to be restored in a short-term CR before the midterms.
- **House bill — BUILD America 250 Act (H.R. 8870).** Introduced May 19 2026; reported by House T&I after a 14–15-hour markup on May 21–22 2026 (62-2 per AASHTO; 61-2 per NACo) with a Graves–Larsen manager's amendment. Five-year, ~$580B bill; **$87.6B guaranteed for FTA (APTA: $103.3B authorized incl. $87.6B guaranteed, FY2027–FY2031)**. APTA scores it **−$16.5B (−14%)** vs. an IIJA baseline of $119.9B/$101.6B guaranteed. Program design: **5307 rises to ~$39.99B over five years ($7.745B in FY2027)**; **5339 state set-aside $4M→$6M**, 62% formula / 38% discretionary with 5% rural set-aside; **no dedicated Low-No program**; **CIG authorized at $3B/yr but subject to annual appropriations**; **no multiyear advance appropriations** (contract authority instead); bridge formula $9.2B/yr and a $2.4B/yr Surface Transportation Accelerator Grant program on the highway side; assured highway funding +7% vs IIJA. CRS notes the bill's tilt toward operating assistance could slow expansion and SGR backlog reduction. As of July 2026 (H&K) the bill had seen no House floor action and the Senate (EPW highways; Commerce rail/safety; Banking transit) had not moved.
- **FY2027 President's request (Apr 3 2026):** FTA −22% vs FY2026 (CRS) / $16.3B, −23% (APTA); CIG new BA $1.7B→$1.2B (CRS) or −$2.1B/−63% including advance appropriations (HDR); no IIJA advance appropriations requested; rescinds $4.2B of unspent EV-charging funds (ACEC). Congress had not acted on it as of the sources' dates.

**Implication for ITS demand:** Base-case federal transit funding FY2027–FY2031 is roughly 85–100% of IIJA levels with a heavier formula share (5307 +19% in the House bill), which favors recurring technology refresh (CAD/AVL, APC, onboard video, PIDs, scheduling software) over new-start megaprojects and ZEB-linked packages. Downside case (FY2027 request enacted or serial CRs without advance appropriations) is a ~20% federal transit cut and ~80% rail cut for the duration.

### 2.4 Federal administration actions 2025–2026 affecting transit grants

- **Grant conditions (immigration/DEI):** Secretary Duffy's Apr 24 2025 letter imposed an "Immigration Enforcement Condition" and anti-DEI certifications across DOT grants (FHWA, FRA, FTA terms and conditions). *California v. USDOT* (D.R.I., 1:25-cv-00208): preliminary injunction June 19 2025; **permanent injunction and summary judgment Nov 4 2025** (no statutory authority; APA violations). Government appealed Jan 2 2026 (1st Cir. 26-1026) and later moved to dismiss its own appeal (per NACo). Sources differ on whether the DEI portion was vacated.
- **Project freezes:** On Oct 1 2025 DOT placed the Hudson Tunnel (Gateway) and Second Avenue Subway under "administrative review" citing DEI contracting, halting ~$18B of remaining federal funding and a $300M reimbursement; a federal judge found the Gateway freeze "flagrantly" illegal and permanently barred repeat freezes; funding restored (construction briefly suspended Feb 2026; restored Mar 10 and fully by June 29 2026 per a single source).
- **Unobligated-grant review:** DOT reviewed >3,200 inherited unobligated grants, approving batches (e.g., 529 grants) after removing "DEI/Green New Scam" requirements, and withdrew $65.8M from seven RAISE projects; Boston sued over withdrawal of the Roxbury Resilient Corridors FTA grant (EV charging element). Congress eliminated unobligated Neighborhood Access and Equity funds in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.
- **Low-No pivot:** FY2025 awards 97% low-emission; FY2026 NOFO cut to $610M and prioritizes low-emission.
- **Buy America:** rolling stock must exceed 70% domestic component cost with US final assembly (49 CFR 661), with pre-award/post-delivery audits; vans/minivans partial waiver extended 5 years (Nov 2024, rescindable); electric minibus partial waiver proposed, supplemental notice Aug 18 2026 (67 comments; closed Sept 2 2026; no final decision seen); DOT OIG found FTA Buy America guidance weaknesses in SEPTA/CRRC oversight.
- **FTA capacity:** staff −30.1% (791→553) by May 2025 (Eno); 781 FTE FY2024 → 552 FY2026 → 547 proposed FY2027 (legis1); FY2027 request −22% for FTA.

**Implication:** Expect slower obligations/reimbursements, more documentation (BABA/Buy America for hardware such as cameras, displays, sensors), and scope re-labelling; no evidence of a cut to formula flows other than via the reauthorization/advance-appropriation mechanics above.

### 2.6 Zero-emission bus mandates and adoption

- **Stock:** 8,116 full-size ZEBs funded/ordered/delivered/deployed as of July 2025 (+16% vs July 2024's 7,028); BEB 7,261 (+13%); FCEB 855 (+49%); ~1,400 small ZEBs (+20%); CALSTART's 2025 edition projected >15,000 full-size ZEBs by 2030 at current pace; growth spreading to Southeast/Midwest (CALSTART ZIO-ZEB March 2026).
- **OEM data:** NFI 2025 deliveries 4,451 EUs (2024: 4,547), 1,326 ZEBs (~30%), Q4 ZEB share 27.4%, backlog $13.0B; NFI estimates 2025 US+Canada heavy-duty transit deliveries at 4,873 units and expects a flat industry; 2026 guidance assumes tariff-related cost increases and a lower ZEB share of deliveries.
- **Supplier shake-out:** Proterra transit unit Chapter 11 (Aug 2023; 26 interested parties, no out-of-court buyer; Phoenix Motorcars acquired the bus business); Nova Bus exiting the US; agencies (e.g., CapMetro) reported Proterra reliability problems; Eno attributes failures partly to over-customization in US procurement.
- **California ICT:** large agencies 25% ZEB purchases 2023–2025, **50% in 2026–2028, 100% from 2029**; small agencies 25% from 2026; CARB will not phase in Class 5–6 cutaways in 2026–2028; CARB review of 2026–2028 program readiness underway; ACF amendments filed Sept 10 2026 add modest flexibility and address contractor fleets.
- **Federal:** Low-No FY2026 at $610M prioritizing low-emission; BUILD America 250 provides no dedicated Low-No; FY2027 request rescinds $4.2B EV-charging funds.

**Implication:** ZEB-linked ITS (charge management, energy telematics, depot software) remains a California/state-mandate story through 2031; nationally, bus-count-driven ITS (factory-fit CAD/AVL, APC, video) tracks a flat ~4,500–4,900 unit/yr heavy-duty market.

### 2.8 USDOT ITS JPO and innovation programs

- **ITS JPO** operates under the USDOT 2022–2026 Strategic Plan / RD&T plan (six goals). **ITS4US**: $40M (one page says launched 2019 with $38M) across four sites — HIRTA (Iowa; Health Connector linking transit scheduling with healthcare appointments), Georgia DOT, **Niagara Frontier Transportation Authority** (connected vehicles, transit signal priority, ML/predictive analytics, accessibility), University of Washington; sites must operate ≥5 years after Phase 3. **ITS4US Deployment Accelerator (2026, with FTA):** up to 5 awards × $325K, no cost share, 18-month performance, replication of HIRTA's model; applications due Jan 11 2027 (subject to change). ITS JPO NOW newsletter (July 2026) continues.
- **SMART grants (IIJA):** $100M/yr FY2022–26; FY2024 round $54M Stage 1 (34 projects) + $85M Stage 2 (8 awards); earlier round $16.7M to 11 transit projects; **$204.9M unobligated reallocated by P.L. 119-75**.

**Implication:** Federal ITS R&D/pilot money for transit is <$0.1B/yr and shrinking; it validates use cases (TSP, CV, demand-response integration) but does not move the market.
