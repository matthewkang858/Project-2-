## 1. Key takeaways

1. **"Incumbents usually win" is wrong for competitive rebids and right for the installed base as a whole.** Across 39 documented decision points on incumbent-held ITS accounts (2016-2026), 25 (64%) left the incumbent in place -- but 21 of those 25 were extensions, sole-source renewals, cooperative-contract buys or contract modifications with no full rebid (CTA 2024 sole source USD 43.4M; Miami-Dade 2025 non-competitive 5-year extension USD 11M; NJ Transit 2026 sole source USD 6.4M; GCRTA 2023 non-competitive USD 4.6M; Broward 2019/2026 amendments to USD 18.8M; AC Transit 2022-2025 extensions then a USD 6.8M renewal; LA Metro GIRO USD 3.4M; Met Council Trapeze USD 5.0M; MTA/Hayden modifications to USD 301.7M). **When an incumbent-held system actually went to a competitive rebid, the incumbent won only 4 of 18 (22%)** -- and in bus CAD/AVL it lost **8 of 8** (LA Metro, TfL, TTC, Kitsap, CapMetro, Santa Monica, Marin, Metrolinx; 7 of 7 inside 2018-2026). The two high-confidence incumbent wins at rebid are maintenance/sub-system contracts (MTA bus-camera maintenance to Seon with 2 bidders; AC Transit APC to its CAD/AVL vendor Clever), plus Metra/Railhead cameras and SFMTA, where the winner (Hitachi Rail GTS) is the corporate successor of the 1998 Thales/Alcatel SelTrac supplier.
2. **The barrier is tenure, not rebid odds: incumbent-held systems reach a decision point after a median 16 years (n=23), and the systems that were rebid and lost were a median 17.5 years old (n=10, range 5-30).** TTC's CIS was 30 years old, Santa Monica's radio-CAD/AVL 19 (2002), LA Metro's ATMS I 21, CapMetro's 19 (2006), Marin's GMV 16 (2009), TfL's Siemens iBus 16, Metrolinx's INIT 13, East Bay Paratransit's platform ~24. In between, the incumbent collects 5-year support renewals at USD 1.7-3.3k per bus per year (SORTA, AC Transit, GCRTA, Miami-Dade, CTA) and sells add-on modules (CTA Disruption Management USD 2.3M; County Connection Clever Insights SaaS; Miami-Dade EVMS). For a new entrant the implication is that the addressable rebid pool is the ~1/16th of the installed base that reaches end-of-life each year, but when it does, the odds favour the challenger.
3. **Bus CAD/AVL full-system replacement prices cluster at USD 18-29k per vehicle (n=8 of 10, 2021-2026) with two outliers at USD 43-45k where radio/VoIP and 5-year hosted maintenance are bundled (Santa Monica USD 8.1M/189 buses; LA Metro USD 129.8M/2,900 vehicles).** MARTA USD 18.1k (USD 10.5M/584, 8-year), Brampton USD 18.3k, Marin USD 18.8k (5-year incl. maintenance), CapMetro USD 20.0k (5-year), TfL USD 23.1k (10-year incl. service), Metrolinx USD 25.4k (15-year incl. options, secondary value), TTC USD 25.7k (10-year), Kitsap USD 28.2k (incl. APC/AVA/RTPI; fleet count low confidence). Median across the ten = USD 24.2k per vehicle; base contract terms median 5 years, total including options median 10 years (n=11).
4. **Recurring CAD/AVL support/hosting for an incumbent is USD 1.7-3.3k per bus per year (n=6 comparable renewals), USD 6.6k at WMATA where a 3-year contract also funds onboard hardware upgrades, and USD 24k at LADOT where the renewal bundles video, infotainment and operations support.** SORTA USD 1,656 (USD 2.79M/5 yrs/337 vehicles), AC Transit USD 2,173 (USD 6.79M/5/625, cloud hosting included: Azure USD 37,955 per month), Miami-Dade USD 2,604 (USD 11M/5/845), GCRTA USD 2,886, CTA USD 3,265 (USD 43.4M/7/1,900; 2017 contract USD 3,001). This is the "service tail" that INIT reports as ~20% of revenue (file 17) and it is sole-sourced almost everywhere.
5. **Real-time/analytics SaaS over an existing CAD/AVL costs USD 0.2-2.0k per vehicle per year depending on module count; scheduling SaaS ~USD 1.1-1.8k per bus per year.** Swiftly: LA Metro USD 169 (predictions only, ~2,000 vehicles), NCTD USD 247-493 (5-year base USD 828,750 for ~336 buses + rail), AC Transit USD 714 (USD 1.35M/3 yrs/630 buses via NASPO/Carahsoft), County Connection USD 1,040 (3 modules, 2023) rising to USD 1,972 (5 modules, 2025), IndyGo USD 2,597 per revenue vehicle in year 1 (USD 384,345 for up to 148 revenue + 65 non-revenue vehicles; stepped pricing explicitly tied to revenue-vehicle count). Optibus at Tulsa: USD 302,591 for 3 years (+2-year option) on 56 buses = USD 1,801 per bus per year (USD 1,082 if spread over 5 years). Clever's own analytics module (Clever Insights) is USD 284-397 per bus per year at County Connection. Spare's microtransit list price is USD 4,500 per vehicle per year (+USD 15,000 base, +USD 650 per vehicle voice) at 10 vehicles, i.e. ~USD 375-620 per vehicle per month; East Bay Paratransit pays USD 4.57M over 7 years (USD 1.52M implementation + USD 2.55M for 5 years O&M).
6. **Enforcement cameras price at USD 20-23k per camera-unit per year on fixed-fee contracts (LA Metro USD 10.5M/100 units/5.25 yrs; LADOT USD 3.43M/50/3 yrs), USD 20.4k per system one-off at WMATA (USD 2.86M/140), and up to USD 30k per bus per year on AC Transit's revenue-share cap (USD 2,500 per bus per month).** MTA's Hayden contract grew from USD 19.7M (300 systems + 200 option, July 2022) to USD 83.1M after Modification 1 (up to 1,023 systems, Dec 2023; USD 81k per system) and USD 301.7M after Modification 2 (2025, incl. options and bus-stop/double-parking scope), with a second vendor (Seon) added at ~USD 58M for 1,000 buses -- i.e. the enforcement incumbent is retained by modification, not rebid, because citation revenue (LA Metro >USD 21M gross) funds it.
7. **Onboard video: USD 11k per bus for retrofit systems (CapMetro/Luminator USD 2.96M/272; Golden Gate/Apollo USD 0.9M/80), USD 22-39k per railcar with nine cameras (Metra/Railhead USD 15.5M for 400-700 cars), and USD 0.9-1.6k per vehicle per year for maintenance-only contracts (SEPTA/Gatekeeper USD 13.4M/5 yrs/~3,000 vehicles; MTA/Seon USD 25.7M base/3 yrs/5,248 buses).** APC: USD 7.3k per vehicle for a turnkey 5-year deployment (Marin/UTA USD 502,875/69 vehicles; USD 5.0k of it sensor hardware), USD 8.2k per newly equipped or upgraded bus at AC Transit (USD 1.7M/3 yrs/~208 vehicles + USD 150k per year hosting), and USD 2.2k per vehicle over 5 years for a SaaS data-refinement contract (Riverside/UTA USD 376,659 vs Clever's USD 501,440 bid).
8. **Rail train-control outside New York: SFMTA's Hitachi SelTrac contract is USD 212.1M capital for 71 route-miles (USD 2.99M per route-mile) plus USD 114.1M for 10 years of support and USD 237.7M of 2x5-year support options (a 29-year relationship, two bidders, Siemens' support price 1.5x Hitachi's); MBTA's Green Line Train Protection System is ~USD 113M for 26.7 route-miles (USD 4.2M per mile) to a new entrant, Piper Networks.** These sit below the NYC USD 18-45M per km programme figures in file 12 and near BART (USD 4.0M/km) and MARTA (USD 6.5M/km).
9. **Hosting is moving to vendor cloud/SaaS inside incumbent contracts, which raises the recurring take without a rebid:** AC Transit migrates Clever from Otava to Azure (USD 37,955 per month); Sound Transit converted its new Clever BOT contract from on-prem to cloud SaaS for +USD 2.1M (+24%) with ~USD 1.5M per year subscriptions from 2028; Miami-Dade's extension adds "perpetual and SaaS licences"; LA Metro ATMS II is cloud-hosted; Santa Monica specified vendor-hosted. Of the 2021-2026 CAD/AVL awards with a stated hosting model, all five are vendor-hosted or cloud.
10. **Procurement intensity is low and getting lower: 2-6 proposals per CAD/AVL RFP (MARTA 6, Kitsap 6, Santa Monica 6, Marin 6 complete of 10, CapMetro 5, AC Transit 3, Miami-Dade 2013: Clever/Trapeze/INIT), 2 for SFMTA CBTC, 2 for MTA bus-camera maintenance, 2 for NCTD and Riverside, 3 for LA Metro enforcement (Gatekeeper, Hayden, Seon) and 3 for LA Metro Swiftly.** Pilots before award are now common (MARTA 90-day live pilot on 10 buses per vendor; Miami-Dade plans a two-phase proof-of-concept RFP; Hayden won AC Transit and NYC after POCs), which favours vendors with deployable kit and reference fleets.

## 5. What the dataset says for the case

- **Barrier-to-entry argument (file 11) should be restated.** The defensible claim is not "incumbents win rebids" but "incumbent systems are rebid roughly once every 15-20 years, and between rebids the incumbent is renewed without competition at USD 1.7-3.3k per bus per year and is the default supplier of add-on modules". When a rebid does occur the challenger wins ~4 times in 5 (and 8 of 8 in bus CAD/AVL). The rebid pool is therefore small but open, and the incumbency moat is about timing, switching cost and the service annuity, not procurement capture.
- **Bottom-up price points for the model (file 17 gaps closed):** bus CAD/AVL capex USD 18-29k per vehicle (core range) / ~USD 24k median, USD 43-45k when radio+hosting+5-yr maintenance are bundled; CAD/AVL support USD 1.7-3.3k per bus per year; real-time/analytics SaaS USD 0.2-2.0k per vehicle per year (USD ~0.7-1.0k typical for a 3-5-module stack), with explicit per-revenue-vehicle pricing at IndyGo; scheduling SaaS ~USD 1.1-1.8k per bus per year; microtransit/paratransit SaaS USD 4.5k per vehicle per year list; enforcement cameras USD 20-23k per unit per year (fixed) or USD 30k per bus per year cap (revenue share); onboard video USD 11k per bus retrofit, USD 0.9-1.6k per vehicle per year maintenance; APC USD 2.2-8.2k per vehicle; LRT CBTC USD 3.0-4.2M per route-mile outside NYC.
- **Cloud/SaaS is being adopted inside incumbent relationships** (AC Transit Azure migration, Sound Transit SaaS conversion, Miami-Dade SaaS licences), which is both a risk (incumbents capture the recurring layer) and an opening (agencies now budget subscriptions, e.g. Sound Transit ~USD 1.5M per year from operations).
- **Scope-expansion by incumbents is the main organic growth mechanism:** CTA Disruption Management (+USD 2.3M), Miami-Dade EVMS, County Connection Insights, MTA Hayden modifications (+USD 282M), Broward +USD 5M renewals.
