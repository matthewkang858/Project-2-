# 13. Technology and business-model trends in public ITS — adoption evidence

**Scope of this note:** technology and business-model shifts reshaping software/hardware/services sold to public bus and passenger-rail operators, with adoption evidence (deployments, volumes, spend, leaders) and implications for a new entrant. Base year 2025/26; forecasts to 2031 where sources give them. Prepared 2026-11-07.

**Method / caveats.** The shared WebSearch budget for this run was exhausted before this agent started, so every fact below comes from pages fetched directly (via the session's HTTPS proxy with curl) and read in full: vendor press pages, agency press releases, EU/ERA/UIC pages, Wikipedia compilations of agency capital-program documents, the Mobility Database feed catalog (CSV), and trade press (Streetsblog NYC, amNY, Sustainable Bus, Railway Gazette). Direct fetches of mta.info, transit.dot.gov, apta.com, gtfs.org, calstart.org PDF and some vendor pages returned 403/404; where a number comes from a vendor it is marked "vendor claim". Pricing / revenue-share terms for bus-camera enforcement contracts could not be verified from a primary document (MTA board documents are behind a 403) and are flagged as a gap.

## Key takeaways

1. **Bus-mounted AI enforcement is the fastest-scaling new hardware+software category in bus ITS.** NYC MTA's ACE program went from 123 buses (ABLE, 2019) to 623 buses/14 routes (Aug 2024), 1,000 buses/34 routes (Nov 2024), 1,400 buses/54 routes (Jan 2026) and 1,900+ buses/64 routes/810 route-miles (Jun 2026), under a USD 141m contract (Apr 2024) that targets 2,000 buses by end-2027 (MTA has also said 93 routes / 3,000+ buses by end-2027). Hayden AI reports >2,100 buses equipped worldwide and 11 US agencies (NYC, DC, LA Metro, AC Transit, Sacramento, SEPTA, Culver City, Santa Monica, West Hollywood, Chicago CTA, MBTA) plus UK VCA type approval and pilots in Barcelona, Belgium and London. Measured outcomes (MTA): +5% average bus speed (up to 30% on segments), -20% collisions, -40% blocked bus stops, 91% of violators ticketed only once.
2. **The economics are driven by citation volumes, which are very large.** NYC cameras issued >400,000 bus-stop/double-parking tickets in the first five months of ACE (Aug–Dec 2024); December 2024 alone produced 76,673 bus-stop and 64,978 double-parking tickets (>USD 11m of fines per tranche); bus-camera and NYPD bus-lane/stop fines totalled USD 225.5m over 2023–24, 76% of them camera-issued (93% in Dec 2024). Philadelphia (152 SEPTA buses) issued 211,000+ violations in year one; LA Metro went from ~570 manual citations/month to ~10,000/month with cameras (vendor claim). This makes enforcement one of the few ITS products with a self-funding / revenue-generating business case rather than a pure cost line.
3. **Zero-emission bus adoption has crossed the tipping point and is pulling charge/energy management into the scheduling stack.** Battery-electric buses were 60% of the EU city-bus market in 2025 (11,607 BEB registrations >8t, +48% YoY; H1 2026: 8,141 units, +49%, 61.2% of city buses). In the US, full-size ZEBs funded/ordered/delivered passed 8,100 as of July 2025 (+16% YoY; fuel-cell +49%), still a small share of the ~70k-bus fleet. Vendors are converging from both sides: schedulers (Optibus EV scheduling/EVEMS), charge-management specialists (The Mobility House ChargePilot, ChargePoint fleet, VEV ">25% of the UK e-bus fleet", Tenix, Hitachi ZeroCarbon) and OEM/utility players.
4. **CAD/AVL is moving from hardware-heavy, 1990s-architecture systems to cloud/AI data platforms with thin onboard hardware.** Swiftly (200+ agencies, 10 countries, >half of the 25 largest US agencies by bus ridership) launched a cloud CAD/AVL in Sept 2026 that "requires less hardware", runs 60+ OTA updates/week and already co-exists with legacy CAD/AVL at 100+ agencies; it cites a third-party survey in which only 6% of 75+ agencies would strongly recommend their CAD/AVL. Optibus's Real-Time Suite (Sept 2026) overlays existing CAD/AVL/GPS "with no new hardware". Standards (ITxPT MQTT-based S04 specs live Apr 2026; new APC, logon and ticketing-validation specs 2026) are unbundling onboard modules. Incumbents are responding by consolidation (Hitachi Rail–Clever Devices, 2026; INIT–Applied Autonomy, Oct 2026; Siemens–MERMEC, Nov 2026).
5. **Open data is effectively universal in North America and Western Europe.** The Mobility Database catalog (downloaded Nov 2026) lists 4,676 GTFS schedule feeds (2,881 active) from 3,537 providers in 94 countries and 2,011 GTFS-Realtime feeds (1,850 active) in 35 countries; the US alone has 1,706 GTFS and 810 GTFS-RT feeds. GTFS stewardship moved from Google to MobilityData (2025/26). Real-time prediction accuracy, not feed existence, is now the differentiator (Swiftly "ETA completeness", Optibus "ghost bus" positioning).
6. **Account-based ticketing / open payments has become the default fare architecture in North America** (Masabi alone: 400+ agencies; 2026 ABT go-lives at St. Louis Metro, Halifax, Saskatoon, Duluth, San Joaquin RTD; open-payments launches at RTD Denver, RTC Southern Nevada, COTA, Rochester RTS, Kaua'i, LAWA; WMATA "Tap. Ride. Go."). Fare collection is adjacent to ITS scope but the convergence matters: the same vendors are bundling journey planning + real-time info + payments into one agency app (Masabi "Passenger", Oct 2026), and APC/validator data is being standardised by ITxPT.
7. **On-demand/microtransit is consolidating around a few platforms and expanding rather than retrenching in 2025–26 at the sources we could reach** (Via IPO Sept 2025, USD 434m 2025 revenue per sibling file; ioki US entry Oct 2026 after Benteler bought it from DB; Argo CAD 10m raise; HRT, RTS Rochester, TfGM expansions; Optibus adding GTFS-Flex DRT planning). Evidence of programme cancellations exists in older commentary (TransitCenter 2019) but we could not verify 2025–26 retrenchment cases — flagged as a gap.
8. **Rail resignalling is the largest single technology-driven spend pool and is accelerating, but it is a closed, duopolistic market.** NYC Subway still runs fixed-block signals on ~85% of the network (2025); the 2025–29 capital program adds USD 5.4bn of CBTC for ~75 route-miles on top of the USD 2.15bn (73 miles) in 2015–19, with only two qualified suppliers (Siemens, Thales — now Hitachi Rail). The EU estimates EUR 75–85bn to equip the whole TEN-T trackside with ERTMS (EUR 37–42bn core) plus EUR 10–15bn onboard; CEF grants have so far funded ~8,800 km trackside and ~8,200 vehicles. GoA4 conversions of legacy suburban networks are now being tendered (Copenhagen S-Bane "Future of S-Bane", Hitachi SuSy contract Sept 2026 incl. platform obstacle detection; Taoyuan GoA4 Signaling X Nov 2026; Torino Line 2 driverless EUR 480m).
9. **FRMCS/5G replacement of GSM-R is a 2027–2035 wave with a defined regulatory clock.** GSM-R covers >130,000 km in Europe (90,000 cab radios; ~210,000 km worldwide) with end of support expected ~2035; FRMCS V1 is in the 2023 CCS TSI, V3 ("first edition") is due in the 2027 CCS TSI after the 34-month MORANE-2 field programme that started Dec 2024; DB plans migration 2026–2035 with live tests from 2027; SNCF has contracted Frequentis for FRMCS by 2030; Switzerland has identified ~3,500 base-station sites. Onboard retrofit (TOBA) and ETCS Baseline 3 re-certification (≥EUR 100k per vehicle) are the recurring cost items.
10. **Predictive maintenance / digital-twin platforms have moved from pilots to fleet-wide default.** Hitachi Rail's HMAX is on ~2,500 trains (pipeline ~3,500) and will be embedded in all new Hitachi rolling stock from 2027; Siemens Railigent X runs RRX fleet maintenance at ~100% availability and Siemens bought MERMEC's diagnostics business (closed Nov 2026); Alstom HealthHub underpins long services contracts (e.g. Greater Anglia GBP 800m 15-year TSA, Oct 2026); KONUX monitors ~6,000 infrastructure assets with claimed 50% fewer delay minutes and 30% lower opex on switches. The OEMs are capturing this value through services contracts rather than selling software.
11. **Cybersecurity is becoming a procurement gate.** CLC/TS 50701 is being superseded by IEC 63452 (Siemens describes it as "upcoming" in Jan 2026; a BS EN IEC 63452 draft was issued Sept 2023); IEC 62443 certification of complete train architectures incl. ATC (Siemens/TÜV SÜD, Jan 2026), the EU Cyber Resilience Act, FedRAMP (Geotab) and ISO 27001/SOC 2 (Hayden AI) are now cited in vendor marketing.
12. **Generic fleet-telematics platforms are entering public fleets from the municipal side.** Geotab reports 3,000+ public-sector customers and >1m public-sector subscriptions in North America and is the single-source GSA Fleet provider; Samsara (USD 2.1bn ARR, +30% YoY, Sept 2026) markets to "thousands of public sector agencies", reports a top-5 US city expanding to transit and a "leading North American passenger transit provider" using its AI agents. They win on safety video and consolidated municipal fleets, not on transit-specific CAD/AVL — but they are on CAD/AVL bidder lists (Geotab, Vermont statewide procurement per sibling file).
13. **Generative/agentic AI is being productised quickly by the SaaS challengers** (Optibus Agent Sept 2025; Swiftly AI dispatcher comms and auto-generated incident/NTD reports; Masabi AI Labs Oct 2026; Samsara Agent Studio with 7x adoption since June 2026) while incumbents lead with AI in asset analytics. Agencies' own surveys report workforce and AI readiness as top struggles (Optibus/Cities Today, Feb 2025).
14. **Implication for a new entrant:** the open doors are (a) bus-side categories where a hardware+software+services bundle creates a measurable, often self-funding outcome (enforcement, safety video, charge management, real-time accuracy), (b) software overlays that sit on legacy CAD/AVL and displace modules incrementally, and (c) data/analytics layers on standardised interfaces (ITxPT, GTFS-RT, GTFS-Flex). Rail signalling, ATO and FRMCS core systems are effectively closed to new entrants (SIL-4 certification, 2–3 qualified suppliers per network, 10–15 year programmes), but adjacent niches (obstacle detection, asset condition monitoring, cybersecurity, onboard telecom migration) are contestable.

## A. AI video analytics on buses — automated bus-lane / bus-stop enforcement

**Adoption evidence (verified).**
- NYC MTA: ABLE launched 2019 on 123 buses (bus lanes only). State legislation (2023 budget) extended authority to bus stops and double parking; MTA board approved a USD 141m contract on 30 Apr 2024 to reach 1,000 buses by end-2024 and 2,000 by end-2027 (Streetsblog, 1 May 2024). ACE went live Aug 2024 on 623 buses/14 routes, 1,000 buses/34 routes by Nov 2024; MTA stated (Mar 2025) 34 routes/700k daily riders, 64 routes by end-2025, 93 routes and 3,000+ buses by end-2027 (Streetsblog, 25 Mar 2025). Nov 2025: 47 routes, 1,400+ buses, 545 miles, ~840k daily riders (amNY). Jan 2026: 54 routes, 1,400 buses, 560 miles, ~1m riders (Streetsblog). Jun 2026: 64 lines, 1,900+ buses, 810 miles, >1m riders (amNY). Fines USD 50 escalating by USD 50 to USD 250; violations issued at most every two hours; evidence reviewed by NYC DOT and Department of Finance.
- NYC outcomes (MTA data reported by Streetsblog/amNY): bus speeds +5% average, +10% in bus lanes, +20% on busways, up to +22–30% on specific segments (Hayden quotes "up to 36%"); collisions -20% (Bx19 -36%); bus stops blocked 40% less of the time; bus-stop crashes -9%; emissions -5–10%; only 9% of violators receive a second ticket; Bx36 bus-stop tickets fell 84.6% (4,905 in Sep 2024 → 754 in Nov 2025).
- NYC volumes: >400,000 tickets in the first five months (Aug–Dec 2024); Dec 2024: 76,673 bus-stop and 64,978 double-parking tickets (>USD 11m each); ABLE peak 90,627 bus-lane tickets in Sep 2024; 1,718,099 drivers ticketed over Jan 2023–Dec 2024 with USD 225,458,863 in fines; cameras issued 76.4% of bus-lane/stop violations in 2023–24 and 93% in Dec 2024 (independent analysis by Jehiah Czebotar reported by Streetsblog).
- LA Metro/LADOT: warnings from Dec 2024, fines from 17 Feb 2025 at USD 293; phase 1 on lines 720 and 212, phase 2 adds 50 cameras on lines 910/950 and 70 for 100 equipped vehicles (LA Metro press release, 14 Feb 2025). Vendor claims LA Metro citations rose from ~570/month (manual) to ~10,000/month. LADOT launched its own DASH-bus enforcement 2 Nov 2026 with penalties from 2 Jan 2027 (Sustainable Bus).
- Philadelphia (PPA/SEPTA): 152 buses; equipped routes 3–6% faster since Apr 2025 while unequipped routes slowed; 63% of ticketed owners did not reoffend; 211,000+ violations in year one; bus-stop violations at Market & 13th fell 96% (515→22); first US trolley-mounted enforcement (2026).
- AC Transit (Oakland): bus-only-lane citations fell from 1,241 (Q2 2025) to 631 (Q2 2026), bus-stop citations from 5,207 to 4,068; California AB 1837 (signed Oct 2026) preserves the enforcement authority.
- Santa Monica Big Blue Bus: 2023 pilot found 7.7 violations per bus per day (606 in 45 days); full enforcement from 1 Sep 2025; after one year bus-lane violations -61%, bus-stop violations -40% (segments -77% to -90%+); extended to 7 city parking-enforcement vehicles (first non-bus deployment).
- WMATA (vendor claim): -32% bus-stop violations in one year, 7,300 fewer violations/yr, +14% bus speeds.
- MBTA: statewide authorisation in 2025; warnings from 1 Oct 2026 across the bus network. Chicago CTA: 10th major US city, 30-day warning from 15 Oct (2025) covering bus lanes, bus stops and bike lanes under the city's Smart Streets initiative.
- International: Hayden AI obtained UK VCA certification (EADM063) for England and separately for Wales; a 7-month, 4-bus pilot with TMB Barcelona (EUR 100k Innova Lab award) logged >13,000 interference events with no tickets issued; a London project uses camera data to detect roadworks; a Belgian channel partnership with InTouch Traffic (back-office processing, GDPR).
- Installed base: Hayden AI passed 2,000 system installations (2025) and reports >2,100 buses globally (2026).

**Business model.** Camera + edge compute hardware per bus, cloud review portal, integration with city citation back-office (NYC DOF; InTouch in Belgium); city staff review each event before issuance. Agency/city economics are driven by fine revenue (NYC ~USD 11m/month per tranche in Dec 2024). We could not obtain the MTA contract's price structure (fixed per-bus vs per-violation) from a primary source; the USD 141m / ~1,900 additional buses implies an order of magnitude of ~USD 70k per bus over the contract term including services, but this is an inference and should not be used without the board document.

**Leaders.** Hayden AI (largest US provider; Rise Fund/TPG-backed, USD 90m Series C 2024 per sibling file); Safe Fleet offers an "Automated Bus Lane Enforcement" product line; Verra Mobility runs NYC DOT fixed-camera programmes (sibling file). Siemens (Yunex) and others operate fixed bus-lane cameras in the UK.

**Implications for a new entrant.** Proven, measurable, revenue-positive category; adoption is gated by state enabling legislation (NY 2019/2023, CA AB 1837, MA 2025, UK VCA approval) and by city back-office integration — a channel partner model (InTouch) lowers entry cost in Europe. Hayden's platform is extending to bike lanes, double parking, trolleys, potholes and roadworks detection, i.e. the camera becomes a general "mobile perception" sensor — the defensible asset is the city-validated detection accuracy and the review workflow, not the camera.

## B. Driver safety, collision avoidance and video telematics (Mobileye, Safe Fleet, Lytx, Samsara, Geotab, Motive)

- Samsara: USD 2.1bn ARR (+30% YoY), 242 new USD 100k+ and 20 new USD 1m+ ARR customers in Q2 FY27 (Sept 2026); markets a public-sector practice ("thousands of public sector agencies"; case results: 81% lower collision risk in six months, 71% less idling) and a passenger-transit vertical (Coach USA: -92% preventable incidents with AI Multicam and pedestrian collision warning); reports one of the five largest US cities expanding from one division to fire, parks, public works and transit; a "leading North American passenger transit provider" uses a Samsara AI agent to separate fuel waste from regeneration/maintenance idling; NFC rider card readers for ridership capture; Mexico City office serves passenger-transit customers.
- Geotab: 100,000+ customers, ~6m connected vehicles, 3,400+ employees; 3,000+ public-sector customers and >1m public-sector subscriptions in North America; single-source telematics provider to GSA Fleet; FedRAMP, FIPS 140-3; VisionTrack 360 cameras; "#1 telematics vendor" per ABI Research (vendor citation). Named on a statewide CAD/AVL bidder list (Vermont; sibling file).
- Lytx: dedicated transit page (video safety, GPS, maintenance) with Pierce Transit testimonial; no transit volume data disclosed.
- Safe Fleet: positions as "#1 provider of mobile video surveillance" for transit; BC Transit states video resolved 64% of liability cases without further investigation; portfolio spans surveillance, infotainment, live streaming, collision-avoidance/360° cameras, pedestrian safety, bus-lane enforcement, mirrors and hatches — i.e. a hardware-led bus safety bundle.
- Mobileye: no current Shield+ product page found (404); corporate news is OEM ADAS and robotaxi (incl. Ruter/Holo selecting MOIA with Mobileye Drive, Dec 2025). Transit collision-avoidance retrofit is now served mainly by telematics players' AI cameras.
- Optibus has entered driver safety with MagicEye (AI fatigue/distraction detection) and a UK partnership with Elite to meet TfL's Bus Safety Standard (Oct 2026).

**Implications.** Safety video is the wedge through which generic telematics vendors enter transit; their advantage is scale pricing, municipal cooperative contracts ("bypass lengthy RFPs" per Samsara) and consolidated city fleets. A transit-specialist entrant should expect Samsara/Geotab/Lytx as price-setters for camera+telematics hardware and should differentiate on transit workflows (CAD, dispatch, NTD, APC integration) rather than on sensors.

- Europe (DVV Media/Chatrou dataset via Sustainable Bus): BEB registrations >8t: 4,152 (2022), 6,354 (2023), 7,843 (2024), 11,819 (2025, originally reported 11,607, +48%); H1 2026 8,141 (+49% vs 5,477). Fuel-cell: 558 (2025), 397 (H1 2026). Leaders 2025: Yutong 1,801 (15.5%), MAN 1,409, Mercedes 1,395, BYD 1,305, Iveco 1,091, Wrightbus 921, Solaris 882. - US (CALSTART "Zeroing in on ZEBs", March 2026 edition): as of July 2025 >8,100 full-size and ~1,400 small transit ZEBs funded/ordered/delivered/deployed; full-size +16% YoY; fuel-cell +49%; >1,000 ZEBs added in the year despite procurement delays, supply-chain constraints and policy uncertainty; growth spreading to Southeast and Midwest. (Share of new orders is not published on the page; the report PDF was not reachable.)
- Example fleet conversion: Frankfurt 109 ZE of 418 buses (26%), full conversion targeted 2033.
- Charge/energy management vendors and evidence: The Mobility House ChargePilot runs "Europe's largest electric bus fleet" depot at 360+ MWh/month with schedule- and tariff-aware load management, plus ACTV Venice; ChargePoint reports 406k+ activated ports and 8k+ connected fleet vehicles with integrated ICE+EV fleet software; VEV says it supports >25% of the UK electric bus fleet (Stagecoach, Nottingham City Transport) as of Nov 2026; Tenix (Norway) signed LETEC (BE), Boreal (NO) and GöVB (DE) in Sept 2026; Hitachi ZeroCarbon (First Bus customer; MUFG financing partnership) offers fleet, charge, battery-management and strategy services; Optibus sells EV scheduling with charging-time/location/battery constraints plus charging-station management and an "EV Energy Management System".

**Implications.** Electrification creates a new, mandatory software layer (schedule-aware charging, range-constrained vehicle scheduling, battery health) that neither legacy CAD/AVL nor charger OEMs own. Winners so far are specialists with depot-integration services (Mobility House, VEV) and schedulers extending downward (Optibus). For a new entrant, the attractive position is the integration layer between scheduling, depot energy and the grid (OCPP/OCPI, ITxPT S01 EV/infrastructure profiles), ideally sold as a service tied to energy savings.

## D. Cloud/SaaS migration of CAD/AVL and software-led stacks (ITxPT, BYO hardware, Swiftly/Optibus)

- Swiftly: founded 2016; 200+ agencies in 10 countries (Sept 2026; 190+ in 12 countries in May 2025), >half of the 25 largest US agencies by bus ridership, 11,000+ agency users, 2.4bn passenger journeys/yr; co-exists with legacy CAD/AVL at 100+ agencies; launched AI-powered CAD/AVL on 22 Sept 2026 (AI-transcribed and urgency-ranked operator calls, auto-completed incident/NTD reports, operator app, "Swiftly Bridge" onboard computer controlling APCs, AVAS, headsigns and fareboxes; "requires less hardware", full-fleet visibility during cutover, 60+ OTA releases/week, 99.9%+ uptime); claimed outcomes: DCTA OTP +18.6% and early departures -65%, MDOT MTA buses 25% more likely on time, SEPTA ETA accuracy +26% (31m additional trips with trustworthy info), Valley Metro OTP +10%, Oulu OTP >85%; acquisitions Hopthru (2024) and TextMarks (May 2026); Cove Hill-led investment (May 2025); new CEO Oct 2026. Survey cited: only 6% of 75+ agencies would strongly recommend their CAD/AVL.
- Optibus: Real-Time Suite (29 Sept 2026) — cloud layer on existing CAD/AVL/GPS for ETA prediction, fleet monitoring, detour detection, passenger information and reporting, marketed as "Real-Time Visibility, No New Hardware"; Carahsoft public-sector channel partnership (Sept 2026); digital driver bidding and vacation bidding (MetroLink Tulsa adoption); intelligent driver/vehicle allocation; wins/renewals: ATM Milan (Nov 2025), First Bus renewal + Performance Suite (Oct 2026), Niagara Transit, Durham NC.
- ITxPT: open architecture for onboard/over-the-air/back-office interoperability; board balanced between PTAs/operators (NTA Ireland, Nobina, Ruter — chair, Västtrafik, LTA Singapore, TL Lausanne, UITP) and industry (INIT, Pilotfish, Luminator, Volvo, Daimler, Iveco); MQTT-based S04 specification series went live 1 Apr 2026; 2026 releases cover ticketing validation counting (Jun), logon (Jul) and passenger counting/occupancy (Sep); roadmap adds heavy-rail profile, signal priority, integrated ticketing and DRT. Member list shows roughly 28 strategic, 33 principal and 110+ associated members (count from the public members page).
- Incumbent consolidation: Hitachi Rail agreed to acquire Clever Devices (Apr 2026; closed Jul 2026 per sibling file) to become a "global digital mobility player"; INIT acquired 67.74% of Applied Autonomy (xFlow autonomous-fleet supervision) on 1 Oct 2026; Siemens Mobility closed the MERMEC diagnostics/signalling acquisition on 4 Nov 2026.

**Implications.** The replacement cycle for 1990s-architecture CAD/AVL is being contested by cloud platforms that enter via real-time/analytics and then absorb dispatch, onboard and NTD reporting. The entry recipe that works: (1) coexist with the incumbent via data feeds; (2) prove OTP/ETA gains; (3) replace modules at renewal with a thin onboard device. ITxPT and MQTT reduce the integration moat of incumbent onboard suites. A new entrant without an installed base should expect to buy or partner for a data platform, since Swiftly, Optibus and Via already own agency relationships at scale.

GTFS-RT entity mix: trip updates 724, vehicle positions 666, service alerts 474 (plus combined feeds).
- Governance: GTFS stewardship transferred from Google to MobilityData (announced 2025/26; "Canadian owned and globally used"); MobilityData is also developing GOFS for on-demand data; Optibus added GTFS-Flex-native DRT planning (2026). Wikipedia notes Transitland indexes feeds in 55+ countries.

**Implications.** Feed availability is no longer a differentiator in NA/EU; accuracy and completeness of predictions are (Swiftly's "ETA Completeness Benchmark"). For an entrant, open data lowers the cost of building passenger-information and analytics products without owning the CAD/AVL, but it also means passenger-facing apps (Transit, Google, Citymapper/Via) commoditise the rider interface.

**Implications.** Fare collection is outside the core ITS scope, but the bundling of payments with real-time information and journey planning in a single agency app pulls passenger-information revenue toward fare-platform vendors; conversely, ITxPT's validator/APC data specs mean an ITS entrant can integrate rather than build.

## H. Automated train operation (GoA2–4) and CBTC resignalling of legacy metros

- Installed base: UITP counted >1,000 km of fully automated (GoA4) metro in 42 cities as of March 2018, with nearly a quarter of metro systems having at least one automated line and an expected tripling of automated km by 2022 driven by China; IRJ reported UITP's forecast of 2,200 km of automated metro by 2025 (2016). Paris Métro reports ~30% lower operating cost on GoA4 lines (Wikipedia citing RATP). (A post-2020 UITP total could not be retrieved — gap.)
- Legacy-metro resignalling (NYC, from MTA capital-program documents compiled on Wikipedia): fixed-block signalling still on ~85% of the subway in 2025; signal problems were 13% of delays (2016); first two CBTC lines (L, 7: 50 track-miles) took 2000–2018; 2015–19 program funded 73.2 route-miles of CBTC for USD 2.152bn (within a USD 2.766bn signalling line) plus USD 337m of substations; Queens Blvd phase 1 contracts USD 205.8m to Siemens and Thales (2015) plus USD 223.3m installation (2016); 8th Avenue USD 245.8m (2020, first axle-counter CBTC); Crosstown est. USD 556.4m (2022, "CBTC-centric" integrated zone controllers); 2025–29 program adds Broadway, Rockaway, Nassau and Astoria lines (~75 miles) for an estimated USD 5.4bn; Byford's 2018 plan priced system-wide CBTC at USD 8–15bn over 10–15 years. Only two suppliers (Siemens, Thales/Hitachi) are MTA-qualified; MTA has piloted UWB positioning and issued RFIs to simplify wayside equipment to cut cost.
- Other programmes: Toronto Line 1 ATC complete, Line 5 GoA2 (GoA4 in the depot); London sub-surface lines ATO by 2022, Thameslink ETCS L2 + ATO; Copenhagen S-Bane conversion to unattended operation with Hitachi's "SuSy" support-systems contract (Sept 2026: SCADA, PIS, CCTV, maintenance management, HMAX and platform obstacle detection ODS-P across 87 stations); Siemens Signaling X with GoA4 for Taoyuan Brown Line (~11 km) and Green Line extension (Nov 2026, commissioning mid-2030s); Hitachi EUR 480m Torino Line 2 driverless trains + signalling (Mar 2026) and Chennai Phase II driverless opening (Oct 2026); Hamburg demonstrated driverless (GoA4 shunting) S-Bahn operation on shared track (Oct 2021); DSD's "AutomatedTrain" project targets driverless stabling. Czech Railways run GoA2 on ~1,500 km with ~400 equipped vehicles.

**Implications.** Resignalling spend is large, multi-decade and growing (NYC alone ~USD 1bn+/yr through 2029), but supplier qualification, SIL-4 safety cases and interface ownership make it a two- to three-player market per network. Contestable adjacencies: platform/track obstacle detection, positioning (UWB/GNSS), wayside simplification, ATS/iOCC software and data, and the "supporting systems" packages (PIS, CCTV, SCADA, maintenance management) that automation programmes bundle.

## I. ETCS / ERTMS mandates and deployment

- Cost envelope (European Commission, Third ERTMS Work Plan): EUR 75–85bn for trackside on the whole TEN-T (EUR 37–42bn core network) plus EUR 10–15bn onboard (EUR 5–10bn core). EU co-funding: EUR 1.2bn (2007–13), >EUR 3.1bn (2014–20: CEF EUR 1.25bn + ESIF EUR 1.9bn). CEF 2014–20 delivered 38 trackside actions = 5,200 km double-track equivalent (EUR 480m) + EUR 242m for interlockings and 35 onboard actions ≈ 3,000 vehicles (EUR 306m). CEF2 (2021–27) so far: 27 trackside projects ≈ 3,600 km ETCS, 1,800 km interlocking, 400 km radio (EUR 638m) and 52 onboard projects ≈ 5,200 vehicles (EUR 414m); Cohesion Fund EUR 518m, ERDF EUR 37m.
- Regulation: CCS TSI Regulation (EU) 2023/1695 as amended by (EU) 2026/693 (19 Mar 2026); mandatory specifications ETCS Baseline 4 Release 1, GSM-R B1 MR1 + FRMCS B0, ATO B1 R1; ETCS L3 merged into L2 (2023). The Commission lists deployment challenges: shifting national strategies, financial/human resource shortages, infrastructure delays, preference for non-obsolete national systems, fleet-equipment lag, cross-border coordination, procurement (few bids, over-budget offers) and technical maturity (Baseline 3 experience, product availability).
- National programmes: Denmark — full-network ETCS L2 replacement, EUR 3.3bn, completion re-planned to 2030; Belgium — Infrabel's 2015 framework for ETCS L2 on >2,200 km (Siemens/Cofely-Fabricom), 1,225 km ETCS-usable by end-2015, convergence to L2 by 2030–35; Austria — 616 km equipped (461 km L2) in Apr 2024, 3,300 km by 2038 (Hitachi Rail named partner for "Austria's largest signalling digitalisation project", Sept 2026); Germany — Digitale Schiene Deutschland (DSD) targets digital interlockings + ETCS across the network (80% of network without lineside signals), Rhine corridor 1,450 km L2, seven "SLP" starter projects and multiple border crossings, a new federal ERTMS coordination office (Apr 2026); Norway — Gjøvik Line North first with newest ERTMS (Siemens/Bane NOR); Switzerland — entire state network on ETCS (L1 LS), L2 on 10 lines; Poland — L2 on 346 km Warsaw–Gdańsk/Gdynia. Onboard: Baseline 3 re-certification costs "at least EUR 100k per vehicle".

**Implications.** ERTMS is a mandated, grant-supported, decade-long spend with a locked supplier set (UNISIG: Alstom, Siemens, Hitachi, CAF, AŽD, MERMEC, Progress Rail, etc.). New-entrant opportunities are in onboard retrofit integration, key management/cyber (DSD "secure ETCS key management", Oct 2026), test/validation labs and diagnostics — not in core ETCS products.

## J. FRMCS / 5G replacing GSM-R

- Installed base and clock (UIC): GSM-R on >130,000 km in Europe with 90,000 activated cab radios and ~210,000 km worldwide; end of GSM-R support expected around 2035; FRMCS V1 in the 2023 CCS TSI (GSM-R and FRMCS jointly "RMR"), will operate alongside GSM-R "until sometime in 2035"; V3 ("FRMCS 1st Edition", first implementable version) is being produced for inclusion in a new CCS TSI in 2027 following the 34-month MORANE-2 field programme (started Dec 2024); FRMCS spectrum secured (ECC(20)02: 874.4–880/919.4–925 MHz paired, 1900–1910 MHz); 5GRAIL prototypes demonstrated Sept 2023 (DB advanced TrainLab).
- National plans (Wikipedia, citing operators): DB transition 2026–2035 with live tests from 2027; SNCF contracted Frequentis for FRMCS with network transformation by 2030; Switzerland identified ~3,500 base-station sites; Finland piloting; recent train tenders require FRMCS-readiness from 2024. ERA requires FRMCS pilot reporting under CCS TSI Art. 10.

**Implications.** A time-boxed replacement wave of trackside radio (base stations, core), onboard radios (TOBA) and ETCS data bearers across >130,000 km — but suppliers are telecom majors (Nokia, Ericsson, Kontron, Frequentis, Siemens, Hitachi) and railway system integrators. Entry points for smaller firms: onboard gateway/antenna integration, test specifications and labs, and migration consultancy.

## K. Digital twins and predictive maintenance (KONUX, Siemens Railigent X, Alstom HealthHub, Hitachi HMAX)

- Hitachi Rail HMAX (launched InnoTrans 2024 with NVIDIA edge AI): installed on ~2,500 trains (Sept 2026; pipeline ~3,500); "200,000 systems"; all new Hitachi rolling stock from 2027 to carry HMAX sensors; HMAX for Infrastructure uses in-service trains (camera, lidar, vibration; Omnicom/Balfour Beatty acquisition 2025) — catenary monitoring from 10-day cycles to real time; HMAX for Signalling; iOCC integration; SMRT 14% traction-energy savings (Sept 2026); HMAX included in the Copenhagen S-Bane SuSy contract.
- Siemens Mobility Railigent X: Foundation (train/signalling data services via API), Insights (health states, remaining useful life, ETCS/point-machine failure prediction) and Workflow (fleet manager) tiers; RRX fleet at the Dortmund Rail Service Center runs at "almost 100%" availability; IT care and cybersecurity managed services; "Connected Trains" standard-data initiative with DB and Knorr-Bremse; MERMEC diagnostics-train business acquired (closed 4 Nov 2026).
- Alstom HealthHub: web platform analysing train, signalling and infrastructure data (e.g. >200 parameters every 30 s per train); deployed on Dubai tram since Jan 2021 under a 13-year maintenance contract; HealthHub appears in Stockholm metro, Norrtåg, Panama Line 1, GTR, GWR, Saudi freight services contracts (Railway-News search); scale of OEM services: Greater Anglia 15-year TSA extension for 133 Class 720 trains worth ~GBP 800m (EUR 947m), Oct 2026.

**Implications.** Predictive maintenance value is being captured inside OEM long-term service agreements (Alstom/Hitachi/Siemens) and by a handful of IIoT specialists on infrastructure (KONUX). A new entrant needs either an asset-owner channel (infrastructure managers) or a niche asset class; "platform" plays without sensors or service contracts have struggled.

## Implications for a potential new entrant (synthesis)

1. **Where new money is created rather than reallocated:** bus-camera enforcement (fine revenue), charge/energy management (energy savings, grid-connection avoidance), video safety (claims/insurance), and real-time accuracy (ridership). These are the categories where a newcomer can sell on outcomes and sometimes on revenue share or savings share — unlike CAD/AVL replacement, which competes for a fixed capital budget.
2. **Architecture shift favours overlays:** cloud layers that ingest legacy CAD/AVL/GPS (Swiftly, Optibus) win early footprint without a rip-and-replace; ITxPT MQTT specs and GTFS-RT reduce integration lock-in. A new entrant should design for coexistence and thin onboard hardware (tablet/gateway), not a proprietary onboard suite.
3. **Regulatory enablers determine timing:** state enforcement laws (NY, CA, MA, UK VCA), ZE mandates and funding, CCS TSI 2027 (FRMCS V3), IEC 63452/CRA cybersecurity, and FRMCS/GSM-R 2035 obsolescence create datable demand windows. Rail windows are large but supplier-locked; bus windows are smaller per contract but open.
4. **Expect platform convergence from three directions:** rail OEMs buying bus ITS (Hitachi–Clever Devices), telematics majors entering public fleets (Samsara, Geotab), and scheduling/real-time SaaS absorbing CAD/AVL and safety (Optibus MagicEye, Swiftly CAD/AVL). An entrant's defensibility must come from (a) validated outcome data, (b) a channel into city/agency procurement (cooperative contracts, Carahsoft-type resellers, local enforcement back-office partners), and (c) certification assets (IEC 62443/63452, FedRAMP/StateRAMP, VCA).
5. **Rail-specific niches that remain contestable:** platform/track obstacle detection, wayside simplification and positioning for CBTC, onboard FRMCS/ETCS retrofit integration, infrastructure condition monitoring from in-service trains, cybersecurity lifecycle services, and the "supporting systems" bundles in automation programmes.
