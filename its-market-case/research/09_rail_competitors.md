## 1. Key takeaways

1. **Railway ITS is an oligopoly of three signalling houses plus a US-centric fourth.** Siemens Mobility (FY2025 revenue €12.4bn, 8.8% margin; Rail Infrastructure ≈33% of revenue ≈ €4.1bn derived, "#1 global market leader in rail infrastructure"), Alstom (Signalling sales €2.7bn FY2025/26, backlog €9.8bn, book-to-bill 1.3x) and Hitachi Rail (turnover >€7bn, ~60% from signalling & systems after the €1,660m Thales GTS acquisition closed 31 May 2024) account for ~84% of the CBTC deployments listed in the public CBTC deployment table (Alstom 36%, Hitachi 29%, Siemens 19% by project count; by route-km Siemens 32% / Alstom 31% / Hitachi 29%). Wabtec is the fourth pole in North America (Digital Intelligence $1.03bn in 2025, PTC on the majority of its ~24,600 locomotives in service) but is freight-weighted.
2. **The rail-control opportunity is mid-single-digit growth, not hyper-growth.** UNIFE WRMS 2024 (as reproduced in Alstom's URD) puts the *accessible* world rail-control market at €15.7bn/yr (2021-23 avg) rising to €19bn/yr (2027-29), i.e. ~3.2% p.a.; services are the largest accessible segment (€34.2bn → €40.5bn). UNIFE WRMS 2026 (Sept 2026, Bain) sizes the total world rail supply market at €221bn (2023-25) growing 3.2% p.a. to €266.8bn by 2029-31; SCI Verkehr (Sept 2026) says >€275bn by 2030 at 3.9% p.a. with Europe (€77.7bn) now the largest region. SCI's CCS 2026 study flags Europe as the largest CCS market ahead of China, driven by resignalling of legacy networks.
3. **Backlogs are long and sticky: this is the main barrier to entry.** Alstom backlog €104.4bn (Signalling €9.8bn = 3.6 years of signalling sales); Siemens Mobility backlog €52bn incl. €15bn Customer Services; Wabtec multi-year backlog $27.4bn; Knorr-Bremse RVS order book €5.9bn; CAF order book €16.2bn (3.6x sales). Framework contracts now run 30 years (ÖBB–Hitachi €1.3bn for ~300 digital interlockings; Ontario Line C$9bn RSSOM incl. 30-year O&M).
4. **Consolidation accelerated in 2024-2026 and is being led by strategics, not sponsors.** Hitachi–Thales GTS (€1,660m, May 2024; remedy sale of Hitachi mainline signalling in FR/DE/UK to MERMEC); Wabtec–Frauscher (~$792m, Dec 2025), Wabtec–Evident Inspection Technologies ($1.788bn, Jul 2025), Wabtec–Dellner (~€890m, Feb 2026); Siemens Mobility–MERMEC key businesses (~€430m FY25 revenue, announced May 2026, completed per Siemens press site); Alstom's North American conventional signalling sold to Knorr-Bremse (FY2024/25; KB Signaling now sells ElectroLogIXS / AFTC5 into MBTA, LA, Chicago, UK); Hitachi Rail–Clever Devices (~$220m, Jul 2026, bus CAD/AVL — not re-verified). Stadler and CAF are diversifying into signalling (Stadler won a "very large" CBTC order in Atlanta in 2024).
5. **Digital/asset-intelligence is where the OEMs are investing and where independents still exist.** Siemens Railigent X / Signaling X / Mobility Software Suite X; Alstom HealthHub; Hitachi HMAX (on ~2,500 trains, pipeline ~3,500, to be embedded in all new rolling stock from 2027; NVIDIA IGX Thor edge AI); Wabtec KinetiX / Digital Intelligence (+31% in 2025 incl. M&A). Independent specialists remain in trackside detection (Konux, Sensonic), onboard connectivity / PIS / APC (Icomera, Nomad – Alstom, Televic, Dilax, Infodev, Teleste €55.9m mobility segment) and perception AI (Rail Vision, revenue only $1.5m). Telecom layer is Nokia (GSM-R → FRMCS/5G) and Huawei (Intelligent Rail 2.0, InnoTrans 2026).
6. **Watch-item for a new entrant / sponsor:** Alstom's H1 2026/27 profit warning (aEBIT ~(4)%, FY26/27 margin guidance cut to ~1%, FCF €(1.5)bn, "review of all possible organic and inorganic options" to cut debt by ~€2bn by Sept 2027) is the first potential source of a scaled signalling / digital carve-out from a big-3 house in a decade; Cubic's distress (not re-verified) is a cautionary tale on leverage in hardware-heavy integrators.
7. **Services share is 20-45% of OEM rail revenue and rising**: Alstom Services 24% of sales (€4.7bn) and 38% of backlog; Siemens Mobility Customer Services 17% of revenue and 29% of backlog; Wabtec Freight Services + Transit Aftermarket = 43% of sales; Hitachi ~60% signalling & systems (incl. services). Services/aftermarket is where margins are (Knorr RVS 17.1% operating EBIT margin vs OEM signalling houses at 6-9%).

## 4. Market-share and structure evidence (for the competitive-dynamics slide)

**CBTC (urban).** Parsing the public CBTC deployment table (130 projects, Wikipedia, read 2026-11-07): Alstom/Bombardier 47 projects (36%), Hitachi/Thales/Ansaldo 38 (29%), Siemens/Invensys 25 (19%) — "big 3" = 84%; by route-km Siemens 32% / Alstom 31% / Hitachi 29%. Challengers: Nippon Signal (5, Japan/Asia), Stadler (2, incl. Atlanta), Mitsubishi, Korean suppliers, MERMEC (1). Regional pattern: Siemens leads Europe (10/25), Alstom leads APAC (13/28) and North America (8/17, with Hitachi 7), Alstom/Hitachi split MEA-LatAm. Company claims exceed the table (Hitachi: SelTrac on 150+ lines/70 cities), so use shares, not absolute counts. Note the table excludes most Chinese domestic lines (CASCO, TCT, CRSC) — China is a closed market (UNIFE accessibility 56%).

**ETCS / mainline CCS.** SCI Verkehr (2019 study): Siemens >35% of the European CCS market, ahead of Thales (now Hitachi), Alstom, Hitachi and Bombardier (now Alstom); CRSC 23% of the global market but <5% international. Post-2024 the European mainline field is Siemens, Alstom, Hitachi (ex-Thales), MERMEC (ex-Hitachi FR/DE/UK units, now partly Siemens), CAF Signalling, Stadler Signalling; Knorr-Bremse (ElectroLogIXS) in UK/US conventional. Evidence of two-supplier framework structures: ÖBB (Hitachi "one of two selected technology partners", €1.3bn/30 yrs); RFI ERTMS frameworks (Hitachi €867m / 1,885 km; €500m / 700 km → ~€0.46-0.71m per km trackside benchmark); DB long-term digitalisation framework with Alstom (FY24/25 PR).

**Signalling backlog sizes.** Alstom €9.76bn (Mar-26). Siemens Mobility total €52bn (rail infra not split). Hitachi n/d. Wabtec $27.4bn total (freight-heavy). Knorr RVS €5.9bn. CAF €16.2bn total.

**Services share.** Alstom Services 24% of sales / 38% of backlog; Siemens Customer Services 17% of revenue / 29% of backlog; Wabtec Freight Services + Transit Aftermarket 43% of sales; Hitachi ~60% signalling & systems (incl. maintenance). Frameworks increasingly bundle 30-year service (ÖBB, Ontario Line).

**Order-intake concentration.** The nine main OEMs booked a record €77.7bn in CY2025 (Alstom URD), vs a UNIFE total market of ~€221bn/yr → OEM-captured share ≈35% of total supply spend (rest is infrastructure civils, in-house, local suppliers).

**Market size anchors for rail-control (signalling + train control) segment.** UNIFE WRMS 2024 accessible rail control €15.7bn/yr (2021-23) → €19bn/yr (2027-29), ~3.2% CAGR; total (incl. non-accessible China etc.) would be higher — UNIFE total market 2023-25 €221bn vs accessible ~€125-139bn implies a ~1.6-1.8x gross-up factor, i.e. total rail control plausibly €25-30bn/yr (derived, low confidence).
