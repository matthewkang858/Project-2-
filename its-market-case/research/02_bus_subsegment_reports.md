# Bus / Roadway Transit ITS: Sub-segment Market Sizing and Penetration

**Research topic:** Bus/roadway public transit ITS sub-segments (CAD/AVL and fleet management, scheduling/planning software, APC, passenger information, onboard video/AI safety, transit signal priority, connected/smart bus, ZEB telematics and charge management, paratransit/demand-responsive software; electronic fare collection flagged as ADJACENT).
**Prepared:** 2026-11-07. Base year targeted: 2025; forecast horizon: 2031.
**Method note:** All figures below come from web-search result snippets of the named sources. Direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl) were blocked by the session network policy for every host tried (media.berginsight.com, iotbusinessnews.com, rosap.ntl.bts.gov, initse.com, gminsights.com), and the shared WebSearch budget was exhausted after ~40 queries, so several planned follow-ups (unit economics per bus, ITS-DTS onboard-camera and fare-media percentages, Conduent/Cubic/IVU financials, ACEA EU bus parc) are listed in Gaps rather than guessed. Nothing below is fabricated; where a number is derived from two sourced figures it is labelled "derived".

---

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The only credible, transit-specific top-down anchor for the bus/tram roadway segment is Berg Insight.** Its 10th edition (March 2026) sizes public transport ITS for buses and trams at **EUR 2.55B (Europe) + EUR 1.22B (North America) = ~EUR 3.8B in 2025**, growing at **5.2% CAGR to ~EUR 4.9B in 2030** (Europe 5.3%, NA 5.0%). This is a mature, high-penetration market: on-board computers with GPS/wireless are already on **90.3% of European and 91.9% of North American buses/trams (2025)**, rising only to ~94% by 2030. Growth therefore comes from replacement cycles, software/SaaS upsell, electrification and passenger-information demand, not from first-time adoption.
2. **Berg has revised its forecasts down between editions.** The 9th edition (March 2024) forecast EUR 4.26B by 2027 (Europe 6.3% CAGR, NA 8.8% CAGR 2022-27); the 10th edition's base of EUR 3.8B for 2025 implies ~7.6% realised CAGR 2022-25 (derived), but forward growth is now cut to ~5%. The deceleration (especially NA, 8.8% to 5.0%) is consistent with the FTA bus-grant pull-back (FY2026 Low-No/Bus NOFO cut to $610M from ~$2.0B in FY2025) and should be reflected in the model's downside case.
3. **US agency-level adoption is high for core functions, patchy for analytics layers.** USDOT's 2023 ITS Deployment Tracking Survey (464 agencies, 63% response, now including small-urban and rural) shows AVL on 85% of fixed-route bus agencies (81% all modes), CAD 70%, MDT 62%, but APC only 54% of fixed-route bus agencies (37% all modes), maintenance-management 32% and transit signal priority 19% (13% all modes). Real-time information dissemination is reported at 81% with GTFS-Realtime at 26% (fragmentary snippet; verify). Whitespace is concentrated in APC, TSP, analytics and small/rural agencies.
4. **Broad market-research-vendor numbers for "passenger information", "APC + PIS" and "APTS" are 2-10x larger than Berg and are not usable as a direct segment size without scope haircuts.** E.g., MarketsandMarkets PIS = $35.66B (2025) at 11.6% CAGR to $68.57B (2031); Mordor PIS = $33.39B (2025) at 9.97% to $59.06B (2031); MarketsandMarkets APC+PIS = $9.70B (2025) to $14.80B (2030) at 8.8%; GMI APTS = $6.8B (2025) to $15.5B (2035) with AVL $1.81B and CAD $1.20B sub-segments. They include rail/airports/airlines, global APAC build-out and station infrastructure. Useful for triangulating growth rates and component mix (Mordor: hardware 46.55% of PIS; software fastest at 14.45% CAGR; on-board fastest at 15.48% CAGR; roadway/bus 50.95% of PIS), not absolute size.
5. **Vendor financials corroborate a ~EUR 4B EU+NA bus market and show strong recent growth at the leaders.** INIT SE revenue grew from EUR 210.8M (2023) to EUR 265.7M (2024, +26%) to EUR 329.7M (2025, +24.1%), with 2026 guidance EUR 380-410M; North America is INIT's largest region (EUR 97.8M in 2024). Clever Devices (acquired by Hitachi Rail, closed 1 July 2026, price undisclosed) projects >$220M 2026 revenue and serves 8 of the 10 largest North American agencies. Via Transportation (IPO Sept 2025) grew revenue from $248.9M (2023) to $337.6M (2024) to $434M (2025) and frames a $250B TAM for "public transit solution providers" in NA+Europe (a scope that includes operations/contracted service, far beyond ITS).
6. **Demand-responsive / paratransit software is the fastest-growing adjacent software pocket** (microtransit software ~$2B in 2025 at ~15% CAGR per syndicated vendors; GMI's broad DRT systems market $28.4B 2025 at 15.3% CAGR includes service delivery). Via holds ~25% of global on-demand launches; US demand-response ridership rose 14% in 2024 to 373M trips (APTA).
7. **Electrification is the main new-spend driver.** 8,116 full-size ZEBs funded/ordered/delivered/deployed in the US as of July 2025 (+16% YoY; CALSTART forecasts >15,000 by 2030); ~25% of surveyed European urban buses were zero-emission in 2023 with 52% expected by 2027 and 70% by 2030 (UITP); every third new EU urban bus registered in 2024 was battery-electric. Berg sized e-bus ITS (charging/energy management, range telematics) at EUR 355M by 2027 for EU+NA. This drives new telematics, depot/charge management and higher-content onboard computers.
8. **Transit signal priority is widely piloted but thinly deployed:** NACTO's 2025 inventory finds 83% of 72 surveyed US/Canadian DOTs and agencies use TSP somewhere, but USDOT's agency-wide survey puts TSP at only 19% of fixed-route bus agencies; TCRP Synthesis 149 (2020) found 61% of 46 larger agencies with active TSP. No credible TSP-specific market-size exists; GMI attributes $735.5M of the $8.2B intelligent traffic signal market (2025) to public transit agencies as a proxy.
9. **Electronic fare collection (adjacent) is a comparable-sized or larger pool than core ITS:** TechSci $10.36B (2025) to $19.68B (2031) at 11.29%; Fortune Business Insights $16.68B (2025) at 15.56% with Cubic ~18% share. Keep out of the core bus-ITS sizing but note it as the main cross-sell pool for CAD/AVL incumbents (INIT, Conduent, Cubic, Vix, Flowbird, Ticketer all span both).
10. **Competitive structure:** two pan-regional incumbents (Trapeze/Modaxo and INIT), NA enterprise leaders (Clever Devices/Hitachi, Conduent, Cubic, Avail), European nationals (IVU in DACH; Vix, Flowbird, Ticketer in UK; EQUANS, RATP Smart Systems in France; Siemens Mobility software), and a cloud/SaaS challenger wave (Swiftly: >200 agencies, now selling CAD/AVL; Optibus: ~$60-75M ARR estimates, $1.3B 2022 valuation; Via/Spare in on-demand). Swiftly's cited survey (75+ agencies, only 6% would "strongly recommend" their CAD/AVL vendor) is vendor-sourced but signals incumbent dissatisfaction, the main entry wedge.

## 3. Sub-segment synthesis (what the numbers imply for the model)

### 3.1 CAD/AVL and fleet management (core)
- **Size anchor:** Berg EU+NA bus/tram ITS EUR 3.8B (2025) is the closest all-in figure (onboard computers, CAD/AVL, PIS, APC, video, related software/services). GMI's global AVL ($1.81B) + CAD ($1.20B) = ~$3.0B in 2025 is the best vendor-side proxy for the CAD/AVL slice globally; GMI puts AVL CAGR at 10.06% (2025-35), which is roughly double Berg's blended 5.2%.
- **Penetration:** ~90-92% of EU/NA buses and trams already carry an onboard computer (Berg); 85% of US fixed-route bus agencies run AVL and 70% run CAD (USDOT 2023). Remaining whitespace is small/rural agencies and the replacement cycle (typically tied to 12-year bus life and 7-10 year electronics refresh).
- **Vendors:** Trapeze/Vontas (Modaxo), INIT, Clever Devices (Hitachi Rail from Jul 2026), Conduent, Cubic, Avail, Siemens Mobility, IVU, EQUANS, Vix, Flowbird, Ticketer, RATP Smart Systems; cloud challengers Swiftly (CAD/AVL launched), Optibus, Connexionz, ETA Transit, Geotab.

### 3.2 Scheduling, planning and operations software
- Market-research vendor figures are inconsistent (bus routing/scheduling $0.94B 2025 per Wiseguy; "public transportation software" $0.85B per Maximize vs $8.7B per MarkWide). Treat as low confidence; better triangulation is vendor revenue: Optibus ~$60-75M ARR, GIRO/HASTUS and Trapeze (private; no disclosed revenue), INIT (EUR 330M group, mostly integrated systems).
- The structural trend is cloud SaaS displacing on-premise scheduling (Optibus, Swiftly) with land-and-expand into CAD/AVL.

### 3.3 Automatic passenger counting (APC)
- Adoption: 54% of US fixed-route bus agencies (2023), up from a large-metro-only 69-77% range reported in 2020 (sample change). APC is the biggest single adoption gap among core onboard functions; NTD now accepts APC-derived ridership, which supports further uptake.
- Size: no clean APC-only transit number; MarketsandMarkets APC+PIS $9.70B (2025, 8.8% CAGR) with APC the fastest-growing offering and buses a high-CAGR application. Europe = $3.15B of that. Key vendors: DILAX, Iris, Hella Aglaia (people-sensing), Eurotech, INIT, Clever Devices, UrbanThings.

### 3.4 Passenger information (onboard and at-stop)
- Global PIS sized $33-36B (2025) by Mordor and MarketsandMarkets at ~10-11.6% CAGR to 2031; roadway/bus = 50.95% of PIS (Mordor); hardware 46.55%; on-board fastest-growing at 15.48% CAGR; station/wayside 55.78% of value.
- Applying Mordor's shares mechanically (derived, illustrative only): bus PIS ~$17B global in 2025, which is far above Berg's entire bus ITS market, so these vendor figures include station infrastructure, airports and APAC projects and should be discounted heavily for an EU+NA bus scope.
- US adoption: 81% of agencies disseminate real-time information (fragment); GTFS-RT 26% (fragment, verify).

### 3.5 Onboard video surveillance and AI safety analytics
- No credible public market size. Omdia's mobile video surveillance report (transit buses, trains, school buses) is the paid reference; Safe Fleet (>1M systems, claims #1 Americas), Luminator/Apollo (claims past Americas leadership), March Networks, Safety Vision, Seon (Safe Fleet) are the players. AI tiers (driver monitoring, incident detection) are being layered on (Luminator RoadRunner Pro, March Networks AI5). Berg's Video Telematics 7th ed. (Apr 2026) says video telematics will double in five years, NA leading (https://media.berginsight.com/2026/04/01140408/bi-videotelematics7-ps.pdf), but that is a commercial-fleet number.

### 3.6 Transit signal priority
- Adoption 19% of US fixed-route bus agencies (USDOT 2023) vs 83% of NACTO's transit-priority-active sample (2025) and 61% of TCRP's 46 large agencies (2020). Market size proxies only (GMI: $735.5M transit-agency share of intelligent traffic signal market, 2025).

### 3.7 ZEB telematics and charge management
- Berg: EU+NA e-bus ITS EUR 355M by 2027. US base 8,116 full-size ZEBs (Jul 2025), >15,000 by 2030; Europe ~25% ZE in 2023 sample heading to 70% by 2030. Generic EV charge-management software sized $1.2-2.4B (2025) at 27-31% CAGR across all fleets.
- Funding headwind: FTA FY2026 Low-No/Bus NOFO cut to $610M (vs $2.03B awarded FY2025) and re-prioritised to low-emission.

### 3.8 Paratransit / demand-responsive transit software
- Microtransit software ~$2B (2025) at ~15% CAGR (syndicated, low confidence); GMI DRT systems $28.4B (2025, 15.3% CAGR) includes operated service. Via ($434M 2025 revenue, ~25% of global on-demand launches), Spare (#2), Trapeze PASS, Ecolane and RouteMatch (both Modaxo) in ADA paratransit. US demand-response trips +14% in 2024 to 373M (APTA).

### 3.9 Electronic fare collection (ADJACENT; flag)
- AFC $10.4-16.7B (2025) at 11-15.6% CAGR (TechSci, Fortune); Cubic ~18% share (Fortune). Excluded from core bus-ITS sizing; included only as cross-sell pool.

### 3.10 Suggested reconciliation for the model (illustrative; derived from sourced figures)
- Core bus/tram ITS, EU+NA 2025: EUR 3.8B (~USD 4.1B at 1.08 USD/EUR; FX assumption, not sourced) growing 5.2% p.a. (Berg) -> ~EUR 4.9B in 2030, ~EUR 5.2B in 2031 (extrapolated one year at 5.2%).
- Split: Europe 68% / NA 32% (Berg 2025 values).
- Component mix proxy: hardware ~47% (R&M/GMI APTS 2025; Mordor PIS hardware 46.55%), software + services ~53%, with software the fastest-growing component (Mordor: 14.45% CAGR).
- Growth-rate cross-checks: Berg 5.0-5.3%; GMI APTS ~8.6%; GMI AVL 10.1%; MarketsandMarkets APC+PIS 8.8%; Mordor PIS 10.0%; MarketsandMarkets PIS 11.6%. A 5-8% range for a mature EU+NA bus scope with upside from software mix and electrification is defensible.
- Vendor sanity check: INIT EUR 330M (2025, global, bus+rail+ticketing) + Clever Devices ~USD 220M (2026) + Trapeze (est. USD 420-680M, low confidence) + Via USD 434M (mostly DRT services) + Conduent/Cubic transit units (not captured) -> top-6 plausibly account for ~40-50% of an EU+NA EUR 3.8B pool, consistent with a concentrated-but-fragmented structure.
