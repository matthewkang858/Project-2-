## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The Clever Devices price is now resolved from a primary source, and it is not $220m.** Hitachi, Ltd.'s *Consolidated Financial Results for the First Quarter ended June 30, 2026* (disclosed 29 July 2026, subsequent-events note, p. 9) state: "The consideration for the acquisition was 302 million dollars (47,919 million yen). In addition to this acquisition, Parent repaid 45 million dollars (7,193 million yen) of certain loans owed by Clever Devices from a financial institution." The agreement was signed 31 March 2026 (announced 2 April) and closed 1 July 2026 via Hitachi Rail U.S. Connect Parent, Inc. / Merger Sub LLC; the consideration "may vary due to transaction price adjustment"; the purchase-price allocation (goodwill, acquired assets) was not completed at Q1 and the Q2 FY2026 results (28 Oct 2026) carry no business-combination note. Hitachi's own press release of 1 July 2026 and every outlet read (Hitachi Rail, Metro Magazine, Pulse 2.0, Clever's own release) give **"projected 2026 revenues of over $220 million"** as a revenue figure, not a price; Pulse 2.0's headline ("$220 Million Clever Devices Acquisition") is the likely origin of the mislabel in files 09 and 10.
2. **Implied Clever multiple: ~1.4x equity consideration / ~1.6x total cash outlay on projected 2026 revenue** ($302m / $220m = 1.37x; ($302m + $45m) / $220m = 1.58x). EBITDA is undisclosed. This is the only completed strategic exit of a core bus-ITS platform in the window, and it prices at **roughly 1.5x revenue, not 5–10x**. File 18's "what we would need to believe (d): exits exist at 5–10x revenue to strategics (Hitachi/Clever, Via IPO)" is therefore **not supported by the Clever data point** and must be rewritten (see Section 5).
3. **Across the in-scope deal set, integrated-system and signalling assets changed hands at ~1.0–1.8x revenue and ~12–19x EBIT/EBITDA; only listed growth SaaS (Via) carries revenue multiples above 5x.** Verified from primary sources: Thales GTS EV €1,660m = 1.04x FY2020 revenue (€1.6bn) / 0.98x LTM H1-2021 revenue (€1.7bn) and 13.7x LTM EBIT (€121m, 7.3% margin), with Hitachi guiding "around 10x" forward EV/EBIT at closing; Iteris AV ≈ $314.5m = 1.83x FY2024 revenue, 24.4x FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA (26.9x LTM per the Board; 18.3x FY2025E; 12.1x CY2025E), against Morgan Stanley's precedent set of 10.1–19.1x LTM EBITDA (median 15.8x) and listed comps at 10.2x/9.3x CY24/CY25 EBITDA (INIT 12.1x/9.8x, IVU 10.2x/9.3x, Kapsch 5.9x/6.9x); Frauscher EV €675m = 4.7x 2025E revenue (€145m) and 12.4x 2025E EBITDA *after run-rate synergies*; Conduent Public Transit $164m = 0.44x 2025 revenue ($371m) and ~5.1x direct (unallocated) 2025 EBITDA (~$32m) / 9.1x pre-tax income ($18m); Intel–Moovit $915m total consideration (goodwill $638m, intangibles $331m) with no revenue disclosed.
4. **TfL iBus2 is verified from two primary sources and the GBP 160m / 10-year figure survives, with an important structural nuance.** TfL's Programmes and Investment Committee paper of 6 December 2023 ("iBus2 Contract Award", public Part 1) confirms scope (~8,850 buses and trams, 700 routes, 19,000 stops, ~3,000 signs), the incumbent contract (in place since 2005, expiring 2 May 2027), the Competitive Dialogue procurement (initial tenders Nov 2021, final tenders Apr 2023), the lot structure (Lot 1A/1B on-bus, 50% of fleet each; Lot 2 back office), the **initial term of 10 years from operational commencement with options to extend up to a further 10 years (max 20)**, commencement February 2024, ~24 months transition + ~12 months rollout, and that the Procurement Authority amount sits in an exempt Part 2 paper. INIT's Annual Report 2024 states the contract "has a value of approximately GBP 80m for the investment phase. The parallel contract awarded to init for system operation over ten years is of a comparable volume. Additional options, such as a contract extension for another ten years, can be commissioned". **GBP 160m is therefore the sum of ~GBP 80m investment + ~GBP 80m ten-year operations**, i.e. ≈ GBP 1.8k per vehicle per year all-in, of which the capital/installation phase alone is ≈ GBP 9k per vehicle. Confidence upgraded from medium (Wikipedia) to high for scope, term and structure, medium-high for the GBP 160m total (it is a derived sum of INIT's two "approximately"/"comparable" figures; TfL does not publish the number).
5. **The Swiftly "only 6% of 75+ agencies would strongly recommend their CAD/AVL" claim could not be attributed to any identifiable survey and must stay flagged as an unverified vendor claim.** Swiftly's 22 September 2026 blog gives no author, link or method ("a 3rd party survey of more than 75 transit agencies found last year"). Swiftly's own *State of Public Transit 2025* report (survey of "more than 80 transit agency staff", data from 300+ agencies) does not contain a CAD/AVL recommendation statistic on the published page, and the only third-party study it footnotes — Caltrans TDDC *Report on Transit Technology Ecosystem* (prepared by Rebel, final May 2024) — is a California-only survey of **42** providers (18% of 233) with a 3-point satisfaction question, not a "recommend" metric. The Caltrans report does supply a citable, methodologically transparent substitute: providers "spoke of continuing relationships with vendors with whom they were only 'somewhat' satisfied because it was too difficult – or costly – to switch ... particularly noticeable in proprietary systems where system components are not interchangeable often due to vendor lock-in", and "consistently cited frustrations with managing their vendors and holding them to their contractual obligations".
6. **The Hayden AI / MTA ACE contract structure (per-bus vs per-violation) remains unresolved at primary-source level; the MTA board book is unreachable from this session, but the NY State PARIS procurement records give the contract architecture and value.** data.ny.gov (MTA Procurements) shows Hayden AI contract B62049 split into a **capital portion** ("2023 ACE Systems (Cap Portion)" $29,303,260.50) and an **operating portion** ("2023 ACE Systems (Opr Portion)" $272,418,719.60), both running Sept 2022 – Dec 2027, awarded 8 July 2022 by competitive bid with one proposal received, exempt from Article 4-C ("Emergency or Critical Need"); $64.5m of the operating and $16.4m of the capital portion had been expended by FY2025. The combined current value (~$301.7m) is larger than the $141m April-2024 modification cited in file 13 (which should be read as an increment, not the total). The ~90/10 operating/capital split and fixed not-to-exceed amounts are consistent with a per-unit service-fee structure (hardware + monthly service) rather than a pure per-violation revenue share, but this is inference (low confidence); the only public per-bus pricing in the dataset remains AC Transit's revenue share capped at $2,500 per bus per month (file 31).
7. **Net effect on the investment thesis:** the exit case for a bus-ITS platform should be underwritten at **~1.5x revenue / ~10–15x EBITDA for an integrated-systems asset** (Clever ≈ 1.6x; Thales ≈ 1.0x and 13.7x EBIT; Iteris 1.8x and 12–18x forward EBITDA; precedent median 15.8x) and **7–11x revenue only if the asset has Via-like growth and net retention** — which is a different business model (managed on-demand services and SaaS, >25% growth) from the CAD/AVL incumbents that Hitachi and Modaxo bought. Modaxo's 0.44x revenue for Conduent transit is the floor; Hitachi's ~1.6x for the North American CAD/AVL leader is, on current evidence, the ceiling for a strategic exit of a hardware-plus-software incumbent.

## 5. Implications for the investment thesis (file 18) and the deal table (file 10)

1. **Replace "exits exist at 5–10x revenue to strategics (Hitachi/Clever, Via IPO)"** with: "Strategic exits of integrated bus/rail ITS businesses have cleared at ~1–2x revenue and ~12–19x EBIT/EBITDA (Clever ≈1.4–1.6x revenue; Thales GTS ≈1.0x revenue / 13.7x LTM EBIT; Iteris 1.8x revenue / 24x LTM but 12–18x forward EBITDA; Frauscher 4.7x revenue only because of a ~35–40% EBITDA margin, at 12.4x synergised EBITDA; Modaxo at 0.44x revenue is the floor). Revenue multiples of 7–11x have been achieved only by Via as a listed, >25%-growth SaaS/managed-services platform." The entry thesis must therefore be an EBITDA story (margin expansion from ~10% integrated-systems EBIT to SaaS-like 20%+) or a genuine high-growth SaaS story, not a hardware-plus-software incumbent re-rated on revenue.

Flag as inference.
