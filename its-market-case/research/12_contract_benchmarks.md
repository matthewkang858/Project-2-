# 12. Public contract award benchmarks and unit economics (Public ITS: bus + rail)

Prepared 2026-11-07 for the Public ITS (Roadway/bus + Railway) CDD-style case. Companion data file: `data/contract_benchmarks.csv` (122 rows; 12 fixed columns; `value_per_unit` is always **USD per unit** — USD per vehicle/display/loco for vehicle-type rows and USD per route-km / track-km for km-type rows).

**Method and caveats.** The shared WebSearch budget was exhausted before this task began and WebFetch could not resolve any external host, so every source below was read directly with `curl` through the session proxy (Railway Gazette, Mass Transit, Metro Magazine, Alstom/Siemens/Hitachi press rooms, LA Metro's Legistar API, Wikipedia, amNY, Indian business press, PIB). Blocked hosts: FRA/railroads.dot.gov, GAO, APTA, IRJ, Railway Age, Businesswire/Globenewswire, tfl.gov.uk press pages, new.mta.info (Akamai 403), cleverdevices.com, initse.com, aar.org (JS-only). Where a primary page was blocked, the figure is cited to the secondary source actually read and the primary URL is listed as "cited by"; confidence is marked accordingly. FX conversions are analyst approximations (EUR 1.10, GBP 1.27 (1.53 for 2015), CAD 0.74, AUD 0.66 (0.77 for 2017), CHF 1.15, INR 82/USD (74/USD for 2020), JPY per the vendor's own USD figure). "Historical" rows (pre-2019) are kept because they anchor the long-run $/vehicle trend; the 2019–2026 window is the core.

## Key takeaways

1. **Bus CAD/AVL replacement is a USD 15–45k-per-vehicle capex event every 15–20 years, trending up as scope widens.** Full-system awards with disclosed fleet counts cluster at USD 15k (Houston 2003), 21k (NJ Transit 2011), 26–31k (Community Transit 2008, TriMet 2009 incl. APC and LRVs), 18k (Brampton 2021/23, cloud-hosted NAVINEO) and **USD 44.7k per vehicle for LA Metro's ATMS II (2025, USD 129.76m / 2,400+ buses + 500 rail cars, 4-year contract, cloud CAD/AVL + LMR/VoIP radio + disruption management + RTPI)**. LA Metro's prior ATMS I dated from 2004 (21-year cycle); TARC's replaced system was ">10 years old"; Metra replaced systems from 2006/2010 in 2018. LA Metro also pays a separate USD 10.8m / 5-year programme-support consultant (8% of the system contract) — a typical hidden cost of big-agency procurements.
2. **The one clean "SaaS-like" bus benchmark is London iBus2: GBP 160m over 10 years for ~8,800 buses = ~GBP 1,800 (USD 2.3k) per bus per year** for CAD/AVL + onboard PIS including service; the LADOT/GMV renewal (USD 43m through 2030 for 400+ vehicles, ~USD 24k per vehicle per year) shows how much richer per-vehicle spend becomes when onboard video, infotainment and ongoing operations support are bundled for a small fleet.
3. **Onboard video is a USD 9–25k-per-bus retrofit (USD 24–25k for NYCT 2010 and RTC Las Vegas 2015 double-deck/artic fleets)**, and is now being re-driven by regulation: FRA 49 CFR 229.136 requires inward/outward image recording on all lead passenger locomotives by 12 Oct 2027 (Metrolink 117 units, PATH USD 10m Wi-Tronix award), while AI bus-lane enforcement cameras (MTA ACE on 1,400+ buses; SamTrans 235 buses) and APC (San Diego USD 21.5k per LRV in 2010; Calgary CTrain 2026) are adjacent onboard-sensor pools.
4. **Brownfield metro CBTC resignalling costs USD 4–30m per route-km, with New York at the top of the world range.** NYC: G line USD 20.4m/km base award (USD 30.9m/km on the USD 556.4m project estimate); A/C Fulton-Liberty USD 1.9bn for 42 track-miles (~USD 28m per track-km, bundled with track renewal); 2015–19 programme USD 18.3m/route-km; 2025–29 plan USD 44.7m/route-km. Elsewhere: BART USD 4.0m/km (USD 798m, 200 km, 11 years), TTC Line 1 USD 10.8m/km (C$562m, 38.4 km, 13 years), MARTA USD 6.5m/km (USD 500m incl. onboard), Berlin U5/U8 USD 5.5m/km (GoA2), Oslo USD 3.2m/km incl. 25 years of maintenance, Copenhagen S-bane GoA4 upgrade USD 1.75m/km incl. onboard for 226 trains. Greenfield Asian metros are USD 1.3–1.8m/km (Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi) and ~USD 6.7m/km where platform screen doors are bundled (Singapore CRL). Conventional (non-CBTC) resignalling is ~USD 1.2m/km (MBTA Red/Orange).
5. **Mainline ETCS L2 trackside is an order of magnitude cheaper per km than metro CBTC: EUR 0.46–0.79m per route-km in Italy's EUR 2.7bn / 4,220 km national programme**, EUR 1.3–1.4m per track-km in a dense urban node (Stuttgart), GBP 2.8m/km for ECDP preparatory works alone, and A$6m/km where a greenfield line is included (Queensland). Onboard ETCS runs EUR 245k per vehicle (upgrade), EUR 540–665k per vehicle (retrofit with maintenance / ATO), GBP 364k per new-build trainset (ECDP). India's Kavach overlay is the low-cost outlier at ~USD 40–61k per route-km and ~USD 85k per locomotive.
6. **US PTC overlays priced at USD 0.31–0.39m per route-km (Wabtec I-ETMS: Rail Runner USD 55m/160 km; Brightline USD 120m/385 km; LIRR/MNR USD 428m/1,100 track-km)**, but full agency programmes including ATC and fibre run higher (MBTA USD 900m / 394 route-miles = USD 1.4m/km). The post-2020 PTC pool is maintenance, software upgrades and onboard replacements, not greenfield.
7. **The signalling market is moving to decade-long frameworks with 25-year service tails**, which locks in incumbents: DB EUR 6.3bn (4 suppliers, >=15,500 units 2025–28, ~EUR 406k per unit), OBB EUR 2.6bn (Hitachi + Siemens, >=10 years + 25-year service), SBB SFr 1.4bn (Hitachi, Siemens, Stadler, 10+15 years + 25-year maintenance), Network Rail CP6 >EUR 7bn, Sydney Trains 20-year ETCS framework, Paris RER EUR 300m framework. Maintenance attach rates visible in awards: G-line USD 37m / 25 years (10% of capex), BART USD 8.6m / 20 years support, Oslo 25 years embedded, Santiago 20 years, Bengaluru 5 years, Muni 10+10 years.
8. **Bundling is pervasive and must be stripped out before using a number**: Santiago L7 (EUR 355m = CBTC + 37 trains + 20 years), Bhopal/Indore (EUR 387m = 156 cars + CBTC + 15 years), Agra/Kanpur, Turin, Lisboa, Sydney Metro C&SW (EUR 350m = 23 trains + CBTC), Ontario Line (C$9bn RSSOM), NSCR CP04 (JPY 114bn E&M + track + ETCS). Only ~25 of the 122 rows are clean single-scope awards with disclosed value and quantity.
9. **Supplier concentration by segment is extreme in rail and moderate in bus.** Every CBTC award captured went to Siemens, Hitachi/Thales, Alstom/Bombardier or (new entrants) Stadler (MARTA USD 500m, 2024 — its first US signalling win) and Mitsubishi Electric (NYC QBL East USD 62.65m, 2022 — MTA's first non-European CBTC supplier). Bus CAD/AVL awards 2019–2026 went to Clever Devices (LA Metro, SacRT), INIT (TfL, King County, Denver TSP), Equans/Systrans (Metrolinx, Brampton, Citilink), Vontas (Hamilton), GMV (LADOT) — a five-vendor oligopoly with regional footholds, and Optibus/Swiftly winning software-only scopes.
10. **Disclosure is the binding constraint on benchmarking**: TTC Line 2 (Hitachi, 2026), Muni TCUP (Hitachi, 2025), Metrolinx CAD/AVL (Systrans, 2026), S-bane SuSy (Hitachi, 2026), Hamilton (Vontas), Vancouver Broadway (Thales) and most SaaS wins publish no value. Board agendas (LA Metro Legistar, TfL ModernGov) are the best primary route to values; vendor releases usually give scope and term but not price.
