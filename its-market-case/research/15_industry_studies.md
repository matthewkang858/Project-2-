# 15 — Existing industry and consulting studies on transit tech and rail digitalisation

**Workstream:** Benchmarking the Public ITS case (Roadway/bus + Railway) against existing industry, association and consulting studies.
**Prepared:** 2026-11-07 (research sub-agent). Base year targeted 2025; forecast horizon 2031.
**Scope reminder:** Public ITS = software, hardware and services sold to public transit operators/agencies. ROADWAY = bus (incl. BRT, paratransit/demand-response, trolleybus). RAILWAY = metro, light rail/tram, commuter/regional and mainline passenger rail. Freight rail is adjacent and is flagged wherever a source bundles it (UNIFE, SCI Verkehr and all "rail supply"/CCS totals bundle freight). Fare collection and roadway traffic management are adjacent and flagged.

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **UNIFE World Rail Market Study (WRMS) is the benchmark for the railway side and is Bain-authored since 2024.** 10th edition (Sept 2024, Bain): average annual global rail supply market **EUR 201.8bn (2021-2023, 2023 real values)** across 66 countries = 99% of global rail traffic; segment split **services EUR 77.1bn, rolling stock EUR 63.3bn, infrastructure EUR 38.1bn, rail control EUR 22.3bn, turnkey management EUR 1.1bn**; forecast **+3.0% p.a. real to EUR 240.8bn (2027-2029)** based on >10,000 identified future project orders. 11th edition (Sept 2026, Bain): **EUR 221bn (2023-2025) growing 3.2% p.a. to EUR 266.8bn (2029-2031)**; historic growth 4.6% p.a. (2021-23 to 2023-25); regional CAGRs to 2029-31: Western Europe 3.7%, Eastern Europe 3.6%, Asia Pacific 3.5% (still largest region), North America 3.0%, Latin America 5.5%, Africa/Middle East 1.2%, CIS 0.7%. The 2026 segment split (incl. CCS) is only in the paid digital modules and was not retrievable.
2. **Rail control (CCS) is ~11% of the rail supply market and two independent houses agree on ~EUR 20-22bn/yr globally, freight included.** UNIFE: EUR 22.3bn (2021-23 avg). SCI Verkehr (CCS study, March 2026): **~EUR 20bn global CCS market volume in 2025, Europe almost half**, train control systems 40% of CCS, Europe the fastest-growing CCS region to 2030; in Europe ~90% of 2025 CCS volume is modernisation/maintenance of the existing network vs ~10% new infrastructure, whereas China is ~40% modernisation / ~60% new build (SCI chart). Both figures include freight, mainline and urban, trackside + onboard. For a passenger-transit ITS case, CCS must be haircut to urban/commuter passenger scope (SCI explicitly notes the metro/commuter CCS segment is "dynamic" and high-cost per project).
3. **UNIFE's "accessible market" concept matters for the competitive/entry story.** Accessible = the share of the total market open to EU-based suppliers, excluding in-house production and countries with protective trade barriers/strong domestic preference. Trend: 70% (2008 ed.) → 63% (2013-15) → 62% (2020 ed.) → 61% (2022 ed.) → 59% (2021-23, 2024 ed.) → **56% (2023-25, 2026 ed.)**; UNIFE puts the missed opportunity at ~EUR 97bn/yr. Alstom's URD 2025/26 reproduces the WRMS 2024 accessible-market chart: **total accessible EUR 119.2bn (2021-23) → EUR 139.4bn (2027-29), +2.6% p.a.**; **accessible rail control EUR 15.7bn → EUR 19.0bn (derived ~3.2% p.a.)**, services 34.2 → 40.5, rolling stock 43.9 → 51.0, infrastructure 24.4 → 27.1, turnkey 1.1 → 1.8; by region Europe ex-CIS 59.9 → 67.4, Americas 33.4 → 41.2, Asia/Pacific 18.9 → 20.8, Africa/ME/CIS 7 → 10. Accessible rail control is therefore ~70% of total rail control (15.7/22.3, derived) — China (CRSC, CASCO) is the main closed block.
4. **The only UNIFE-sourced CCS growth rate retrievable is ~3.2% p.a. real for the accessible rail-control market (2021-23 → 2027-29, derived from the Alstom-reproduced chart).** This is lower than most vendor "railway signalling" CAGRs (5-9%) and than SCI Verkehr's earlier 4.6% nominal (2023-28, via sibling file 03). The 2024 abstract says the Bain forecast is built bottom-up from >10,000 projects and that Western Europe grew 7.3% p.a. in 2021-23 with ERTMS trackside and onboard among the strongest growth areas. The 2024 study also contains a railway cybersecurity market estimate 2023-2029 (paid content, not retrieved).
5. **SCI Verkehr's broader world market study (Sept 2026) is more bullish than UNIFE and now ranks Europe first:** global rail industries market **>EUR 275bn by 2030 at 3.9% p.a. (2025-30)**; average annual volume 2024-26 by region: **Europe EUR 77.7bn (4.4% CAGR), Asia 73.9 (3.0%), North America 29.9 (4.2%), CIS 28.5 (2.9%), Africa/Middle East 9.0 (6.1%), South/Central America 4.5 (5.2%), Australia 4.5 (4.5%)** (sum ~EUR 228bn, consistent with UNIFE's EUR 221bn for 2023-25). SCI states "increased investment in the digitalisation of rail operations is a key driver" of growth, urban rail technology is the fastest-growing product market, passenger rail drives growth while freight-related segments are "under severe pressure". SCI's metro-vehicle study (May 2026): EUR 17.6bn (2025) → EUR 22.9bn (2030), 5.4% p.a.; after-sales = 52% of Europe's and 56% of North America's 2026-30 metro-vehicle volume; ~850 vehicles in explicitly automated projects plus ~550 linked to CBTC/signalling scope in the 2025-30 pipeline.
6. **USDOT ITS Deployment Tracking Survey (DTS) 2023 is the best US adoption benchmark but is not comparable with 2020 at headline level.** 2023 wave (fielded 3 Oct 2023 - 19 Jan 2024; random NTD sample of large-urban, small-urban and rural agencies; 464 completes, 63% response): all modes AVL 81%, CADS 70%, MDT 62%, APC 37%, MMS 27%, TSP 13%; fixed-route bus AVL 85% (73% of agencies at 100% fleet coverage), CADS 62%, MDT 63%, APC 54% (36% at full coverage), MMS 32%, TSP 19%; large-urban vs rural: AVL 85% vs 76%, APC 60% vs 10%, MMS 36% vs 22%, TSP 25% vs 2%; real-time info via one or more channels 84% large urban / 83% small urban / 66% rural; open data feed 42% (59% large urban); GTFS 33%, GTFS-RT 26%; EFP 42%; in-vehicle dynamic signage 43% of agencies with fixed-route service; 56% of agencies serving bus stops have no dynamic signage at stops; 39% plan to expand/upgrade ITS in 2024-26 (45% large urban) and 34% plan new/emerging ITS; 52% plan to upgrade fare systems within 5 years; only 9% have an ITS-specific cybersecurity policy. The 2020 wave (large metros only, 136 completes) reported AVL 92%, CADS 85%, MDT 79%, APC 71%, MMS 34%, TSP 32%, and fleet coverage among adopters of AVL 99%, CADS 99%, MDT 100%, APC 92%, TSP 47%.
7. **Within the historically surveyed large-metro panel the DTS shows the core stack saturating and the analytics/priority layers still climbing (fixed-route bus, 2016 → 2020 → 2023):** AVL 87% → 89% → 95%; CADS 57% → 73% → 80%; MDT 65% → 70% → 82%; APC 69% → 77% → 85%; TSP 30% → 32% → 47%; MMS n/a → 32% → 56%; in-vehicle dynamic electronic signage 42% → 78% (2020 → 2023, partly a question-wording artefact); plans to expand/upgrade ITS 48% (2016) → 68% (2020) → 65% (2023); plans to invest in new ITS 54% (2020) → 57% (2023). Read-across for the model: large-agency CAD/AVL is a replacement/upgrade market; growth whitespace is APC, TSP, MMS/asset management, signage and the small-urban/rural tail. DTS sample sizes for rail modes were too small for subgroup analysis, so DTS gives **no rail-mode adoption rates**, and DTS does not ask about onboard cameras.
8. **TCRP syntheses corroborate saturation and name the whitespace:** TCRP Synthesis 155 (2021) states CAD/AVL "have become quasi-universal in urban bus operations"; TCRP Synthesis 149 (2020) found 61% (28 of 46) of surveyed agencies had active TSP and 28% were in pre-deployment/planning; TCRP Synthesis 158 (2022) covers transit cybersecurity; TCRP Synthesis 77 (2008) is the APC state-of-practice baseline (86 agencies surveyed, via sibling file 02).
9. **UITP's October 2026 "From Digital Investment to Value" (45 organisations, 25 countries) frames the demand side:** nearly two-thirds of respondents self-rate as only at the "developing" stage of digital maturity; financial constraints are the most-cited barrier, followed by skills gaps and compatibility with legacy ecosystems; ~45% name operational efficiency the top priority for the next three years. UITP's Global Economic Outlook 2026 (quoted in the same report) ranks operators' 2026 capex priorities: fleet renewal/expansion 78%, electrification 48%, **digital systems and data platforms 46%**, core infrastructure 43%, depots 41%, ticketing/fare collection 32%, stations/stops 30%, safety and security systems 27%. UITP/Axis video-surveillance brief (Sept 2018, 61 respondents, 67% Western Europe): 95% have video surveillance installed; on-board rolling-stock cameras rose from 76% to 90% of respondents (2015 → 2018); average cameras per network ~2,900 → ~4,900; 82% have a digital/IP component; 73% report positive passenger reaction.
10. **Consulting-house views are thin in the public domain.** Roland Berger (as WRMS author) gave the 2020 and 2022 baselines (EUR 177bn in 2017-19, +3.6% p.a. since 2017; EUR 176.5bn in 2019-21 → EUR 211bn in 2025-27 at 3.0%; "services, rolling stock and infrastructure segments" to grow 2.5-3.8% p.a.; digital technologies ERTMS/CBTC/predictive maintenance/DAC/5G/passenger information/ticketing/cybersecurity named as growth factors). Bain (WRMS 2024/2026) quotes: "Digitalisation continues to expand its impact across the entire rail value chain"; "industry set to grow by over EUR 40 billion annually by 2029". McKinsey, BCG, Arthur D. Little, Oliver Wyman, L.E.K. and ITF/OECD pages were blocked or carried no quantitative transit-technology content — see gaps. The only ITS-association market datapoint is ERTICO's 2015 relay of Berg Insight: European public-transport ITS **EUR 1.03bn (2014) → EUR 1.46bn (2019), 7.2% CAGR** — a useful early point on the Berg series that sibling files 01/02 carry forward (EUR 2.55bn Europe in 2025).

## 4. Reconciliation notes for the model

- **Rail control anchor.** Use UNIFE EUR 22.3bn (2021-23 avg, 2023 real) as the total world rail control market and SCI ~EUR 20bn (2025) as the independent cross-check; the difference is within the real-vs-nominal and period definitions. Both include freight and mainline. Accessible (non-China) rail control is ~EUR 15.7bn → 19.0bn (2021-23 → 2027-29). Urban/commuter passenger CCS is a sub-scope that neither free source quantifies; sibling file 03 collects vendor CBTC/urban-signalling estimates (USD 4-5bn in 2025) for that carve-out.
- **Growth.** UNIFE total market 3.0-3.2% p.a. real; accessible rail control ~3.2% p.a. real (derived); SCI world market 3.9% nominal (2025-30) with Europe 4.4%; SCI CCS "solid growth", Europe fastest; SCI metro vehicles 5.4%. A 4-6% nominal CAGR for passenger-rail CCS/digital to 2031 is consistent with these benchmarks; anything above 8% needs a scope reason (e.g., CBTC resignalling waves in NYC/London/Paris, India metro build-out).
- **Bus-side adoption.** DTS 2023 (all US agencies) vs 2020 (large metros) must not be chained as a time series; use the historically surveyed panel (D15-D17) for trends and D2-D5 for penetration by agency size. For a bottom-up US installed-base model: AVL ~85% of fixed-route agencies with 73% at full fleet coverage; APC 54% with 36% at full coverage; TSP 19% (mostly partial fleets); MMS 32%.
- **Demand-side priorities.** UITP 2026: digital systems/data platforms are the #3 capex priority (46%) behind fleet renewal (78%) and electrification (48%); financial constraints and skills are the binding barriers — supports a SaaS/opex-light, outcome-priced entry thesis.
- **Edition history of WRMS totals (useful for showing forecast reliability):** 2017-19 EUR 177bn (2020 ed.) → 2019-21 EUR 176.5bn (2022 ed., forecast EUR 211bn for 2025-27) → 2021-23 EUR 201.8bn (2024 ed., forecast EUR 240.8bn for 2027-29) → 2023-25 EUR 221bn (2026 ed., forecast EUR 266.8bn for 2029-31). The 2024 edition's 2021-23 actual (201.8) already exceeded the 2022 edition's path to 211 by 2025-27, i.e. UNIFE forecasts have been conservative post-Covid.
