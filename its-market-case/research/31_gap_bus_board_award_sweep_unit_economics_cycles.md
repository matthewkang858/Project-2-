# 31 — Gap fill: North American bus onboard-technology awards 2019–2026 — unit economics and realised replacement cycles (board-record sweep)

**Prepared:** 2026-11-07 | **Scope:** software, hardware and services bought by US/Canadian public bus agencies for onboard and fleet-management technology (CAD/AVL, onboard computers/MDTs, APC, onboard video, real-time/passenger information, scheduling software, bus-lane enforcement cameras, radios/routers). Fare collection and microtransit operations are adjacent and only noted where bundled. Rail is out of scope here (see files 12, 21, 22).

**Companion data file:** `data/contract_benchmarks.csv` — **58 new rows appended by this task (rows 171–228; same 12 columns; `value_per_unit` = USD per vehicle/unit/sign/router/camera).** A sibling gap task appended 48 tagged rows ([A03]–[H03], rows 123–170) minutes before this one; 17 awards found independently here (CapMetro, Kitsap, MARTA, Santa Monica, TTC VISION, NJT 2026, WMATA 2023, CTA 2024, AC Transit maintenance/APC/Hayden, CCCTA, NCTD, Marin APC, MTA ABLE, LA Metro Hayden, CTA Hayden) were removed from my append to avoid double counting; where my primary-source value differs from the sibling row it is flagged in Section 7.

## 0. Method and caveats (read first)

- **Sources actually opened:** Granicus Legistar web API (`webapi.legistar.com/v1/{client}/matters`, `/attachments`, `/versions`, `/texts`) for 12 clients (metro, kingcounty, octa, actransit, miamidade, milwaukeecounty, montgomerycountymd, phoenix, broward, nashville, austintexas, mtc; ~56 title terms × 12 clients, 2017+), plus attachment PDFs from AC Transit, Broward, OCTA, Milwaukee and LA Metro and council-report text from Phoenix; data.ny.gov **MTA Procurements: Beginning 2018** (dataset `twsw-2mqa`, SODA queries by vendor and description); agency board PDFs read directly with curl (MARTA, Kitsap, CTA, Miami-Dade, TTC, WMATA CIP project pages, GCRTA, TARC, SMART, WeGo, Metropolitan Council, TriMet, Marin, NCTD, CCCTA, CapMetro, SBMTD, SFMTA, GCTD, Topeka Metro vendor bids, Pace and Valley Regional TAM plans, Virginia DRPT useful-life chart, Oregon ODOT asset handbook/APC white paper); trade press (Mass Transit, TAM America, Houston Public Media, Yahoo/NJ.com, Metro Magazine); vendor pages (Avail, GMV, Consat, Hayden AI, INIT).
- **Blocked / not usable:** mta.info board documents (385-byte block pages), transit.dot.gov FTA Report 0088 (GCRTA Radio/ITS — only the search snippet was readable), itskrs/itscosts.its.dot.gov (TLS/403), file.lacounty.gov (Foothill 2015 staff report; value taken from search snippet), citizenportal.ai and withpavilion (JS-only; values from search snippets), MBTA/SEPTA/TriMet/UTA board index pages (JS; no document links), Massachusetts CTHRU (dataset `pegc-naaa` holds **only payroll object classes for MBTA**, so no vendor payments — a dead end for Clever/Swiftly payments), Montgomery County and Miami-Dade Legistar clients (0–1 matter returned; Miami-Dade uses its own `legistarfiles` PDFs, one of which was read).
- **Search budget:** the shared WebSearch cap (200 calls per turn) was reached after ~45 queries from this task; Ride On, Fairfax Connector, HRT, Westchester, CDTA, TransLink's 2026 re-procurement, Metrolinx value and Edmonton/OC Transpo/Winnipeg were therefore **not searched** (listed as gaps).
- **Conventions:** USD unless stated; CAD converted at 0.74 (file 12 convention); "NTE" = not-to-exceed authority (usually includes contingency, so true spend is lower); fleet denominators are FY2025 NTD active full-size buses from `data/ntd_2025_bus_fleet_by_agency.csv` unless the board document gives the installed count. Confidence: **high** = primary board/contract document or two independent sources; **medium** = single secondary source or snippet; **low** = inferred.

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **A full CAD/AVL platform replacement in 2021–2026 costs USD 19–45k per bus (median ≈ USD 40k on clean rows), i.e. the pre-2015 anchors (USD 15–33k) have risen ~30–50% in nominal terms while scope widened to APC, AVA, RTPI, 5G routers, EV management and cloud hosting.** Clean 2021–2026 rows: CapMetro 2025 USD 20.5k/bus (NTE incl. 10% contingency, 449 buses, Vontas), Marin 2025 USD 19.1k (68 vehicles, Systrans, 5-yr incl. maintenance), MARTA 2023 USD 18.1k over 8 years (584 vehicles, Clever; USD 12.7k base), Santa Monica 2021 USD 42.1k (193 buses incl. 5 yrs maintenance), TARC 2023 USD 42.2k (186 buses incl. options), Kitsap 2023 USD 41–68k (139 full-size / 84 routed buses), Evansville 2024 USD 40.2k (31 vehicles, 5-yr), LA Metro 2025 USD 44.7k (2,900 vehicles incl. rail; USD 54k bus-only). Historical anchors with fleet counts now confirmed from board documents: AC Transit 2013/15 USD 33–37k (637 vehicles), Broward 2014 USD 38.3k (328 buses, 6 yrs maintenance), Foothill 2015 USD 39.2k (357), Miami-Dade 2013 USD 18.2k over 12 years (791), PennDOT statewide 2018 USD 32.0k (1,018 vehicles, 33 small agencies), TTC VISION 2016 USD 26k (C$35.4k incl. 10 yrs vendor maintenance; 2,300 vehicles).
2. **Mid-life "refresh" awards are a distinct, cheaper event at USD 2.5–17k per bus every 8–13 years:** WMATA 2023–26 IVN/APC/touchscreen upgrade USD 13.4k/bus (contract value USD 18.04m, USD 25.0m allocated; 1,349 buses), SMART Detroit 2023 IVN5 + radio refresh USD 17.4k/bus (264 buses; system from 2013), Phoenix 2018 surveillance + yard RTLS change order USD 15.6k/bus (800 regional buses; capital USD 11.5k), OCTA 2019–2024 upgrades to a 2011 OrbCAD system USD 10.3k/bus cumulative, NJ Transit 2026 GPS/modem upgrade USD 2.5k/bus (2,542 buses; onboard kit from 2013). Mega-refreshes keep platforms alive far beyond accounting life.
3. **Realised full-replacement cycles are 15–21 years (median ≈ 19; n = 7 dated pairs) — roughly double the useful-life benchmarks agencies file in TAM plans (4–10 years).** LA Metro 2004→2025 (21), CapMetro 2006→2025 (19), Santa Monica 2002→2021 (19), Phoenix 2001→2016 (15), AC Transit 1999→2013/15 (14–16), MCTS ~1996→2021+ (≈25, award still open), TTC CIS 1980s→2016 (30+). Life-extensions now in force push several more past 15 years: Metro Transit MN OnRoute 2002→2031 (amendment March 2026), Miami-Dade Clever 2013→2030, Broward ISR 2015→2027+, GCRTA Vontas ~2004→2028, OCTA 2011→2026+. TAM/guidance benchmarks: mobile data computers 7 yrs, communications/radios 10 yrs, software 4 yrs (Virginia DRPT); communications 10 yrs, HASTUS 4 yrs (Valley Regional TAM); 5 yrs for computers/radios/cameras (Oregon ODOT); CAD/AVL 7–10 yrs, radio 10–15 yrs (FTA Report 0088, GCRTA); Equans states its CAD/AVL hardware is designed for a 12-year life.
4. **Recurring vendor maintenance + hosting for an installed CAD/AVL platform is USD 1.1–3.3k per bus per year (median ≈ USD 2.2k) at tier-2/3 agencies and USD 0.15–0.4k per bus per year where the agency self-maintains hardware (NYCT).** CTA 2024 USD 3.32k (USD 43.4m / 7 yrs / 1,870 buses incl. parts and field repair), GCRTA 2024–28 USD 2.89k (316 buses), AC Transit 2025 USD 2.16k (630 buses: hardware warranty USD 217, software maintenance USD 919, cloud hosting USD 514–723, modules/services), Miami-Dade 2025–30 USD 2.6k (incl. upgrade and EV features), Metro Transit MN 2026 USD 3.2k (equipment + support), NORTA FY25 ≈ USD 1.8–2.1k, SORTA 2023 USD 1.55k, MARTA option years USD 1.0k, NYCT Clever software maintenance USD 155 (2020–25; 5,979 buses). Hosting alone (AC Transit Azure) is USD 0.5–0.7k per bus per year.
5. **Pure data/analytics SaaS layered on top of a CAD/AVL (Swiftly, Optibus, Clever Insights) prices at USD 0.5–1.0k per bus per year for 300–650-bus fleets and USD 1.4–2.0k for sub-150-bus fleets buying several modules**: AC Transit USD 546→829 (2024–27, 630 buses), MARTA USD 926 (535 buses), NCTD base USD 493 (336 vehicles), CCCTA USD 1,972 (125 buses, 5 modules), Marin Optibus scheduling USD 789 (68 vehicles), LA Metro Nextrip predictions USD 128–175. Scheduling (HASTUS) licence/support runs USD 0.2–0.8k per bus per year (Broward USD 215–312, Phoenix regional USD 580–780, Metro Council USD 656–731, AC Transit ≈ USD 555 incl. hosting) plus a version upgrade of USD 1.4–4.6k per bus every 7–10 years (OCTA 2013→2020 USD 3.05k, LA Metro 2015→2024 USD 1.4k per vehicle, AC Transit 2022 USD 4.6k, Metro Council USD 2.6k, TriMet +USD 4.5m for crew modules).
6. **Component price points now have 2020–2025 primary anchors:** APC full turnkey USD 7.05k per vehicle capital + USD 358 per vehicle per year (Marin 2024, 69 vehicles; sensors USD 5.0k, install USD 0.6k, PM/NTD certification USD 0.7k, software USD 0.7k), ≈ USD 5.3k per bus for fleet gap-fill + USD 238 per bus per year support (AC Transit 2022), USD 4k + USD 1k/yr (Blacksburg via ODOT 2021); onboard video full retrofit USD 14.5k per bus (Broward 2020, 276 buses, Apollo), camera-system replacement USD 5.0k per bus (GCTD 2020), DVR/NVR-only swap USD 2.8–3.5k per bus plus USD 140–395 per bus per year extended warranty (Topeka 2020 bids), video-management software licence + remote install USD 1.03k per new bus (Broward 2023); mobile routers USD 5.25k per unit installed (OCTA 2025, 801 Cradlepoint R1900); cellular data ≈ USD 1k per bus per year (Santa Monica); wayside digital signs USD 8.7k per sign over 7 years incl. CMS and maintenance (Houston/GMV, 3,000 signs); after-market CAD/AVL kit + vendor install on small buses USD 27.9k per bus (WeGo 2025, 19 buses).
7. **Bus-lane/bus-stop enforcement cameras are the fastest-growing onboard pool and are priced as a service at ≈ USD 20–30k per bus per year, largely citation-funded:** MTA 300 ABLE systems USD 14.2k capital + USD 51.3k operating per system for the first 15 months (data.ny.gov), with the follow-on "2023 ACE systems (operating portion)" contract carried at **USD 272.4m for Sep-2022–Dec-2027 (USD 64.5m expended by FY2025)** — larger than the USD 141m cited in file 13; LA Metro USD 105k per system over 5.25 years (≈ USD 20k/yr); LADOT USD 68.6k per camera over 3 years; CTA USD 52.6k per bus initial; AC Transit no upfront cost with revenue share capped at USD 2,500 per bus per month (USD 30k/yr).
8. **Awards remain lumpy and concentrated:** LA Metro's USD 129.8m (2025) alone equals ~10% of Berg's North American bus/tram ITS market for one year; the next tier of 2023–2026 CAD/AVL events (WMATA USD 18–25m, Metro Transit MN USD 10.5m, Miami-Dade USD 11m, MARTA USD 10.5m, CapMetro USD 9.2m, Santa Monica USD 8.1m, TARC USD 7.9m, Kitsap USD 5.7m, Broward USD 5m extension) are USD 5–25m each. Clever Devices (now Hitachi) won or retained every tier-3/4 CAD/AVL award found in 2021–2026 except CapMetro (Vontas), Metro Transit MN and GCRTA (Vontas incumbency), Marin (Systrans), Calgary (Consat), Evansville (Trapeze) and TARC (Avail); INIT won no new North American CAD/AVL award found for 2019–2026 (its wins are add-ons: King County CarMedialab, Denver TSP, NCTD COASTER APC).
9. **The 2026–2031 refresh wave is now dated agency by agency:** platforms installed 2009–2016 at TriMet (2009, INIT, 668 buses), Denver RTD (2011, INIT, 968), NJ Transit (2013, Clever, 2,542 — partial refresh 2026), Miami-Dade (2013, Clever, 845 — extended to 2030, replacement solicitation planned), SMART (2013 — refreshed 2023), Broward (2015, ISR, 459 — solicitation 2026), AC Transit (2015, Clever, 630 — extended to 2026, 5-yr maintenance 2025–30), Phoenix regional (2016, Clever, 800+), TTC (2016–18, Clever, 2,300), King County (~2016, INIT, 1,450) and TransLink (2016, Clever, 10-year term to 2026) will be 15–20 years old in 2026–2031. That cohort is ≈ 12,000 buses; at USD 25–40k per bus it represents USD 0.30–0.48bn of full-replacement awards in the forecast window, on top of CTA (Clever since ~2007, 1,870 buses), Metro Transit MN (2002, 650), GCRTA (~2004, 316), OCTA (2011, 446) and MCTS (open RFP, 384) whose decisions are already overdue.
10. **Bottom-up cross-check lands at USD 0.6–1.6bn (mid USD 1.0bn) for North American bus ITS spend in 2025 (≈ 50% capex / 50% recurring), consistent with Berg's EUR 1.22bn (USD 1.34bn, which includes fare validators/ticketing back-office and journey planners; ≈ USD 1.07bn ex-fare per file 17-gap) and with NTD bus-mode communication & information capex of USD 0.37bn (2025) / USD 0.39bn (2023–25 average).** The capex half (CAD/AVL replacement + factory-fit onboard kit + video + APC + wayside PIS ≈ USD 0.53bn, US share ≈ USD 0.43bn) sits 15% above the NTD CI figure because NTD books much factory-fit ITS inside the bus purchase.
11. **For the SaaS-versus-capex thesis:** a tier-3 agency's all-in cost of an incumbent CAD/AVL platform is ≈ USD 2.2k per bus per year of vendor maintenance/hosting plus ≈ USD 2.0k per bus per year of amortised replacement capex (USD 30–40k over 15–19 years) — i.e. ≈ USD 4–4.5k per bus per year, versus USD 0.5–1.0k for an overlay data SaaS and ≈ USD 2.3k for TfL's fully bundled iBus2 service contract (file 12). The gap is the entry wedge; the obstacle is that 80% federal capital funding makes the capex path cheaper to the agency's own budget (file 17).

**Useful-life benchmarks versus practice.** TAM/grant guidance puts onboard ITS at 4–10 years (DRPT: MDC 7, comms 10, software 4; VRT: comms 10, HASTUS 4; ODOT: 5; FTA Report 0088: CAD/AVL 7–10, radio 10–15; Equans design life 12). Realised full-platform cycles are 15–21 years (median 19) with hardware-generation refreshes at 8–13 years and connectivity refreshes at 6–8 years. For the forecast this means: (i) the "90%-penetrated fleet" replaces its central platform roughly every 18 years (≈ 5.5% of fleet per year ≈ 4,500 NA buses per year) but buys a mid-life kit refresh roughly every 10 years (≈ 10% per year), and (ii) because the bus itself is replaced at 12–14 years, most onboard hardware is now bought with the bus (factory fit), which is why agencies can run central platforms for two bus generations.

## 6. What this changes for the forecast and the SaaS-versus-capex comparison

- **2026–2031 refresh timing:** the 2009–2016 install cohort (≈ 12,000 buses at TriMet, Denver, NJT, Miami-Dade, Broward, AC Transit, Phoenix, TTC, King County, TransLink, SMART) hits 15–20 years inside the window; with CTA, Metro Transit MN, GCRTA, OCTA and MCTS already overdue, **≈ 15,000 buses (≈ 18% of the NA fleet) face a platform decision by 2031**, worth USD 0.4–0.6bn of full-replacement awards at USD 25–40k per bus — a step up from the 2019–2023 trough when most large agencies extended rather than replaced (Miami-Dade, Broward, Metro Transit MN, OCTA, GCRTA all chose sole-source extensions).
- **Price trend:** per-bus platform prices rose ≈ 1.5–2× nominal between the 2003–2015 anchors (USD 15–39k) and 2021–2025 awards (USD 19–45k) once APC, AVA, routers and cloud hosting are bundled; but software-centric awards (MARTA USD 12.7k base, CapMetro USD 20.5k with hardware from Vontas) show that an entrant with a thin-hardware architecture can undercut the USD 40k+ full-stack price by half.
- **Recurring economics:** incumbents' sole-source maintenance/hosting at USD 2–3.3k per bus per year, rising 3–5% per year (GCRTA +5%/yr; AC Transit hosting +40% from year 2), is the annuity that funds the integrators' 20% maintenance / 30% components revenue mix; it is also the line item a SaaS challenger can attack with a USD 0.5–1.0k overlay today and a USD 2–4k full-service subscription tomorrow (TfL iBus2 benchmark USD 2.3k).
- **Funding structure still favours capex:** every replacement found was 80% federal (5307/5339) or state-funded capital; recurring SaaS and enforcement services are paid from operating budgets or citation revenue (AC Transit, NYC) — see file 17 for the §5307 constraint.
