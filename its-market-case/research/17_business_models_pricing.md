## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The dominant public-ITS business model is still "capex system + long-tail recurring": a one-off project (hardware + software + integration) followed by 5-20-year maintenance/operating contracts and spares.** INIT SE, the closest listed pure-play, reported FY2025 revenue of EUR 329.7m split **49.5% project business / 19.7% maintenance & operation / 30.8% system components (spares, replacements, direct deliveries)**; the same split was 51/21/28 in 2024. Support & maintenance contracts "usually have a term of five years and are paid quarterly or yearly"; operating/technology-partnership contracts now run "up to 20 years"; contracted performance obligations stood at ~EUR 994m at end-2025 (3.0x annual revenue). [P: INIT AR 2024 and AR 2025]
2. **Recurring/aftermarket revenue is 20-50% of revenue for bus-ITS vendors and is the margin engine for rail OEMs.** INIT: ~20% maintenance & operation plus a large spares stream (~31%). IVU Traffic Technologies: 51.8% recurring (hosting + maintenance) [S]. Alstom: Services = 24% of FY2025/26 sales (EUR 4.7bn) but carries a **"mid-teens" operating margin versus a 6.1% group adjusted EBIT margin**; Signalling (14% of sales, EUR 2.7bn) earns "double digit"; Rolling Stock is "breakeven to high-single digit". Services backlog EUR 40.1bn (8.5x services sales) with "longer contracts, up to 30 years" and "indexation as general rule". [P: Alstom equity story Oct 2026]
3. **Margins cluster by layer: hardware-heavy integrators earn ~9-12% EBIT, rail OEM services/signalling mid-teens, SaaS challengers ~40% gross margin but near-zero EBITDA, telematics/enforcement platforms 40%+ EBITDA.** INIT FY2025: gross margin 36.6%, EBITDA margin 14.9%, EBIT margin 9.9% (2021-2025 EBIT margin range 9.2-11.0%) [P]. Cubic Transportation Systems FY2019: operating margin 9.1% on USD 849.8m sales (6.9% in FY2017) [P]. Via FY2025: revenue USD 434.4m (+29%), gross margin ~40%, still loss-making [P GovTech / S 10-K]. Kapsch TrafficCom (tolling/traffic, adjacent) FY2025/26: EBIT margin 1.8% on EUR 430.6m (-19% YoY) [P]. Trimble (software-mix benchmark): 76% of revenue software/services/recurring, 65.1% gross margin, 19.7-34.1% segment operating margins [P]. Verra Mobility (enforcement, adjacent): 42% adjusted EBITDA margin [S].
4. **INIT's 2026 re-segmentation makes the layer economics visible inside one company.** 2026 guidance: Integrated Systems EUR 280-300m revenue / EBIT EUR 34-36m (~12%); Cloud Solutions (SaaS) EUR 40-45m / EUR 5-7m (~13%); Passenger Intelligence (APC/sensors; iris + DILAX) EUR 90-100m / EUR 7-9m (~8%). Indicative revenue mix per BU: Integrated Systems 20% projects / 60% maintenance & operations / 20% sales orders; Cloud Solutions 50% SaaS / 50% other; Passenger Intelligence 70% product sales. H1 2026 actuals: Integrated Systems EBIT margin 8.9%, Cloud 5.6%, Passenger Intelligence 6.2%; group EBIT guidance was cut on 2 Nov 2026 on mix/material availability. [P: INIT FY2025 presentation, H1 2026 report; S: EQS]
5. **Pricing/unit-cost benchmarks that could be sourced:** CAD/AVL full replacement ~USD 49k per vehicle (USD 9.8m for a 200-vehicle Florida fleet, FY2020, ITS JPO) [S]; NTD "communication & information systems" capex ~USD 5.8k per full-size bus per year and ~USD 61k per rail car per year (FY2025, derived) [S]; NYC CBTC USD 18.3m per km for the 2015-19 programme (USD 2.152bn / 117.8 km, CBTC-only) rising to ~USD 45m per km for the 2025-29 programme (USD 5.4bn / ~121 km, full signal modernisation incl. interlockings) [P Wikipedia/MTA]; London 4LM resignalling GBP 760m (Thales, 2015) [P]; UK East Coast digital signalling GBP 350m for 161 km (~GBP 2.2m/km) [P]; ETCS Baseline-3 onboard re-certification "at least EUR 100k per vehicle" [P]; MTA PTC contract USD 428m for LIRR + Metro-North (2013), nationwide PTC USD 6-22bn, commuter-rail PTC > USD 2bn for up to 25 services [P]; NYC major interlocking modernisation ~USD 178m each (USD 356.5m for 34th St + W 4th St) [P]. **Not sourced (gaps): APC per door/bus, onboard camera per bus, PIS display per stop, ETCS onboard unit per train, standard interlocking per station, PTC per locomotive, CAD/AVL SaaS USD per vehicle per month, maintenance as % of licence.**
6. **Customer tenure is measured in decades, which is the core barrier to entry.** Cubic ran TfL's Oyster system from the 1998 Prestige PFI contract (17-year term to 2015, GBP 1.1bn lifetime cost) until Indra took over in 2026 under a seven-year contract (28 years of incumbency) [P]. Cubic's NZ National Ticketing Solution is NZD 1.3bn over 15 years [P]. INIT describes "long-standing customers" and "long-term customer relationships" generating follow-up orders, with operating contracts up to 20 years [P]. Clever Devices (now Hitachi) has supplied agencies "for over thirty years" with customers from 30 to 6,000+ vehicles [P]. Alstom services contracts run up to 30 years; Greater Anglia extended Alstom's train-services agreement by 15 years (GBP ~800m) [S]. Cubic states the average lifecycle of its revenue-management systems is ~10 years, "providing long-term recurring sales visibility" [P].
7. **Implementation cycles are long and slip: 4-8 years award-to-service for metro CBTC, multi-year for integrated CAD/AVL mega-projects.** NYC: L and 7 lines (50 track-miles) took 2000-2018; Queens Blvd phase 1 awarded Aug 2015, integrated testing completed May 2019; Flushing full CBTC service Nov 2018 after funding in the 2010-14 programme; London 4LM: Bombardier awarded 2011 (GBP 350m), terminated 2014 (GBP 80m payout), Thales awarded 2015 (GBP 760m), Circle line ATO March 2022 and H&C January 2023 [P]. INIT's two "mega projects" (TfL iBus NextGen, MARTA AFC 2.0 targeted at the 2026 World Cup) each span several years and depress gross margin during delivery (FY2025 gross margin 36.6% vs 37.0%) [P]. Cubic: "profit margins during the design and build phase ... can be slightly lower than during the operate-and-maintain phase"; some contracts pay nothing during development and recover over the O&M period [P].
8. **Bidding is expensive and the risk is rising with contract size.** INIT raised "risk of losing tenders" from low to medium in 2024 because "larger volume contracts imply a higher exposure if a tender is not won (due to higher selling expenses)"; tender costs are expensed as incurred (no capitalised contract-acquisition costs); sales & marketing expense was EUR 31.3m (11.8% of revenue) in 2024 and EUR 34.8m (10.6%) in 2025 [P]. Alstom signalling contracts are "typical small size; < 2 years" with "selectivity" as an explicit lever; Kapsch "did not win all of the projects it had expected" and lost ~EUR 80m revenue from two cancelled operations projects [P].
9. **US funding architecture biases agencies toward capital purchases and makes SaaS harder to fund.** 49 U.S.C. §5307(d): a capital grant is 80% of net project cost; operating grants may not exceed 50% and are only available to urbanized areas under 200,000 population (plus narrow 76-100-bus exceptions) [P]. §5302 defines "capital project" to include preventive maintenance, **leasing equipment or a facility**, "safety and security equipment ... (including lighting, surveillance, and related intelligent transportation system applications)" and "the introduction of new technology" [P]. Implication: hardware + perpetual licences + multi-year maintenance fit the 80/20 capital template; pure subscriptions are typically paid from local operating budgets unless structured as leases/preventive maintenance. FTA's specific SaaS-eligibility guidance could not be opened (transit.dot.gov blocked) and is listed as a gap. NTD data show the federal share of transit capital at ~40% and of operating at 13.5% in 2025 [S].
10. **Business-model implication for a new entrant:** the defensible wedge is a recurring software/data layer (real-time, analytics, scheduling, enforcement) sold on subscription with thin hardware, because (a) incumbents' economics depend on 5-20-year service tails and spares that a SaaS entrant can bypass, (b) SaaS gross margins (~40% at Via, 65% at Trimble) are structurally above integrator gross margins (~37% at INIT), but (c) funding rules, 10-30-year asset lives and bid costs mean an entrant must either partner with an incumbent on the hardware layer or buy an installed base. Valuation evidence: Via trades ~7-8x revenue while INIT trades ~1.4-1.6x sales / ~14-16x EBIT and Cubic was taken private at 15.7x NTM EBITDA [S].

## 3. Business-model archetypes and unit economics (synthesis)

| Archetype | Typical vendors | Revenue structure | Contract length | Margin evidence | Funding fit (US) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Integrated system + service tail** (bus CAD/AVL, PIS, APC, ticketing) | INIT, Clever/Hitachi, Trapeze/Modaxo, Conduent, Avail, IVU | ~50% project (hardware + software + install), ~20% maintenance/operation, ~30% spares/components (INIT 2025); IVU 52% recurring | 5-year S&M contracts; operating partnerships up to 20 years (INIT) | Gross 36-38%, EBIT ~10%, EBITDA ~15% (INIT); CTS op margin 7-9% (Cubic) | Capital-eligible at 80% federal share; maintenance can be "preventive maintenance" capital |
| **Rail OEM services + signalling** | Alstom, Siemens Mobility, Hitachi Rail, Wabtec | Services 24% of sales but 39% of backlog (Alstom); signalling 14% of sales | Services up to 30 years, indexed; signalling contracts < 2 years | Services mid-teens, signalling double-digit vs 6.1% group aEBIT (Alstom); Wabtec group op margin 16% | Large capital programmes (CIG/formula) with 25-45-year asset lives |
| **Design-build-operate-maintain / outsourcing** | Cubic (fare + ITS), Kapsch (tolling), Conduent | Fixed-price build (sometimes unpaid during build) then SLA-based operate fees | ~10-year system lifecycles; 15-17-year concessions (Oyster, NZ NTS) | Lower margin in build phase, higher in O&M (Cubic); Kapsch EBIT 1.8% after project cancellations | Capital + operating mix; agency outsources ops |
| **Cloud SaaS / data layer** | Via, Swiftly, Optibus, Spare, Moovit, INIT Cloud Solutions | Subscription per agency/vehicle; little hardware | Annual/multi-year subscriptions; renewals | GM ~40% (Via), 65% (Trimble); INIT Cloud EBIT ~6-13%; Via near breakeven | Typically operating budget (50% federal cap, large UZAs excluded) unless structured as lease/preventive maintenance |
| **Enforcement / telematics as-a-service** | Hayden AI, Verra, Samsara | Per-device subscription or revenue-linked (fines) | Multi-year | Verra 42% adj. EBITDA; Samsara USD 2.1bn ARR | Self-funding from citation revenue (NYC > USD 11m/month per tranche [S]) |

**Pricing benchmarks (sourced):** CAD/AVL ~USD 49k per vehicle full replacement (2020) [S]; NTD comm & info capex ~USD 5.8k per bus per year [S]; CBTC USD 18-45m per km (NYC), GBP 760m for four London sub-surface lines [P]; UK mainline ETCS ~GBP 2.2m per km trackside funding [P]; ETCS onboard re-certification >= EUR 100k per vehicle [P]; PTC USD 428m for two commuter railroads (MTA) [P]; major interlockings ~USD 178m each (NYC) [P]. **Unsourced in this run:** USD per vehicle per month for CAD/AVL SaaS; APC per door/bus; onboard camera per bus; PIS display per stop; ETCS onboard unit per train; standard interlocking per station; PTC per locomotive; maintenance as % of licence (the 15-20% rule of thumb).
