**Prepared:** 2026-11-07 | **Scope:** transactions and valuation marks for software, hardware and services vendors selling to public transit (bus) and passenger-rail operators. Adjacent segments (fare collection, tolling/traffic enforcement, parking, industrial inspection, rolling stock) are captured only where a deal bundles them and are **flagged**.

## Key takeaways

1. **The transit-tech deal tape is dominated by strategic consolidators, not PE platforms.** Hitachi Rail (Thales GTS EUR 1.66bn, 2024; Clever Devices ~USD 220m, 2026), Alstom (Bombardier Transportation, 2021; Nomad Digital, 2017), Modaxo/Constellation Software (Conduent transit USD 164m, 2026; Conduent curbside USD 230m, 2024; SISCOG 2025; Fiscaltech 2026) and Via (Remix 2021, Citymapper 2023) account for most of the disclosed-value activity in the 2018–2026 window.
2. **Headline multiples cluster in a wide 1.5x–11x revenue / ~15–16x EBITDA band, depending on the asset type.** Hardware/system-integrator assets trade at ~1–2x revenue (Cubic ~1.9x FY20 sales; Iteris ~1.9x equity/revenue), rail-signalling/industrial-tech at ~15–16x EBITDA (Cubic takeout 15.7x NTM EBITDA per Raymond James; Wabtec/Evident 15.9x CY24 EBITDA), and pure SaaS at 7–11x revenue (Via at IPO ~10.8x trailing revenue, ~7.4x FY25 revenue today).
3. **The listed pure-play comps are small and mixed.** INIT SE (bus ITS, EUR 330m revenue, ~10% EBIT margin, ~USD 0.5bn market cap) guided 2026 revenue growth of 15%+ but cut its EBIT outlook on 2 Nov 2026; Via (USD ~3.2bn market cap) grows ~27–29% with ~40% gross margin and is only now approaching adjusted-EBITDA breakeven; Kapsch TrafficCom (tolling/traffic, adjacent) shrank 19% with a 1.3% EBIT margin; Verra Mobility (enforcement/tolling, adjacent) runs 40–42% adjusted-EBITDA margins but is flat.
4. **Leverage risk is real in hardware-heavy transit platforms.** Cubic (Veritas/Elliott-Evergreen, USD 2.8–3.0bn EV in 2021) completed a creditor-backed recap in July 2025 (USD 275m new liquidity, USD 170m sponsor equity), deferred an interest payment in January 2026, and was back in talks for new money in July 2026 with ~USD 2.1bn of debt and some loans quoted near 10 cents.
5. **Venture marks peaked in 2021–22 and have been quiet since.** Optibus became the first public-transport unicorn at USD 1.3bn (May 2022, USD 100m Series D); Hayden AI raised USD 90m (July 2024, Rise Fund/TPG) at a reported ~USD 400m post-money; Spare raised C$42m (Sept 2024); Swiftly took a Cove Hill-led strategic round (May 2025, undisclosed). No new unicorn rounds in transit tech were found for 2025–26; the exits have been to strategics (Clever Devices) or via IPO (Via, Sept 2025, ~USD 3.65bn).
6. **Fare collection is being carved out of diversified groups and re-platformed.** Conduent sold transit fare + fleet management to Modaxo (now "Novvan" and "Routeward") and kept tolling; Flowbird was absorbed by EasyPark (closed Jan 2025, rebranded "Arrive" June 2025); Genfare remained inside SPX Technologies as of the latest source found (2022).
7. **Implication for a new entrant / sponsor:** entry via a software-led, asset-light platform (scheduling, CAD/AVL, passenger information, enforcement analytics) at 5–8x revenue is the realistic path; hardware-heavy integrator roll-ups carry Cubic-style leverage risk; exit buyers are a short list of strategics (Hitachi Rail, Alstom, Siemens Mobility, Modaxo/CSU, Via, Wabtec for rail) plus EasyPark/Arrive and Verra for adjacent payments/enforcement.
