## 1. Key takeaways

1. **Three distinct capital theses coexist in this market, and they price assets very differently.** (a) *Permanent-capital roll-up*: Modaxo (Constellation Software) buys "mission-critical" people-transportation software of any size, promises a "forever home" and "never sells"; it bought Conduent's $371m-revenue transit unit for $164m (≈0.44x revenue, derived) and now spans 33+ brands, 3,500+ customers and 41+ countries. (b) *Strategic platform extension*: Hitachi Rail paid €1,660m for Thales GTS to move "the majority of its revenues" to "higher-return signalling and systems" (~60%), then bought Clever Devices (projected 2026 revenue >$220m) to "broaden our scope beyond rail" and feed its HMAX Mobility AI/asset-management suite; Siemens folded Hacon (2017) and Sqills (2021) into a "Mobility Software Suite X"; Alstom's equity story targets "35% of installed base under services contract" and describes signalling as a market "consolidated around 3 global players" (down from 8 in 2010). (c) *Growth/venture capital into SaaS wedges*: Insight/Bessemer/Pitango made Optibus "the first unicorn dedicated to public transportation" ($1.3bn, May 2022); TPG's Rise Fund led Hayden AI's $90m Series C on an "accessibility, safety and sustainability" thesis; Cove Hill/JMI (Swiftly), Inovia (Spare) and Accel-KKR (Masabi) all cite agencies "underserved by rigid, stifling systems".
2. **The one leveraged take-private in scope is the cautionary tale.** Veritas Capital and Elliott's Evergreen took Cubic private in May 2021 for ~$3.0bn including debt (58% premium; "financed through a combination of equity and debt"), citing Veritas' "expertise in the government technology market". By October 2026 the trade press described Cubic Transportation Systems as "troubled", "losing major contracts" (Miami $42m to Masabi, Sacramento to Masabi, Vancouver at risk), with a "venture-capital-owned parent dealing with growing debt woes"; sibling file 10 records a July 2025 creditor recap ($275m liquidity, $170m sponsor equity), a January 2026 interest deferral and ~$2.1bn of debt in July 2026. Hardware-heavy, long-cycle integrators do not support LBO leverage.
3. **Via's S-1 is the only investor-grade public sizing of the opportunity, and it is a *services-inclusive* number.** Via claims a $545bn global "public transportation market" growing ~5% p.a., a $250bn TAM for "public transportation solution providers" in North America and Europe, and an $82bn SAM ($38bn NA + $44bn Western Europe) that deliberately *excludes* bus networks over 250 vehicles. These figures include operated service (microtransit, paratransit, school, NEMT) and are not comparable with Berg Insight's EUR 3.8bn (2025) bus/tram ITS market for Europe + North America; a CDD must reconcile scope before using either.
4. **Via's own numbers show what a venture-scale "platform" entry costs and yields:** revenue $434.3m in 2025 (+29%), gross margin 40%, GAAP net loss $96.4m, 821 customers (+23%, ~9% organic), platform net revenue retention >120%, ARR $543m (+27%) and adjusted EBITDA of –$3.4m in Q2-2026; IPO September 2025 at $46/share raising $493m ($3.65bn headline; $4.2bn fully diluted per Renaissance Capital). Management now talks of "targeted and selective acquisitions" (Downtowner, $40.7m, December 2025) and "opportunities to take over entire transit networks".
5. **The white space most consistently named by practitioners is the ageing CAD/AVL stack and the long tail of agencies it never reached well.** Swiftly cites a third-party survey in which "only 6% of agencies would strongly recommend their CAD/AVL system" and calls it "a category problem" rooted in 1990s architecture; its 2026 cloud CAD/AVL "requires less hardware" and coexists with legacy systems at 100+ agencies. Via's S-1 describes "fragmented technology systems with limited functional flexibility, aging infrastructure, and poor end-user experience" and targets fleets under 250 vehicles first. Modaxo's criteria say "big is beautiful. Small is too." TripSpark positions on "mid-sized urban, suburban" agencies; Via bought Downtowner to "penetrate the destination cities market"; Optibus signed Carahsoft as public-sector distributor to get "through procurement quicker".
6. **The incumbents' own strategy statements confirm a shift from product to long-dated service relationships.** INIT: "evolving from being a provider of technology towards becoming a service partner... transferring their services and operations to a technology platform provider... with contract terms of up to 20 years"; it rates "risk of new market entrants" **low** but "loss of tenders" **medium**, runs two Virginia plants for Buy America, and now sells "cyber defence" as a service line. Alstom: Services "#1", signalling "#3", mid-term ambition 35% of installed base under services contract; Greater Anglia 15-year extension ≈€947m. Hitachi: HMAX sensors on ~2,500 trains, on *all* new rolling stock from 2027, claiming 15% maintenance and 15% energy savings where "approximately 50% of railway costs are attributed to maintenance and energy".
7. **Open architecture and vendor lock-in have become a regulator-level theme in rail and a procurement-scoring theme in bus.** The European Commission's October 2026 study says "fragmented deployment practices... are the root cause of high ERTMS deployment costs", that "vendor lock-in situations can favour incumbent suppliers even more, and limit competition", and that only ~10% of the TEN-T network is ERTMS-equipped against a €75–85bn completion cost. In Sacramento's 2025 SaaS ticketing evaluation, evaluators "credited Masabi with an ability to integrate with other vendors... while expressing doubts about Cubic's willingness to do so". Luminator's September 2026 repositioning from "hardware supplier" to "digital ecosystem partner" explicitly rests on "open standards for smooth interoperability".
8. **AI enforcement is the fastest-scaling new category and the clearest example of a hardware+software+services bundle with a self-funding business case.** Hayden AI grew from a $53m Series B (Drawdown Fund) to a $90m Series C (TPG Rise, July 2024) and reports 2,500+ equipped transit vehicles, UK VCA type approval, pilots in Barcelona, Braga, Gdańsk and Tallinn and an NEC Australia partnership; the MTA attributes 5% faster buses and 20% fewer collisions to it. Execution risk is visible: two CEO changes in two years (Marty Beard appointed, then stepped down July 2026; Jeffrey Tarr as Executive Chair/PEO while a search runs).
9. **Sponsors named in the brief as "active in transportation tech" are mostly *not* in public-transit ITS.** Thoma Bravo, Vista and Hg portfolio pages (fetched) list no transit/rail technology holdings; Blackstone, KKR, EQT, Partners Group, Francisco Partners, Oak Hill and Battery Ventures pages could not be fetched or showed none. The verified sponsor positions are: Accel-KKR (Masabi, growth, 2022), Cove Hill + JMI (Swiftly, 2025), Inovia/Kensington/Nicola (Spare, 2024), TPG Rise/Drawdown/Autotech (Hayden AI), Insight/Bessemer/Pitango/SOMV/Tencent/Verizon Ventures (Optibus), BBH Capital Partners (Clever Devices minority 2021 → Hitachi 2026), Veritas/Elliott (Cubic), Genstar via Clarience (Safe Fleet, 2024, ex-Oak Hill) and SPX Technologies (Genfare; no Battery Ventures link found). Audax's portfolio page no longer lists Luminator (possible exit; Gemspring link unconfirmed).
10. **Exit routes are a short list of strategics plus one listed comp.** Disclosed 2024–26 exits went to Hitachi (Clever), Modaxo (Conduent transit, SISCOG, FARA, Cammax, Fiscaltech), Siemens (MERMEC businesses), EasyPark/Arrive (Flowbird) and the public market (Via). Modaxo's "we never sell" policy structurally removes a growing share of installed base from any sponsor's future target list, while Alstom's November 2026 profit warning (adjusted EBIT ≈(4)% H1, FY margin ≈1%, FCF ≈–€1.5bn, "deleveraging objective of around €2 billion") is the first plausible source of a scaled carve-out from a big-three signalling house.
11. **Implied entry thesis for a new entrant / sponsor (synthesis, see §5):** enter asset-light through a software or AI wedge that *coexists* with the incumbent stack (real-time/analytics, scheduling, demand-response, enforcement, charge management, cyber/lifecycle services), sell to the under-250-vehicle tier and to contracted operators where procurement is lighter, use cooperative/distributor channels (Carahsoft, state contracts), and plan the exit to a strategic or to Modaxo-style permanent capital. Avoid leveraged acquisition of hardware-heavy integrators and avoid rail signalling cores (SIL-4, three-player market, 30-year frameworks); rail adjacencies (asset condition monitoring, cyber, retrofit integration) are contestable.

**Build vs buy (synthesis).** *Build* has worked for cloud-native SaaS with a coexistence wedge (Swiftly, Optibus, Spare) because open data (GTFS/GTFS-RT) and overlay architectures let them sell before the incumbent's contract ends. *Buy* has worked for permanent capital at low multiples (Modaxo) and for strategics with an installed base to cross-sell into (Hitachi, Siemens). *Buy with leverage* failed (Cubic). *Buy-and-build by a sponsor* in core bus ITS has no completed example in this window — the sponsor deals are growth minorities (Clever/BBH, Swiftly/Cove Hill, Masabi/Accel-KKR), which exited or will exit to strategics.

## 5. Implied investment thesis for a potential new entrant (synthesis)

**Where to play.** (1) Software overlays on legacy CAD/AVL (real-time accuracy, dispatch workflow, NTD reporting) sold to the 250–1,000-vehicle tier and to contracted operators; (2) demand-response/paratransit and low-density DRT, where Via's own SAM logic and Spare's traction show open demand; (3) enforcement and safety-video bundles with a measurable, often self-funding outcome, gated by enabling legislation; (4) ZEB charge/energy-aware scheduling, a mandatory new layer neither CAD/AVL incumbents nor charger OEMs own; (5) rail adjacencies — infrastructure condition monitoring, cyber lifecycle services, onboard retrofit integration — not signalling cores.

**How to win.** Coexist first, replace at renewal (Swiftly recipe); thin onboard hardware and over-the-air delivery; open interfaces (ITxPT, GTFS-RT, SIRI) as a procurement advantage, which evaluators now score (Sacramento); cooperative/distributor channels for the long tail; Buy America-compliant hardware partner or local assembly if any hardware is in scope (INIT's two Virginia plants are the benchmark); cyber certifications (ISO 27001/SOC 2, IEC 62443 in rail) as bid prerequisites.

**What we would need to believe.** (a) Agency technology budgets are protected relative to capital programmes (Via: RFP flow "pretty consistent" despite federal cuts; sibling 06 on formula-heavy reauthorisation); (b) the 15–25-year CAD/AVL replacement wave of 2000s-era systems (TfL iBus, LA Metro ATMS, sibling 11) is real and winnable by non-incumbents; (c) net revenue retention >120% is achievable in public-sector SaaS (Via evidence); (d) exits exist at 5–10x revenue to strategics (Hitachi/Clever, Via IPO) rather than at Modaxo floor prices.

**What to avoid.** Leveraged acquisition of hardware-heavy integrators (Cubic); competing head-on for Tier-1 enterprise CAD/AVL rebids without a local reference (INIT rates entrant risk "low" for a reason); rail signalling/ATO cores (three-player market, SIL-4, 30-year frameworks); relying on US federal discretionary grants for demand timing.

**Key diligence questions for any target.** Share of revenue under multi-year service/hosting contracts and their remaining term; coexistence/integration references with each major incumbent; hardware content and Buy America exposure; cyber certification status; win/loss on last 20 bids by agency size; dependence on state enabling laws (enforcement) or mandates (ZEB); customer concentration in top-10 agencies.

## 7. Investor / acquirer profiles (one-liners)

- **Modaxo (Constellation Software, TSX: CSU)** — permanent-capital VMS roll-up; 33+ brands incl. Trapeze, Vontas, TripSpark/Routematch, TransLoc, Novvan, Routeward, SISCOG, FARA; buys founder exits and carve-outs; "never sells".
- **Hitachi Rail (Hitachi, Ltd.)** — >€7bn turnover, 24,000 staff; signalling-led after GTS; now owns NA bus-ITS leader Clever Devices; HMAX Mobility as the integrating AI/asset platform; investing in NA capacity.
- **Alstom** — Services #1 / Signalling #3; signalling ~€17bn addressable at ~4% CAGR; 35% installed-base services ambition; H1 2026/27 profit warning and €2bn deleveraging objective — watch for disposals.
- **Siemens Mobility** — €12.4bn, 8.8% margin, #1 rail infrastructure; software via Hacon, Sqills, eos.uptrade, Padam under "Mobility Software Suite X"; MERMEC businesses added 2026 (sibling).
- **Wabtec** — freight-weighted; digital/inspection M&A at ~15–16x EBITDA (sibling).
- **Veritas Capital / Elliott (Evergreen)** — government-technology thesis on Cubic (2021, ~$3.0bn); now a restructuring and turnaround story.
- **Accel-KKR** — growth investor in Masabi (fare SaaS); thesis: agencies underserved by monolithic AFC.
- **Cove Hill / JMI** — long-duration growth capital in Swiftly (transit data platform → CAD/AVL).
- **Inovia / Kensington / Nicola** — Spare (paratransit/DRT SaaS; aging-population thesis).
- **TPG Rise / Drawdown / Autotech** — Hayden AI (impact thesis: accessibility, safety, sustainability; enforcement economics).
- **Insight / Bessemer / Pitango / SOMV / Tencent / Verizon Ventures** — Optibus (end-to-end planning/ops platform; $1.3bn, 2022).
- **BBH Capital Partners** — Clever Devices minority (2021) → Hitachi (2026).
- **Genstar (Clarience)** — Safe Fleet onboard video (2024, sibling).
- **SPX Technologies** — Genfare (fare hardware + Genfare Link).
- **Via Transportation (NYSE: VIA)** — the listed platform comp; $434m revenue, 40% GM, ARR $543m, GAAP loss; acquisitive.
- **INIT SE (XETRA: IXX)** — organic incumbent; 20-year service contracts; rates new-entrant risk low.
