## 1. Key takeaways

1. **Procurement is slow, formal and legally exposed, which protects incumbents and penalizes under-capitalized entrants.** Federal grant rules (2 CFR 200.318-.324) require documented procedures, full and open competition above the simplified-acquisition threshold (sealed bids or competitive proposals), cost/price analysis and an independent cost estimate before bids, and make the agency responsible for settling protests. The best-documented recent CAD/AVL replacement (LA Metro ATMS II) ran from a 2016 strategic plan to a June 2025 board award of **$129.8M (4-year contract, Clever Devices)**, with base and phase 1-2 functionality not due until **July 2028**, plus a separate **$10.8M** program-support contract because, in the agency's own words, without it Metro "would face significant risks of delays, operational disruptions, and cost overruns". Awards are made "subject to the resolution of any properly submitted protest(s)".
2. **System lives are 15-25 years, so the addressable rebid pool each year is small and switching is a multi-year capital project.** LA Metro's ATMS I was awarded in 2001, live in 2004 and still in service in 2025 ("over 25 years old... software and hardware no longer in production or supported"); TfL's Siemens iBus (rolled out 2007-09) is only now being replaced under a **10-year, £160M** INIT contract signed in 2024; MTA's first AVL vendor (Orbital, 1996) missed most deadlines and was terminated in 2000, a 2005 **$13M** Siemens countdown-clock system was removed in 2012, and Clever Devices has been the onboard supplier since ~2010. INIT reports customer contracts "of up to 20 years" and says follow-up contracts are increasingly awarded. Rail signalling is slower still: TTC's Line 1 ATC (Alstom CBTC) was announced in 2007 as a five-year project, cost **C$562.3M**, was reported in 2019 as three years late and **C$98M over budget**, and went fully live in September 2022.
3. **Incumbents usually keep accounts within a contract life but do lose at end-of-life rebids.** Of three large, well-documented CAD/AVL replacements, two switched vendor (LA Metro from the Orbital/Xerox lineage to Clever Devices; TfL from Siemens to INIT) and one expanded the incumbent (SacRT extending Clever Devices from bus to rail). INIT's 2025 risk report rates "loss of tenders" a medium risk, "risk of new market entrants" **low**, and notes its win/loss ratio "improved slightly"; its North American order intake was "largely due to follow-on orders from MARTA and stable aftermarket business with existing customers". Clever Devices (now Hitachi Rail) serves **8 of the 10 largest North American agencies** and 300+ agencies; Modaxo (Trapeze and 30+ other brands) claims **3,500+ customers** and "never sells" businesses. This is a market where entry is typically by acquisition or by a wedge product sold alongside, not instead of, the incumbent CAD/AVL.
4. **Buy America / BABA is a hardware barrier, not a software one, and enforcement scrutiny is rising.** Statute: rolling stock (including "train control, communication, and traction power equipment") needs **>70% domestic component cost (FY2020+) and US final assembly**; other manufactured end products need 100% domestic components unless waived (public interest, non-availability, or price differential >25%). FTA's regulation lists "computers; information systems; security systems; data processing systems; fare collection systems" and "a communication... system" as representative end products subject to Buy America. BABA (2 CFR 184) adds a **55% domestic-content** test for manufactured products on infrastructure projects. A July 2024 DOT OIG audit of SEPTA's $138M CRRC railcar contract found FTA guidance gaps (no requirement to retain pre-award audit evidence or verify supplier certifications), **$30.8M in questioned costs** and up to **$35.5M** that could be put to better use. INIT runs two production firms in Chesapeake, VA "to improve its sales chances under Buy America"; a hardware-led entrant must do the same or partner.
5. **Cybersecurity is now a compliance cost line and an operational-risk headline.** TSA's November 2024 NPRM would impose a full Cyber Risk Management program on **34 of ~92** US public transportation agencies and passenger railroads (those with >=50,000 average daily unlinked trips for rail transit; >=5,000 for passenger railroads, or hosting Amtrak/Class I) plus 73 freight railroads and 115 pipelines, at an estimated **$3.09B undiscounted over 10 years ($2.16-2.63B discounted; ~$308M/yr annualized)**, of which **$1.26B** falls on the PTPR industry (~$3.7M per covered agency per year undiscounted, our arithmetic). Security Directives SD 1582-21-01 and SD 1580/82-2022-01 have been renewed annually since December 2021 and were still being extended in 2026 (see 06). Incident evidence: TfL (Sept 2024) — 148 systems taken offline, services disrupted for months, ~10M customers' data stolen, **£29M financial impact plus ~£10M lost income**; Pittsburgh Regional Transit (Dec 19 2024) — ransomware delayed light rail ~20 minutes, took down customer service and reduced-fare card processing, and exposed employee SSNs; TTC (Oct 29 2021) — ransomware knocked out the Clever Devices "Vision" CAD/AVL system (fallback to radio), next-vehicle information, Wheel-Trans booking and email, and exposed up to 25,000 employees' data; SEPTA (2020) lost real-time information for two weeks. The UK DfT's March 2026 guidance now writes IEC 62443 / TS 50701 security levels and mandatory penetration testing into rolling-stock tenders.
6. **The customer base is a steep power law.** NTD 2025 lists **2,897 reporters** (520 full, 643 reduced, 429 reduced-asset, 1,208 rural, 54 state, 26 group-plan sponsors). Of 2,692 agencies with vehicle records, **13 agencies with >=1,000 bus-type vehicles hold 22.6%** of the 99,261 bus/articulated/cutaway vehicles, **74 agencies with >=250 hold 51.2%**, while **1,641 agencies with 1-24 vehicles hold 13.3%**. Enterprise CAD/AVL economics therefore depend on roughly 70-90 accounts; the long tail is only addressable with low-touch SaaS (Swiftly: 200+ agencies in 10 countries) and through state/cooperative purchasing.
7. **Standards and safety certification set the pace in rail and are becoming contractual in bus.** ETCS Baseline 4 was published 8 September 2023 (CCS TSI 2023/1695, amended by Reg. 2026/693); ERA trackside approval must be sought **before any call for tenders** launched after 16 June 2019 (ERA charges **€130/hour**); vehicle (type) authorisation decisions are due within **4 months** of a complete file after a 1-month completeness check; moving a fleet to Baseline 3 requires onboard re-certification of "at least €100k per vehicle" (Wikipedia, low confidence). US PTC needed FRA Type Approval, a PTC Safety Plan and System Certification with independent V&V (49 CFR 236 Subpart I); the statutory deadline slipped from 2015 to 2018 to 2020, only 4 railroads met the 2018 date, and commuter-railroad cost exceeded $2B. On the bus side, TfL specified ITxPT compliance for iBus2, SIRI (CEN) and GTFS/GTFS-RT (6,000+ feeds in 100+ countries; NTD now collects agencies' GTFS links) are de-facto interface requirements, and FTA grantees running rail must certify a cybersecurity process for hardware/software under 49 U.S.C. 5323(v).
8. **Fiscal, political and tariff risk is real but mostly affects timing and hardware margins rather than the existence of demand.** (See 06 for the federal cliff: IIJA advance appropriations lapsed, FTA staff down ~30%, selected grants pulled, state rescue packages.) Local example: PRT projected a **$100M FY2026 deficit** and proposed cutting 40 routes. US tariff policy whipsawed in 2025-26: China IEEPA tariffs peaked at **145%** (April 2025), fell to 30% (May 2025) and the fentanyl component to 10% (Nov 2025); Section 232 steel/aluminum rose to **50%** (June 2025) with 407 derivative products added (Aug 2025) and an April 2026 schedule of 25% on derivatives / 15% on electrical-grid equipment; the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026 (~$166B to be refunded) and a 10% Section 122 tariff ran to July 24 2026. INIT attributed its 2025 margin miss partly to US tariffs. USDOT suspended DBE goals and enforcement effective October 3 2025 (LA Metro board report), one of several rule changes mid-procurement.
9. **Supplier failure is a live counterparty risk for agencies and raises the bar for new entrants' balance sheets.** Proterra (first US e-bus OEM) filed Chapter 11 on 7 August 2023 citing supply-chain penalties, "highly customized products for each transit agency" and inflation; Eno's NTD analysis shows it built 91 buses for 17 customers in 2020 (~5 per customer) vs New Flyer's ~15, with payment only after delivery and testing ~18 months after capital is committed. Nova Bus exited the US after 2025; BYD was barred from FTA-funded purchases under the 2019 Chinese-rolling-stock ban. Agencies have internalized this: tenders now weigh financial capacity, long-term support obligations (INIT: operation and maintenance for 5-10 years as standard scope) and escrow/continuity terms.
10. **Agency capacity is the hidden constraint.** APTA's February 2022 survey of 117 agencies found 71% had cut or delayed service for lack of workers and >90% had hiring difficulty; INIT cites a shortage of control-centre staff; LA Metro needed $10.8M of outside program support to run its CAD/AVL replacement; DfT tells UK authorities to buy in cyber expertise where internal capability is limited. For an entrant this means long sales cycles, heavy pre-sales and implementation services, and a premium on turnkey/hosted delivery.

## 3. Risk-by-risk narrative (for the "Risks & barriers" slide)

### 3.1 Public procurement
- **Rules.** Grant-funded agencies must use documented procedures, compete formally above the simplified-acquisition threshold, perform cost/price analysis and an independent estimate, and resolve protests themselves (rows 1-3). Rolling-stock option terms are capped at 5 years (bus) and 7 years (rail) and cooperative ("piggyback") procurement is expressly authorized (row 4). ITS contracts have no statutory cap and in practice run from 4-year implementation deals (LA Metro) to 10-year platform deals (TfL/INIT) and "up to 20 years" (INIT), typically with multi-year O&M attached (INIT Grenoble: 10 years of maintenance; Luxembourg GloTel: hardware for 1,700 buses plus 5 years O&M).
- **Timelines.** The LA Metro case is the clearest fully documented cycle: needs assessment and strategic plan in 2016, capital project, scope development, RFP, board award June 2025, base scope plus two phases due July 2028 (12 years from plan to full operation; rows 6-7). TTC's bus CAD/AVL took ~3 years from contract to full fleet (row 16); its rail ATC took 15 years from funding to full operation (rows 17-19).
- **Past performance / prequalification.** Board reports and vendor releases emphasise "proven experience deploying large-scale... systems" (SacRT) and the agency's confidence in a known supplier; INIT lists "loss of tenders due to technological or commercial reasons" as a standing medium risk (row 20). Together with 15-25-year system lives, this makes references the binding constraint for a new entrant.

### 3.2 Buy America / BABA
- Hardware sold into FTA-funded procurements must either be rolling stock-type equipment (>70% domestic components, US final assembly) or a manufactured end product with 100% domestic components unless waived; FTA's end-product list explicitly covers communication systems, computers, information systems, security systems and data-processing systems (rows 23, 27-28). BABA adds the 55% test on infrastructure projects (row 29). The OIG's 2024 SEPTA/CRRC audit is the most recent signal that documentation and supplier verification will be scrutinised (row 30). Vendors respond with US assembly (INIT Chesapeake, row 31). Software and services are not enumerated as end products; the practical implication is that a software-only entrant faces little Buy America friction while an onboard-hardware entrant must localise assembly or partner.

### 3.3 Cybersecurity
- **Requirements.** TSA SDs since Dec 2021 (coordinator, CISA reporting, vulnerability assessment, incident response plan, then performance-based mitigation) renewed annually (rows 37-39); the pending NPRM would make this a permanent CRM program for the 34 largest PTPR entities at ~$1.26B over 10 years (rows 33-36). FTA grantees running rail must certify a hardware/software third-party testing process (row 26). In the UK, DfT now requires IEC 62443 security levels, TS 50701 and penetration testing in rolling-stock tenders (row 40). For vendors this means security questionnaires, SBOMs, pen-test evidence and incident-reporting SLAs in every RFP, and an advantage for hosted platforms with existing attestations.
- **Incidents and cost.** TfL: £29M impact + £10M lost income, 148 systems offline, months of disruption, ~10M records (rows 41-42). PRT: ransomware affecting light-rail dispatch timing and customer systems, employee SSNs exposed (rows 43-44). TTC: CAD/AVL and paratransit booking lost, 25,000 records (rows 46-47). SEPTA 2020: two weeks without real-time information (row 45). A Hitachi group company (GlobalLogic) was caught in the 2025 Clop/Oracle campaign (row 48). The CAD/AVL and RTPI stack is the operational system most visibly disrupted in these cases, which raises both the compliance burden and the value proposition for resilient, segmented, cloud-hosted designs.

### 3.4 Budget uncertainty
- Covered in detail in file 06 (federal cliff, FTA staffing, grant withdrawals, state rescues). Local evidence here: PRT's $100M FY2026 deficit and proposed 40-route cut (rows 52-53). LA Metro's ATMS II was funded from state TDA funds rather than federal grants (row 56), an example of large agencies de-risking technology programs from federal timing. Mid-procurement policy changes (DBE suspension, row 55) add administrative churn.

### 3.5 Legacy integration and switching costs
- Evidence of long lives and painful replacements: LA Metro (2004 system still in use in 2025; unsupported hardware/software; 2015 sole-source integration to the incumbent), MTA (two failed or abandoned systems before Bus Time), TfL (iBus 2007-09 replaced only from 2024), TTC ATC (schedule and budget slippage; "no going back" once activated). Ransomware events show that CAD/AVL is now a single point of failure with manual fallbacks (TTC). Replacement scope is broad (radios, routers, APCs, cameras, signage, yard management, EV charge management at LA Metro), which favours integrators with multi-domain portfolios and makes point-solution entry hard unless via open interfaces.

### 3.6 Standards and safety certification
- **Rail:** ETCS baselines, ERA trackside pre-tender approval and vehicle authorisation timelines, SIL-based EN standards, FRA PTC Type Approval/System Certification (rows 57-64). Lead times are set by regulators and notified bodies, not by vendors; baseline migrations impose per-vehicle re-certification cost.
- **Bus:** ITxPT, SIRI, GTFS/GTFS-RT and NTCIP (rows 65-69) are increasingly written into tenders (TfL/ITxPT). Open standards lower integration barriers for new entrants but also commoditise interfaces, so differentiation shifts to analytics, operations tooling and hosting.

### 3.7 Vendor concentration and incumbency
- Clever Devices (8 of top-10 NA agencies; 300+ agencies; now Hitachi Rail), INIT (1,400+ operators; NA its largest region), Modaxo/Trapeze (3,500+ customers; permanent holder) dominate enterprise CAD/AVL; Swiftly shows SaaS can reach 200+ agencies (rows 70-74). Rebids do change hands (TfL, LA Metro) but typically only every 15-25 years, and incumbents win cross-sell (SacRT, MARTA follow-ons).

### 3.8 Long tail of small agencies
- 2,897 NTD reporters, of which 1,208 rural and 1,072 reduced/reduced-asset; 74 agencies hold 51% of bus vehicles; 1,641 agencies have fewer than 25 (rows 75-78). Federal cyber mandates cover only 34 PTPR entities (row 79). The long tail buys via cooperative contracts, state DOT programs and SaaS, with micro-purchase/simplified-acquisition thresholds permitting informal buys at small ticket sizes (row 1).

### 3.9 Agency staffing and skills
- Rows 80-82: operator shortages (71% of agencies cut/delayed service in 2022), control-centre staffing shortages (INIT), and reliance on consultants for program management (LA Metro $10.8M). Entrants must budget for heavy implementation services and training.

### 3.10 Tariffs
- Rows 83-87: 2025-26 tariff volatility on China-origin electronics and on steel/aluminum/copper content (enclosures, poles, displays), with legal reversal in 2026; INIT's margin and inventory commentary shows the direct P&L effect on hardware-heavy vendors. Fixed-price, multi-year hardware contracts carry the risk unless indexed.

### 3.11 Political risk
- Chinese rolling-stock ban (row 25), DBE suspension (row 55), grant-condition litigation and FTA staffing (see 06). Agencies increasingly fund core technology from state/local sources (row 56) and re-label projects to fit federal priorities.

### 3.12 Ridership and fiscal risk
- See 06 (ridership ~81-85% of 2019; farebox recovery structurally lower; state rescue packages). Local example: PRT (rows 52-53).

### 3.13 Supplier failures
- Proterra, Nova Bus, Orbital (MTA 2000), EasyMile (headline) (rows 14, 88-92). Agencies now test financial capacity and continuity; entrants need balance-sheet strength or a strong parent (Hitachi/Clever, Constellation/Modaxo, Volvo/Nova as a cautionary case).
