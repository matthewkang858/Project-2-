# 14. Transit operator capital plans and technology spend evidence

**Topic:** Bottom-up evidence of ITS / technology spending from transit-operator budgets, capital programs and national statistics, to anchor the Public ITS (Roadway-bus and Railway) market sizing.
**Date compiled:** 2026-11-07. **Author:** research agent (CDD support).
**Method note:** The WebSearch budget for this run was exhausted before this topic started, and the sandbox DNS path used by WebFetch does not resolve most agency hosts. All evidence below was therefore pulled by direct `curl` of primary documents (PDF/CSV/HTML) through the session proxy, converted locally (pdftotext / pandas) and read. Hosts that were blocked at the proxy (403): apta.com, transit.dot.gov, tfl.gov.uk, sncf-reseau.com, ratp.fr, bahn.de, transport.nsw.gov.au, sec.gov, bts.gov. Every number below comes from a file or page that was actually opened; URLs are given per row. Currency as stated in source; "CI" = NTD "Communication & Information Systems" capital-use category.

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The US federal statistics give a hard floor for ITS-type capex: FTA NTD "Communication & Information Systems" (CI) capital expense was $1.90B in RY2025 (5.9% of $32.35B total transit capex) and averaged $2.11B/yr over 2022-2025 (7.3% of capex).** CI was $2.11B (2022), $2.17B (2023), $2.24B (2024) and $1.90B (2025). Rail took 78-80% of CI every year (2025: rail $1.49B, bus $0.37B, demand-response $0.02B). 92% of 2025 CI was state-of-good-repair/replacement, only $144M (8%) expansion. Adding NTD "fare collection equipment" ($521M, 1.6%) gives a combined "systems" pool of ~$2.4B (7.5%) in 2025 and ~$2.5B/yr (8.7%) on the 4-year average. Source: data.transportation.gov dataset fphd-jyyj (NTD definition of CI explicitly includes two-way radio, cab signaling and train control, AVL, automated dispatching, PA, and operations software such as scheduling/planning/maintenance).
2. **Long-run NTD time series (TS 3.1) shows the "Other" capital-use bucket (fare collection + CI + other) has been flat at ~$2.6-3.4B/yr since 2015 while total capex grew from $19.5B (2015) to $32.4B (2025).** Rolling stock jumped from $5.2B (2022) to $9.5B (2025) - the ZEB/railcar wave is absorbing agency capital, so the ITS share of capex fell from 7.9% (2022-23) to 5.9% (2025). This is the structural replacement for the unavailable APTA Fact Book capital-expense split (rolling stock / facilities / other).
3. **ITS capex is extremely concentrated.** The top 10 NTD agencies account for 64.5% of 4-year CI spend and the top 25 for 81.2%; only 328 of 2,221 capex-reporting agencies recorded any CI capex in 2025. By agency size tier (4-yr avg, 2022-25): agencies with 1,000+ vehicles (29 agencies) spent $1.73B/yr on CI (7.6% of their capex, $29.2k per vehicle-in-max-service); 250-999 VOMS (94 agencies) $664M (7.0%, $15.2k/VOMS); 100-249 VOMS (127) $100M (5.8%, $5.2k); 25-99 VOMS (718) $196M (6.0%, $6.1k); <25 VOMS (2,128) $53M (3.3%, $2.6k). Bus-only agencies (2,983 of them) spent just $268M/yr in total, $2,626 per VOMS, 4.6% of their capex.
4. **Implied unit intensities (derived from NTD, 2025 fleet/track denominators):** rail CI capex ~ $64k per active rail car (2025) / $73k (4-yr avg) and ~$109k per track-mile (~$68k per track-km); bus CI capex ~ $5,760 per full-size bus (2025) / $5,860 (4-yr avg) across all agencies, but ranging from ~$0.5-3k per bus at CTA, SEPTA, NJ Transit, MBTA, SFMTA to $18-22k per bus at LA Metro and King County Metro (agencies in an active CAD/AVL-TSP-camera refresh). Rail intensities range from $4k per car (CTA, between signal programs) to $258k per car / $898k per track-mile at WMATA (radio, fiber, ATC component replacement running concurrently). Agency-level numbers are lumpy: NYCT CI fell from $569M (2024) to $322M (2025) as 2020-24 CBTC contracts wound down ahead of the 2025-29 awards.
5. **MTA is the single largest ITS buyer in North America.** The $68.4B 2025-2029 Capital Plan carries $6.907B of NYCT "Signals & Communications" ($5.43B signal modernization incl. $3.19B "CBTC: Various Lines", $1.44B "Additional CBTC Work", $183M CBTC on-board for rolling stock, $326M ATS-A replacement, $295M signal component replacement; plus $1.52B communication systems incl. $828M "Station Information Systems", $200M comm cable, $184M network upgrades, $100M electronic security), $505M LIRR and $598M Metro-North communications & signals, $600M next-generation turnstiles (fare, adjacent), $219M SCADA, $80M MIS, and only ~$22M explicitly labelled "Bus Technology". Keyword-defined ITS content is ~$8.7-10.1B = 12.8-14.8% of the plan; the comparable share was 12.7% of the $55.1B 2020-2024 plan ($6.29B signals & communication) and 12.9% of the $33.0B 2015-2019 plan. Commitments: $7.5B of 2025-29 committed by April 2026; MTA committed a record $15.8B across all plans in 2025 (NY State Comptroller). MTA C&D reports cutting CBTC project cost by one-third by reducing customised equipment - a pricing signal for incumbents.
6. **Other US agency plans confirm 5-25% ITS/technology shares depending on whether a signalling program is in flight:** WMATA FY2027-32 CIP $11.30B with Technology $606M (5.4%) plus Signaling System Components $211M, Radio ($609M project, $78M remaining), Communications $116M, Fare Systems $238M, Security Technology & Cameras $50M, Technology Software $306M / Equipment $136M, Back-up OCC $28M, and a Rail Modernization (CBTC) Program of $913M in FY27-32 / $1.62B long-range ($7.62B if the DMVMoves funding scenario is adopted). BART's 10-year CIP (FY25-34, $9.79B constrained) has Train Control & Communications at $2.195B (22%), of which Train Control Modernization (Hitachi CBTC) $1.95B; BART's FY26/FY27 capital budgets put TC&C at $196M/$269M (17%/35% of the year). MBTA FY27-31 CIP ($10.28B) has Technology & Innovation $654M (6.4%) plus signal/train-protection projects ($384M Red/Orange signals, $214M GLTPS2) and AFC 2.0 ($891M, adjacent). CTA sits at the other extreme: ~$90M of comms/IT/Train Tracker/security-comms in a $6.75B 5-year CIP (~1.3%), with a $0.9B unfunded comms/IT backlog. LA Metro's FY25 CIP has a $69.8M "Technology" line (12.5% of the $559M CIP; $370M life-of-project) plus $63M of rail wayside systems (A Line resignaling, digital rail radio, SCADA, CCTV).
7. **Operating-budget IT shares cluster at 4-5% of opex where disclosed:** WMATA Digital Modernization $126.6M in FY2027 = 4.8% of the $2.617B operating budget (up 33% since FY2024); CTA Technology department $85.0M = 3.8% of the $2.23B FY2026 baseline. TfL's "Technology" capital line is £62-68m/yr (3.3-3.6% of £1.9bn capex). MTA's statement of operations does not isolate IT, but its "Professional Services Contracts" ($785M) and "Maintenance & Other Operating Contracts" ($1,005M) pools (10% of $17.8B 2025 opex) are where SaaS and maintenance contracts sit.
8. **UK rail: Network Rail CP7 (Apr 2024-Mar 2029) is a £45.1bn plan with £18.7bn renewals & other capital; signalling renewals are £2.8bn (down from £3.1bn in the March 2024 plan) plus £0.7bn telecoms; ORR's PR23 determination priced CP7 signalling renewals at £3.55bn GB (18% of £19.7bn renewals; 4,801 signalling equivalent units in England & Wales, i.e. ~£675k per SEU).** The ETCS/digital-signalling portfolio has been cut, not grown: Network Rail-wide ETCS spend in CP7 is £994m in the March 2026 plan vs £1,544m in the March 2024 plan (ETCS signalling renewals £257m vs £545m; fleet fitment £535m vs £812m), with West Coast North Modernisation reprofiled and c.£1bn plan funding gap closed partly by £260m taken from the digital portfolio. (This corrects file 07, which has the two figures reversed.)
9. **Germany: DB spent ~€19bn on rail infrastructure in 2025 (gross group capex €21.9bn), of which "command and control technology" was €2.0bn (2024: €1.3bn) within €14.9bn existing-network capex, plus €0.2bn ETCS on Corridor A; 64 old interlockings were replaced in 2025 (target 50). The 2026 federal budget earmarks €21.9bn for rail infrastructure, including €2.5bn for digitalisation from the SVIK special fund, and ~€107bn is planned for federal tracks 2025-2029.** But DB's April-2024 ETCS migration strategy was withdrawn in summer 2025 because vehicle fitment was judged infeasible in the timeframe and because of "uncertainties regarding Government funding for the infrastructure" - the clearest primary-source evidence of the 2025 DSD stretch-out. The €6.3bn DSD volume contract (Mar 2025; Siemens Mobility/Leonhard Weiss, Alstom, Hitachi Rail, MerMec) with purchase obligations is the demand anchor for digital interlockings.
10. **Other operators:** TfL 2026/27 Budget: £1.87bn capex (Piccadilly line upgrade £347m, DLR trains £116m, Elizabeth line £110m, Technology £62m, renewals £800m) on £8.83bn operating costs; LTA Singapore FY2025 expenditure S$5.72bn, S$4.1bn of rail contracts awarded in the year (incl. Alstom/ST Engineering signalling + PSD and ST Engineering communications for the TEL extension), and a post-disruption Rail Reliability Taskforce prioritising renewal of power, signalling and trains; RATP Group invested €2.0bn in 2025 (95% Ile-de-France) and €848m in H1 2026; NSW 2025-26 capex: Transport for NSW A$7.47bn + Sydney Metro A$4.99bn. SNCF Reseau and Delhi Metro could not be reached (gaps).
11. **Implication for sizing:** the US public-transit ITS-addressable capex pool (NTD CI + fare) is ~$2.4-2.5B/yr with a 4-year mean CI of $2.1B; agency plans show the next five years are back-end loaded for rail signalling (MTA $8.0B signals & comms, WMATA RMP, BART CBTC, MBTA, SFMTA TCUP) while bus ITS is small per vehicle (~$6k/bus/yr) and concentrated in a few agencies with active CAD/AVL, TSP and camera programmes. Signalling renewals run at 13-18% of rail infrastructure-manager capex in the UK and Germany.

## 3. Agency-by-agency evidence notes

### 3.1 FTA NTD (United States) - what the data can and cannot do
- Dataset `2022-2025 NTD Annual Data - Capital Expenses (by Capital Use)` (id fphd-jyyj) reports, per agency/mode/type-of-service, capital spending in 10 use categories. The CI category definition (from the dataset's column metadata) covers two-way radio, cab signaling and train control, AVL, automated dispatching, vehicle guidance, telephones, PA, and computers and associated software supporting operations (scheduling, planning, maintenance). It is capital-only: SaaS subscriptions, maintenance contracts and hosted services funded from operating budgets are not captured (they sit in NTD "services" opex). It also includes non-ITS IT (office/enterprise systems).
- Only Full Reporters itemise capital uses; Reduced/Rural reporters post a single "reduced reporter" total ($846M in 2025), so CI for small agencies is understated.
- Time Series 3.1 (id 8ffu-2anj) gives Rolling Stock / Facilities / Other splits back to 2015 and is the practical substitute for APTA's Fact Book capital-expense tables (apta.com and transit.dot.gov were blocked in this session).
- Agency names are not perfectly normalised (e.g., WMATA, BART, LA Metro, TriMet each appear under two strings); the table above sums where obvious.

### 3.2 MTA (New York)
- The 2025-2029 plan project list on data.ny.gov (6ifx-7aaq) reconciles exactly to $68.4B. Signals & communications (NYCT $6.907B + LIRR $0.505B + MNR $0.598B = $8.01B) is 11.7% of the plan; with fare ($600M turnstiles), SCADA, MIS and bus technology, ITS-type content is $8.7-10.1B depending on definition.
- NYCT's bus category ($3.29B) is almost entirely vehicles; explicit "Bus Technology" is $12M (NYCT) + $10M (MTA Bus) - bus ITS at MTA is embedded in bus procurements and operating contracts rather than visible as capital lines.
- The capital dashboard (ehz8-ag3n) shows the signal/communication share has been stable at ~12.7-12.9% across the 2015-19, 2020-24 and 2025-29 plans, i.e. MTA ITS demand scales with the overall plan size (which rose from $33.0B to $55.1B to $68.4B current budgets).
- Actual spend timing: NTD NYCT CI was $543M / $581M / $569M / $322M in 2022-25 - the 2025 dip reflects the gap between 2020-24 CBTC contract completion and 2025-29 awards. The MTA Capital Project Budgets dataset (f6fd-xfps) only covers a dashboard subset (186 projects, $10.8B of the 2020-24 plan) and is not usable for a full category-level spend history.
- Operating side: 2025 non-reimbursable expenses $17.8B; no IT line. Professional services ($785M) and maintenance/other operating contracts ($1,005M) are the pools where ITS SaaS/maintenance sits.

### 3.3 WMATA (Washington)
- FY2027-32 approved CIP of $11.3B (investments) with a separate "Technology" mode of $606M (5.4%); however signalling components ($211M), radio ($609M project, nearly complete), communications ($116M), security tech & cameras ($50M), fare systems ($238M) and the Rail Modernization Program ($913M in-window) sit under Rail/Bus/Business Operations. The DMVMoves regional funding scenario would lift six-year investments to $15.85B and the long-range RMP to $7.6B - a clear example of funding-contingent ITS demand.
- Digital Modernization operating budget: $126.6M in FY2027, up 33% from FY2024 actual ($95.5M); 4.8% of the net operating budget.

### 3.4 BART (San Francisco Bay Area)
- Train Control & Communications is the largest program in the 10-year CIP ($2.195B, 22%), driven by the Hitachi CBTC contract ($1.95B). FY26/27 capital budgets show TC&C at $196M/$269M; the FY26-27 xlsx lists 24 TC&C projects (CBTC design-build, deployment, enabling works, SCADA PLC replacement, SORS/MUX, wayside antenna, non-vital relays, network hardening). Unfunded needs include $531M train-control rehab and $572M communications/computer rehab.

### 3.5 LA Metro
- FY25 CIP: Technology subtotal $69.8M (12.5% of the $559M CIP; $370M life-of-project) and Wayside Systems $63.2M (A Line resignaling rehabilitation $118M LOP; digital rail radio $25M LOP; SCADA; train-to-wayside comms; CCTV upgrade $15.6M LOP). Bus-side ITS is visible as TSP ($25.6M LOP), bus mobile validators ($18.1M LOP), camera bus-lane enforcement ($11M LOP), ATMS II replacement. NTD shows LA Metro as the largest bus CI spender ($43.7M/yr, ~$22k per bus).

### 3.6 CTA (Chicago)
- FY2026-2030 CIP $6.75B is dominated by RPM/RLE bond repayment ($978M), ASAP stations and rolling stock; comms/IT-type lines total ~$91M (fiber optics $14.9M, IT $6.4M, Train Tracker signage $15.0M, security & communications projects $55.2M). CTA states a $0.9B unfunded Comms/IT need within $30.9B unfunded. Technology department opex $85.0M (3.8% of $2.23B). Fare: Ventra 3.0 pilot (open architecture), new fareboxes on ~2,000 buses in 2025.

### 3.7 MBTA (Boston)
- FY27-31 CIP $10.28B; Technology & Innovation $654M (6.4%), led by AFC 2.0 ($423M in-window; adjacent fare), systemwide radio ($113M), PA/ESS ($32M), fare back-office migration ($11M), CTC train control replacement ($10M), dispatch/tracking tools, regional-rail APCs ($15M total), PTIS, bus and LRV cameras. Signal work sits under Guideway, Signal & Power ($1.44B): Red/Orange signal program ($384M total), GLTPS2 ($214M total).

### 3.8 NJ Transit, King County Metro, SEPTA, SFMTA
- NJT: FY2026 capital appropriation $1.688B; >$75M fare technology; NTD CI $35-51M/yr. No line-item CIP found on njtransit.com (capital-program page is JS-rendered).
- King County Metro: biennial budget shows ITS mostly as operating decision packages (headway-based Advanced Service Management, HASTUS/asset-management systems) and small capital planning lines; NTD CI $16-24M/yr (~$19k per bus).
- SEPTA: site is JS-rendered; NTD CI $39-118M/yr (13.8% of capex; Key fare and rail signal/communications programmes).
- SFMTA: TCUP page confirms a 7-phase CBTC programme (subway + on-street), cost not published on page; NTD CI $3-4M/yr through 2025 (spend ramp still ahead).

### 3.9 Network Rail / ORR (Great Britain)
- CP7 signalling renewals £2.8bn (Mar 2026 plan) vs £3.1bn (Mar 2024), telecoms £0.7bn (£0.8bn). ORR's determination priced signalling renewals at £3.55bn GB (2023-24 prices) against 4,801 SEUs in England & Wales + 631 in Scotland. Digital signalling (ETCS) spend in CP7 has been cut from £1,544m to £994m with the West Coast North Modernisation reprofiled, fleet fitment reduced to £535m and back-loaded (£266m in 2028/29). This matters for any 2026-2031 forecast: UK ETCS trackside volumes are deferred into CP8 (2029-34).

### 3.10 Deutsche Bahn / DSD (Germany)
- Primary evidence of both the demand (€2.0bn command & control capex in 2025, 64 interlockings, €6.3bn DSD volume contract, €2.5bn digitalisation in the 2026 federal budget, ~€107bn 2025-29 federal track investment) and the 2025 setback (ETCS migration strategy withdrawn over vehicle-fitment feasibility and infrastructure-funding uncertainty; federal equity/loans dropped from the 2026 budget in favour of the SVIK special fund).

### 3.11 TfL, LTA, RATP, NSW
- TfL's visible "Technology" capex is small (£62-68m/yr) but signalling demand is embedded in the Piccadilly line upgrade (£347-451m/yr), Four Lines Modernisation (£72m in 2025/26), DLR and District line signalling; renewals are protected at £800m rising to ~£850m/yr. Operating costs £8.8bn.
- LTA: S$5.7bn FY2025 expenditure; S$4.1bn rail contracts awarded including TEL-extension signalling/PSD (Alstom + ST Engineering) and communications (ST Engineering); reliability taskforce explicitly prioritises signalling renewal.
- RATP: €2.0bn 2025 investment; NSW: A$7.5bn TfNSW + A$5.0bn Sydney Metro capex in 2025-26.

## 5. Gaps and caveats
1. APTA Public Transportation Fact Book (apta.com) and FTA NTST summary (transit.dot.gov) were unreachable (HTTP 403); NTD Time Series 3.1 was used instead and reconciles to the same source data.
2. MTA operating IT share: no IT line in the MTA Statement of Operations GL or in the headcount "function" field; would need the MTA Adopted Budget/February Plan department tables (mta.info document pages returned 404/403).
3. TfL operating technology spend and the full 2026 Business Plan tables (tfl.gov.uk blocked); only the Board budget presentations were available.
4. Transport for NSW Digital Systems (ETCS/ATO) program cost: not itemised in Budget Paper 3; transport.nsw.gov.au blocked.
5. SNCF Reseau (sncf-reseau.com, sncf.com blocked), Delhi Metro (JS-rendered annual-report page, no PDF links in HTML), SEPTA and SFMTA capital budget PDFs (JS-rendered sites) could not be retrieved; NTD values are used for SEPTA/SFMTA.
6. Digitale Schiene Deutschland federal budget line history (2024 vs 2025 cuts) is evidenced only indirectly (DB Integrated Report: withdrawal of the ETCS migration strategy; 2026 budget digitalisation line €2.5bn).
7. NTD CI is capital-only, includes enterprise IT, and agency-level values are lumpy by contract timing; per-vehicle and per-track-mile intensities use 2025 denominators against 4-year average numerators.
8. Correction to research file 07: Network Rail's CP7 digital-signalling portfolio moved from £1,544m (March 2024 plan) to £994m (March 2026 plan), not the reverse.
