## 1. Key takeaways

1. **The "SaaS 7–8x revenue vs integrators ~1.5x sales / 14–16x EBIT" thesis survives in direction but not in magnitude, and it needs restating on observed 6 Nov 2026 data.** Via trades at **5.6x LTM / 5.0x NTM EV/revenue** (EV $2.76bn after $336m net cash; the stock is 28% below its 52-week level and 18% below its $46 IPO price), not 7–8x; INIT trades at **1.0x revenue / 7.0x EBITDA / 9.9x EBIT** after its 2 November EBIT guidance cut (not 1.4–1.6x / 14–16x); IVU — the software-led, 52%-recurring pure play — trades at **2.6x revenue / 15.2x EBITDA / 18.5x EBIT / 28.7x P/E**, i.e. the market already pays a software premium inside the "integrator" bucket when margins (14% EBIT) and recurring mix justify it. The cleanest statement for the deck is: *transit-ITS integrators 1.0–2.6x revenue and 7–15x EBITDA; transit SaaS 5–6x revenue (loss-making); horizontal fleet SaaS 12x revenue (Samsara)*.
2. **Large-cap rail is valued on margin quality and backlog, not on "ITS".** Siemens AG trades at 17.9x LTM EBITDA / 3.1x sales with Mobility earning an 8.3% LTM profit margin (€12.7bn LTM revenue, €1.22bn LTM EBITDA); Wabtec at 19.0x LTM adjusted EBITDA / 4.4x sales on a 23.2% margin (freight-weighted; Transit segment is 28% of sales at a 13.5% operating margin); Hitachi at 10.6x EBITDA / 1.7x sales (group; Hitachi Rail turnover ">€7bn" but segment EBITA not reachable); Alstom at **4.2x stale EBITDA / 0.33x sales** after the 2 November profit warning (H1 aEBIT ~(4)%, FY margin guidance cut from 6.5% to ~1%, FCF guidance to €(1.5)bn, net debt €1.1bn, a ~€2bn deleveraging review "including inorganic options", shares −50% over 52 weeks).
3. **Three of the twelve listed names issued profit warnings or entered restructuring in 2026 — all on the project/hardware-heavy side:** Alstom (2 Nov 2026), INIT (2 Nov 2026: Q3 EBIT €1.6m vs €8.7m; FY EBIT cut to €33–37m on Middle-East demand, materials availability and mix) and Kapsch TrafficCom (Feb 2026 guidance cut; 26 Jul 2026 standstill and binding term sheet with lenders to March 2028; all €110m of financial liabilities classified current after a covenant breach; net debt €119m vs €54m market cap). Execution and project risk, not demand, is the common thread — a direct input to the "avoid leveraged hardware integrators" recommendation.
4. **Adjacent enforcement/tolling platforms show customer-concentration risk rather than leverage risk.** Verra Mobility's equity fell 90% in 52 weeks to a $333m market cap (EV $1.24bn, 3.1x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $402m, net leverage 2.2x) after Avis Budget "significantly and abruptly reduced its fleet volumes" and Verra terminated the contract on 2 Oct 2026, Hertz renewed on "materially less favorable terms" and Enterprise (expiring Sep 2027) went to procurement; Government Solutions (incl. bus-lane cameras) still grew 13%. Trimble (T&L segment 14% of revenue) trades at 12.0x LTM adjusted EBITDA / 3.6x revenue on a 29.6% margin; Constellation (Modaxo's parent) at 4.4x revenue / 22.7x EBITDA with 19% acquisition-led growth.
5. **Via's net-loss conflict is resolved from the primary 8-K: GovTech's $31.9m is Via's non-GAAP "Adjusted Net Loss" (FY2025 $(31,865)k); GAAP net loss was $(96,361)k; Adjusted EBITDA was $(33,394)k (−8% margin).** Adjusted Net Loss excludes stock-based compensation and related payroll taxes, amortisation of the convertible-note discount, the $10.9m loss on extinguishment of the convertible notes at IPO, derivative/warrant revaluations, patent-litigation and transaction costs. EV conversion: 81.5m shares (77.66m Class A + 3.85m Class B per the Q2-2026 10-Q cover) × $37.72 = $3.07bn market cap; cash $335.9m at 30 Jun 2026, no bank or convertible debt (revolver undrawn; notes converted at IPO; only $18.0m of operating-lease liabilities) → **EV ≈ $2.74–2.76bn**. Guidance: FY2026 revenue $550–553m (+27%), adjusted EBITDA $(12.5)–(7.5)m, first positive adjusted-EBITDA quarter in Q4 2026.
6. **Cubic's cautionary tale is now anchored on primary 2021 filings plus one directly-read secondary digest; the rating-agency texts remain out of reach.** The merger proxy (DEFM14A, 26 Mar 2021) shows the LBO was financed with a $2,175m first-lien facility ($1,475m term loan B, $300m term loan C, $175m LC facility, $225m revolver) plus a $325m second-lien term loan — about **$1.8bn of funded term debt on FY2020 adjusted EBITDA of $158.3m (≈11.4x) and on management's FY2021E EBITDA of $180m (10.0x)**, with a plan that required EBITDA to double to $326m by FY2025E. The Briefs.co digest (14 Jul 2026, citing Bloomberg data) puts total debt at ~$2.1bn with some loans at ~10 cents, after a July 2025 recapitalisation with $170m of new sponsor equity; it quotes S&P (Feb 2026) as no longer expecting default within six months "but still sees one as possible within a year" and calling the debt load "onerous", and Fitch as saying the deferred interest payment "underscores Cubic's liquidity problems". Fitch/S&P/Moody's rating levels, recovery ratings and post-2021 revenue/EBITDA could not be read (sites blocked) and remain a gap.
7. **Transit pure plays are small and illiquid relative to the universe:** INIT (€362m market cap, 4 analysts), IVU (€457m, 2 analysts), Kapsch (€54m, 3 analysts) and Via ($3.07bn, 10 analysts) versus Siemens (€202bn), Hitachi (¥20.4tn ≈ $129bn), Wabtec ($47.6bn), Constellation (C$74.7bn ≈ $53bn), Samsara ($24.0bn) and Trimble ($12.4bn). A new entrant's exit multiples should be benchmarked on the pure plays and on Via, not on the large-cap rail or software conglomerates.
8. **Recommended reference bands for the model's valuation tab (observed, 6 Nov 2026):** EV/LTM revenue — transit ITS integrators 1.0x (INIT) to 2.6x (IVU); transit SaaS 5.6x (Via); horizontal fleet SaaS 12.3x (Samsara); rail OEM/signalling 0.3x (Alstom) to 4.4x (Wabtec); adjacent enforcement/tolling 0.4x (Kapsch, distressed) to 1.2x (Verra). EV/LTM EBITDA — INIT 7.0x, IVU 15.2x, Trimble 12.0x (adjusted), CSU 22.7x, Siemens 17.9x, Wabtec 19.0x (adjusted), Hitachi 10.6x, Alstom 4.2x (stale), Verra 3.1x (TTM adjusted). Via and Samsara are n.m. on EBITDA.
